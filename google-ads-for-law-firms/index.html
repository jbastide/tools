Google Ads for law firms and lawyers: does it work, what it costs, and who should run it

Google Ads works for a law firm when the budget buys enough clicks to judge it, the firm counts signed cases instead of leads, and someone answers the phone. It is expensive: WordStream’s 2026 benchmarks (vendor data, medians from 13,474 US search campaigns, April 2025 to March 2026) put Attorneys and Legal Services at $9.87 a click and $131.63 a lead, the highest cost per lead of any industry in the report. Law firm owners on r/LawFirm reported a wider range (owner-reported, paid): $80 to $1,000 per Google Search ads lead, and a median of $5,000 a month in ad spend (24 owners).

By Copy for Originals · Published October 13, 2026 · Last updated October 31, 2026

Educational only, not legal advice. This page describes Google Ads practice and points to public rules, opinions and cases. Your state bar’s rules and your own counsel decide what your firm may do.

Does Google Ads work for law firms?

Google Ads works for a law firm when three things are true: the budget buys enough clicks to judge the campaign, the firm counts signed cases instead of leads, and someone answers the phone. When one of those is missing, law firm owners report paying for clicks and getting no cases.

In 312 r/LawFirm threads from October 2023 to October 2026, 188 had law firm owners describing their own paid marketing. In 63 of those 188 (34%) the owner paid and got no cases or no return, in 57 (30%) the owner described junk leads, and in 37 (20%) the market was too expensive for the budget. The counts and the coded threads are in the law firm Google Ads agency scorecard.

Whatever the firm calls it (Google Ads, AdWords, Google PPC or pay-per-click advertising for lawyers), this guide covers Search ads bought by the click and Local Services Ads bought by the lead.

How much does Google Ads cost a law firm?

Benchmarks put a legal click at $9.87 and a legal lead at $131.63 (WordStream 2026, medians), but law firm owners reported paying $80 to $1,000 per Google Search ads lead, so the figure that matters is the firm’s own cost per click divided by its own conversion rate. At $100 a click and a 5% conversion rate, a lead costs $2,000.

WordStream’s 2026 Google Ads benchmarks are vendor data from 13,474 US search advertising campaigns that ran from April 1, 2025 to March 31, 2026, and WordStream says its “averages” are medians. Attorneys and Legal Services was among the industries with the highest cost per click, at $9.87, and had the highest cost per lead in Google and Microsoft Ads, at $131.63. LocaliQ’s benchmarks page, built with WordStream from Google Ads and Microsoft Ads campaigns and stating no period or campaign count, lists a 5.55% conversion rate for Attorneys & Legal Services and $5.42 as the cost per click across all industries.

The three legal figures are separate statistics and don’t combine: $9.87 divided by 5.55% is about $178 (our arithmetic), not $131.63. For your own plan, use the cost per click in your account, or the top of page bid (high range) in Google’s Keyword Planner, which Google describes as an approximation of the 80th percentile of what advertisers have historically paid for a keyword’s top of page bid. For injury keywords, the personal injury cost per click section sets the benchmark median, Keyword Planner’s bid range and forum reports side by side.

Owner-reported figures (paid, r/LawFirm 2023–2026): Google Search ads leads cost $80 to $1,000, Local Services Ads leads about $30 (family law) to $300 (personal injury and criminal defense), and bought leads $40 to $150. Each row is in How much do legal marketing agencies charge? Injury firms have their own guide: Personal injury Google Ads. What an agency charges on top of the ad spend, from agencies’ own published prices, is in our Google Ads management cost study.

How much should a law firm spend on Google Ads?

A law firm should spend at least three times its expected cost per lead a month on each Google Ads campaign, where expected cost per lead is cost per click divided by conversion rate. At $100 a click and a 5% conversion rate, that is $6,000 a month.

That minimum is this site’s rule, not Google’s. At three times the cost per lead, a campaign converting as expected would go a whole month without a lead only about 5% of the time, by the site’s own calculation in the PPC Gumball Rule, so a month with zero leads is a sign something is broken rather than bad luck. Law firm owners on r/LawFirm reported spending $433 to $30,000 a month on Google Ads, a median of $5,000 (24 owners, highest monthly figure each owner gave).

The law firm Google Ads budget guidelines give ten rules and a calculator. By practice area: the personal injury budget guide, and the guidelines’ sections on criminal defense, family law and estate planning.

Should a law firm use Local Services Ads or Google Search ads?

Local Services Ads charge a law firm for each lead and Google Search ads charge for each click, so compare the two by cost per signed case, not cost per lead. Cost per signed case = cost per lead ÷ share of leads that are real potential clients ÷ sign rate.

Google charges for each valid lead, and that includes a call the firm missed during business hours when the caller stayed on the line more than 20 seconds. In 37 r/LawFirm threads where lawyers described their own Local Services Ads, 10 reported paying for junk leads, and in 30 of the 37 at least one lawyer said the ads worked for them. Firms that pass Google’s screening carry the Google Verified badge.

Change under way, checked October 30, 2026. Google is moving existing Local Services Ads campaigns automatically into Google Ads as Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals. Firms are still charged only when someone contacts them through the ad, the ads still show only on Google Search and Google Maps, and the new campaigns offer no keyword targeting. Professional services are listed for late 2026 on one Google page and 2027 on another, and neither page names law firms.

The Local Services Ads vs Google Ads guide for lawyers covers what the move changes, which leads Google credits, and a calculator for cost per signed case on your own numbers.

Should a law firm run its own Google Ads or hire an agency?

A law firm can run Google Ads itself or pay an agency, and either way the firm should own the account and see what Google charged and how many cases signed. An agency’s fee comes on top of the ad spend. For Local Services Ads, see Do you need an agency for Local Services Ads, and what does LSA management cost?

Four law firm owners on r/LawFirm reported paying $500 to $2,500 a month for Google Ads management, a median of $1,175, with the ad spend paid to Google separately; the figures are in How much do legal marketing agencies charge? In 45 of the 188 owner threads, owners said doing it themselves or in-house worked better, mostly for content and SEO. The scorecard’s own view: a firm with one practice area, a modest budget and someone who can spend a couple of hours a week can run a single Google Ads campaign or Local Services Ads itself, and paying for management makes more sense when the fee is a small share of spend and the agency reports cost per signed case.

The law firm Google Ads agency scorecard has 12 checks a firm can run inside its own account. Three are must-haves: Admin access to the account, the Google Ads customer ID, and the exact amount Google charged, reported separately from the agency’s fee.

Is Google Ads worth it for a law firm?

Google Ads is worth it for a law firm when the cost per signed case is lower than what the firm can afford to pay for a case, and a firm can only know that by counting signed cases. The widely repeated figure that 82% of law firms say Google Ads isn’t worth it comes from a December 2021 survey and is narrower than it sounds.

The 82% comes from CallRail’s “2022 Marketing Outlook for Law Firms,” a vendor survey of 508 US law firm employees in December 2021. Among the firms that ran paid search, 82% agreed that “Paid search marketing is too expensive to get good ROI”: a question about expense, not about tracking, lead quality or signed cases. Almost 65% of respondents worked at firms with 250 to 999 employees, and the most common job title was IT manager (26%). Do 82% of law firms say Google Ads isn’t worth it? sets the figure beside CallRail’s later surveys and the versions that drop its conditions.

Can a law firm bid on a competitor’s name in Google Ads?

Whether a law firm can bid on a competitor’s name in Google Ads depends on the state. Ethics opinions in North Carolina, Ohio and Michigan say no, those in Texas and South Carolina say yes with conditions, and New Jersey’s ethics committee reached the same conclusion as Texas in 2019.

Google’s own trademark policy is a separate matter: when a trademark owner complains, Google won’t restrict a trademark used as a keyword, but will restrict one used in an ad from a direct competitor or in a confusing, deceptive or misleading way. In court, the Ninth Circuit held that a competitor’s use of the “Lerner & Rowe” mark was not likely to confuse consumers (October 22, 2024); the Fifth Circuit let Adler’s claim go forward over keyword purchases of the Adler marks followed by generic ads (August 10, 2021); and a Colorado suit by Franklin D. Azar & Associates over Google ads ended with the Alabama firm agreeing to pay $1.55 million (BusinessDen, December 12, 2024).

Other states may have their own opinions, or none; check your own state bar before bidding on another firm’s name. Can a law firm bid on a competitor’s name in Google Ads? quotes each opinion and the court cases, and covers what to do when the name being bid on is yours.

Does Google record calls to a law firm from its ads?

Yes, in the United States and Canada. Google’s Local Services Ads help says all inbound calls through the Google forwarding number are recorded by Google, and Google Ads Help says the call recording setting is now enabled by default. Callers hear an automated notice first.

In Google Ads, recording covers calls through a Google forwarding number from call ads, call assets or website visits, not calls from location assets. It stays off for advertisers who turned it off earlier and for businesses Google identified as healthcare or financial services; legal services are not named. The setting is in Google Ads under Admin, Account settings, Call ads, Call recording.

North Carolina’s ethics committee proposed an ethics opinion on Local Services Ads in 2021 (Proposed 2021 Formal Ethics Opinion 5) and withdrew it in April 2022 after concluding the recordings “are not uniquely vulnerable to harmful third-party disclosures.” The State Bar of Wisconsin’s ethics counsel wrote in January 2021 that without the caller’s informed consent, a lawyer who authorizes Google to record the calls “violates the duty of confidentiality”; that article is commentary, not a formal opinion. The North Carolina State Bar Journal also quotes the comment to the state’s prospective-client rule: where a lawyer’s advertising invites information about a possible case without clear warnings, the lawyer, to avoid creating a duty to the person under that rule, has an affirmative obligation to warn that a communication with the lawyer will not create a client-lawyer relationship and that what is said will not be confidential or privileged.

Google’s “About Local Services data” page says that in North America, Local Services call recordings and messages in legal verticals are kept for 60 days, against up to 180 days for other categories. Which searches waste a law firm’s Google Ads budget?

Searches from people who are not looking to hire a lawyer waste a law firm’s Google Ads budget: job seekers, students, people who want free forms, and people with a legal problem the firm doesn’t handle. Negative keywords block those searches, and phrase and exact match keywords keep Google from matching the ad to loose ones.

The five short answers for law firm Search campaigns list starter negative keywords by type of searcher and explain when broad match is safe to use. The Negative Keyword Finder reads a search terms report in the browser and lists the terms that spent money without converting.

Why don’t Google Ads leads turn into signed cases?

Google Ads leads fail to become signed cases mostly after the click: the call isn’t answered, nobody calls back, or the caller was never a potential client. A firm that only counts leads can’t see which of the three it has.

In the secret-shopper test in Clio’s 2024 Legal Trends Report, as summarized by the Illinois Supreme Court Commission on Professionalism, 33% of law firms responded to an email inquiry and 48% neither answered the phone nor called back. In the Chicago personal injury lead generation study, 38% of 207 lowest-rated Google reviews at 11 firms describe no callback or an unreachable lawyer, and 20 of 42 advertisers show no call tracking script on their homepage (scripts loaded through Google Tag Manager aren’t detected). What call tracking costs, vendor by vendor, is in our call tracking comparison for law firms.

Google Ads can only bid toward signed cases if the firm sends them back as offline conversions. Google won’t import an offline conversion uploaded more than 90 days after the last click, or more than 63 days for enhanced conversions for leads, and its help page says that starting June 15, 2026, API uploads of both move to the Data Manager API and are blocked in the Google Ads API. How does a law firm track signed cases back to Google Ads clicks? gives the setup steps and what each method sends to Google.

How can a law firm tell if its Google Ads is working?

A law firm’s Google Ads is working when its cost per signed case is below what the firm can afford to pay for a case. Cost per signed case = (ad spend + agency fee) ÷ signed cases.

Zero leads is a verdict only after enough clicks. After 3 ÷ conversion rate clicks with no conversion (120 clicks at a 2.5% conversion rate), a campaign that really converts at that rate would get zero conversions less than 5% of the time, by the site’s own calculation in the PPC Gumball Rule; the PPC Troubleshooter runs that math and lists likely causes. Few advertisers do this: in our study of 62 forum threads asking why Google Ads got clicks but no conversions, only 3 posters stated a target CPA or expected conversion rate, and those who gave a click count had a median of 190 clicks before asking.

Limitations

  • This page covers Google Ads Search campaigns and Local Services Ads (now moving into Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals) for lead generation, not standard Performance Max, display or social campaigns.
  • The benchmarks are vendor data for all legal services together, not for one practice area or city, and they come from Google Ads and Microsoft Ads campaigns together, not Google Ads alone.
  • The Azar settlement, the Clio figures and New Jersey’s 2019 committee view were read through the publications named in Sources, not the court filing, the Clio report or the New Jersey opinion itself.
  • Seven states are listed for competitor-name bidding. Other states are not covered here.
  • Forum counts come from r/LawFirm threads and are what owners reported, not a sample of all law firms.
  • Not legal advice. Rules and Google’s help pages change; each external source below was read on October 30, 2026.
