How much should a criminal defense law firm spend on Google Ads?

A criminal defense law firm should budget at least 3 times its expected cost per lead each month for Google Ads. Below that, a month with no leads can’t be told apart from bad luck. This guide gives the minimum by cost per click and conversion rate, the published benchmark for criminal defense search ads, and what criminal defense firm owners reported spending.

By Copy for Originals · Published October 13, 2026 · Last updated October 13, 2026

Minimum monthly budget = 3 × cost per click ÷ conversion rate

How much should a criminal defense law firm spend on Google Ads at minimum?

A criminal defense law firm should budget at least 3 times its expected cost per lead each month, where cost per lead is cost per click divided by conversion rate. At $40 a click and a 10% conversion rate, a lead costs $400 and the minimum budget is $1,200 a month.

That is the PPC Gumball Rule checkpoint. A campaign that spends three times its expected cost per lead with no lead at all is only about 5% likely to be unlucky. With a smaller budget the firm can still get leads, but a month without any tells it nothing.

The conversion rate is the share of ad clicks that call or submit a form. Find your cost per click in Google Ads under Campaigns, in the Avg. CPC column. For a new account, use the “Top of page bid (high range)” in Keyword Planner for the keywords you will bid on.

The minimum gets you a verdict, not a caseload. To plan for a number of leads, budget leads wanted × cost per lead. The law firm Google Ads budget guidelines cover daily budgets, bid strategy and when to raise or cut spend.

What does a criminal defense lead cost on Google Ads?

LocaliQ’s legal benchmarks put the median criminal defense search ad at $12.30 a click, a 9.90% conversion rate and $101.49 a lead, from campaigns that ran between April 2022 and March 2023. Three times that cost per lead is about $304 a month, which is a floor and not a plan, because the keywords a firm most wants cost more than the median.

Read the benchmark with three cautions. LocaliQ says its averages “are technically median figures,” so half of campaigns paid more. The whole legal sample was 256 campaigns, with at least 10 in each practice area. And the data is from 2022 and 2023.

The figures also do not multiply out: $12.30 ÷ 9.90% is $124, not $101.49, because each is a separate median. Use your own cost per click and conversion rate in the table above.

Is $600 a month enough for criminal defense Google Ads?

It can be, if clicks are cheap enough. At $25 a click, $600 buys 24 clicks, and at a 10% conversion rate the chance of no leads at all from 24 clicks is about 8%, with 2.4 leads expected. At $50 a click the same budget buys 12 clicks and the chance of a month with no leads rises to about 28%.

The three-times minimum at $25 a click and 10% is $750 a month, so $600 is just under it. One owner in our thread data ran Google Ads at $20 a day, about $600 a month, next to a Google Business Profile he looked after himself, and reported five to ten leads a week, most of them through the profile.

The odds come from one line of arithmetic: the chance of zero leads is (1 − conversion rate) raised to the number of clicks. The PPC Troubleshooter runs it for any budget and cost per click.

What can a criminal defense firm afford to pay for a lead?

A criminal defense firm can afford to pay for a lead the fee from an average case, times its margin, times the share of leads that sign, times the share of that profit it is willing to spend on ads. The answer comes from the firm’s own fees and sign rate, not from a benchmark.

One solo criminal defense owner reported fees of $5,000 to $50,000 a case and about $1,000 of Local Services Ads spend per signed case. At those figures a lead costing a few hundred dollars pays for itself if even one lead in ten signs.

Should a criminal defense firm use Local Services Ads or Search ads?

Compare them by cost per signed case, because Local Services Ads charge per lead and Search ads charge per click. Criminal defense owners reported Local Services Ads leads at $100 to $300 each.

One was quoted $100 to $150 a lead by Google and paid $250 to $300 in practice. The same owner spent about $3,000 on the DUI category, signed one client from six hours of consultations, and turned that category off. Another said a $10,000 monthly spend had produced about $50,000 a month until call quality fell.

The Local Services Ads vs Google Ads guide for lawyers has a calculator that compares the two on your own numbers and explains which leads Google credits.

Limitations

  • The budget is only as good as the cost per click and conversion rate you enter. Check both against your own account after the first month.
  • The benchmark is a median from a small sample of campaigns in 2022 and 2023, and its definition of a lead is whatever each advertiser tracked.
  • The owner reports are what individual owners wrote on r/LawFirm, one firm each, with vendor names withheld.
  • Criminal defense covers very different matters, from traffic tickets to felonies. Cost per click and the fee per case differ for each, so run the numbers per case type.
  • The three-times rule treats clicks as independent. Repeat clicks, bots or one bad keyword make a month with no leads more likely than the math says.
  • This guide is about Google Ads budgets, not legal advertising rules. Check your state bar’s advertising rules separately.
