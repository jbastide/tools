# Behavioral-economics biases in LLMs: do human nudges work on AI shopping agents? (evidence as of Oct 2026)

Scope note for the writer: Two distinct literatures are mixed together in this area and must be kept apart.
(A) **LLMs as synthetic humans** (survey respondents, "homo silicus", simulated consumers) — the question there is whether the LLM *reproduces* human bias magnitudes. (B) **LLMs as decision-making agents** (choosing a product, ranking a list, negotiating a price on someone's behalf) — the question there is whether the LLM's *own* choices are moved by the cue, regardless of whether it matches humans. The user's question is about (B). Each finding below is tagged [SIM] for (A) or [AGENT] for (B) where the design makes that clear. Era flags: [GPT-3.5-era] = result from 2022–early-2024 models (GPT-3/3.5/early GPT-4, Llama-2) that may not hold for current models.

Model-name caveat: several 2026 preprints name models I am reporting exactly as the source names them (e.g., "GPT 6 Luna", "Claude Sonnet 5", "Gemini 3.1 Pro", "GPT 5.6 Terra"). I could not independently verify these designations.

## KQ1. Inventory: which human biases have been tested in LLMs, by whom, in which models, with what result?

### Takeaway
Essentially every bias on the user's list has been tested in at least one LLM, and almost all are reported as *present in some models* — but the pattern across models is heterogeneous, the direction sometimes reverses (framing, status quo, scarcity), and the largest, most consistent agent-side effects are not the classic cognitive biases at all but rather hypersensitivity to ratings, price, social-proof/authority text, platform endorsements and list position. Below is the bias-by-bias inventory; detailed numbers for the shopping-relevant biases are in KQ2–KQ6.

### Cited Findings

**Broad surveys / benchmarks**
- Malberg et al. (2024/2025, NLP4DH@ACL), 30 biases × 20 LLMs × 200 decision scenarios (30,000 tests), bias score on [-1, 1] where 0 = no bias: evidence of all 30 biases in at least some of the 20 models; "nearly all 30 biases have positive average scores"; **Status-Quo Bias and Disposition Effect are the only two with negative averages (models on average prefer change)**; no clear correlation between model size/Chatbot Arena capability and biasedness; Gemini 1.5 Pro least biased, followed by Qwen2.5 72B, GPT-4o mini, Mistral Large, Gemini 1.5 Flash; "the larger OpenAI models are somewhat more biased"; Llama 3.2 1B looked unbiased only because 33% of its decisions failed to parse. — [Malberg et al., arXiv 2410.15413](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.15413); [ACL Anthology version](https://aclanthology.org/2025.nlp4dh-1.50/); [code/data](https://github.com/simonmalberg/cognitive-biases-in-llms). (Per-bias numeric averages for anchoring, framing, loss aversion, endowment, availability are only in a heatmap figure; not extractable from the text.)
- Echterhoff et al. 2024 (Findings of EMNLP 2024, "BiasBuster"), 13,465 prompts, college-admissions decision task, models GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4, Llama-2-13b, Llama-2-7b [GPT-3.5-era] [AGENT-like decision task]: framing Δ(admit−reject rate) = 0.165 (GPT-3.5), −0.405 (GPT-4), −0.060 (Llama-2-13b), 0.002 (Llama-2-7b); self-help debiasing (model rewrites its own prompt) cut GPT-4 framing residual to 2.7% of prompts but GPT-3.5 to 17.2%; anchoring (to the model's own prior decisions) was not reliably debiased; authors conclude higher-capacity models debias more successfully. — [Echterhoff et al., arXiv 2403.00811](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.00811v3)
- Itzhak et al. "Instructed to Bias" (2023/TACL 2024): tests decoy effect, certainty effect and belief bias in Flan-T5, Mistral-Instruct, GPT-3.5 and GPT-4; biases appear across GPT-3, Mistral and T5 families and **are more prevalent in instruction-tuned (RLHF/IT) models than in their base counterparts** [GPT-3.5-era]. — [arXiv 2308.00225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00225) (numeric effect sizes are in the PDF which I could not parse; see Gaps)
- Suri et al. 2023 ("Do LLMs show decision heuristics similar to humans?"), GPT-3.5 [GPT-3.5-era] [SIM]: anchoring (random anchors shifted numeric estimates), representativeness/availability (conjunction-style error; swayed by vivid anecdote), framing (same product rated more effective when features described positively), **endowment effect (valued an owned item more than an identical newly found one)**; humans run on the same prompts showed similar effects. — [arXiv 2305.04400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04400)
- Binz & Schulz 2023 (PNAS), GPT-3 [GPT-3.5-era] [SIM]: GPT-3 "solves vignette-based tasks similarly or better than human subjects" but "small perturbations to vignette-based tasks can lead GPT-3 vastly astray"; makes "decent decisions from descriptions". — [arXiv 2206.14576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14576) (the abstract page does not list which specific heuristics-and-biases vignettes it passed/failed; search snippets elsewhere attribute to this line of work findings of certainty effect, overweighting and framing in GPT-3 — [arXiv 2410.02820 related-work](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02820v1))
- Hagendorff, Fabi & Kosinski (2022/Nature Computational Science 2023), GPT-3 vs ChatGPT vs GPT-4, CRT and semantic illusions [GPT-3.5-era]: GPT-3 showed "human-like intuition" with the associated errors; ChatGPT and GPT-4 "learned to avoid these errors and perform in a hyperrational manner". — [arXiv 2212.05206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.05206)
- Jones & Steinhardt 2022 (NeurIPS), OpenAI Codex code generation [GPT-3.5-era]: errors depend predictably on prompt framing; outputs adjust toward anchors; bias toward outputs resembling frequent training examples. — [arXiv 2202.12299](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12299)
- Horton 2023/2025 ("Homo silicus"), now covering GPT-4, GPT-4o, Claude Sonnet 3.5/3.7, Claude Haiku 3, Llama-3-70B, DeepSeek, GPT-3.5-Turbo [SIM]: **status quo bias (Samuelson–Zeckhauser budget allocation)** — in conditional logits every model shows a large significant positive coefficient on the status-quo indicator (≈0.32 for GPT-4o to 0.93 for GPT-4); price-gouging fairness (Kahneman et al. 1986): a $1 price increase lowers fairness rating by 0.004–0.009 points (GPT-4, temp 1, 2,400 obs), with left-leaning personas rating hikes as more unfair. — [arXiv 2301.07543v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.07543v2)
- Chen, Liu, Shan & Zhong 2023 (PNAS), GPT-3.5 [GPT-3.5-era] [SIM/AGENT]: GARP budget-allocation tasks across risk, time, social, food domains; GPT's rationality (CCEI) exceeds human subjects; preference parameters differ slightly from humans and show less heterogeneity; rationality robust to demographics but "sensitive to contextual framing". — [arXiv 2305.12763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12763v3); [VoxEU summary](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/emergence-economic-rationality-gpt)
- Goli & Singh (Marketing Science 2024, "Can LLMs capture human preferences?"), GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 [GPT-3.5-era partly] [SIM]: both GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 chose smaller-sooner rewards more often than humans (less patient); GPT-3.5 showed lexicographic preference for earlier rewards; GPT-4 not lexicographic but discount rates still "considerably larger" than humans; more patient when prompted in weak-future-tense languages (German, Mandarin); chain-of-thought conjoint mitigates but does not eliminate discrepancies. — [arXiv 2305.02531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02531v6); [INFORMS release](https://informs.org/News-Room/INFORMS-Releases/News-Releases/New-Research-Provides-Insights-on-How-Generative-AI-Can-Mimic-Human-Responses-to-Preference)
- Brand, Israeli & Ngwe (HBS WP 2023; ACM EC 2024; MSI 2025 update), GPT-3 then GPT-3.5-Turbo [SIM]: GPT as survey respondent reproduces downward-sloping demand, declining marginal utility, state dependence; higher simulated income → higher price tolerance; conjoint WTP for existing features replicated and new-feature WTP predicted after demographic fine-tuning. — [HBS working paper](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/23-062_47458a4a-8be2-4b53-a3a3-9b5af20578b1.pdf); [MSI 2025 version](https://www.msi.org/working-paper/using-llms-for-market-research-2/)
- "How price sensitive is AI?" (Journal of Marketing Analytics 2026), ChatGPT-4 (Studies 1–3) and GPT-4o (Study 4) [SIM]: synthetic WTP came out ~27% lower than real BDM-elicited human WTP in one product study. — [search summary; full text paywalled](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/414905330_How_price_sensitive_is_AI_Benchmarking_LLM-generated_willingness_to_pay_in_pricing_analytics)
- "GPT's Judgements Under Uncertainty" (2024), GPT-4o, 1,350 runs of Kahneman–Tversky vignettes [SIM]: conjunction fallacy absent; representativeness present (~99% intuitive); loss aversion present (ignored expected value in >95%); prospect-theory certainty effect present (86% chose risk-free option); sunk cost largely absent (82% correct); framing "inconsistent/contradictory"; overall 658 statistical vs 692 intuitive responses. — [arXiv 2410.02820](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02820v1)
- "Planted in Pretraining, Swayed by Finetuning" (2025), 30+ biases: pretraining backbone is the main determinant of a model's bias profile; models sharing a backbone resemble each other more than models sharing finetuning data. — [arXiv 2507.07186](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07186)

### Inferences
- The inventory is complete in breadth but thin in depth for the shopping-specific biases: decoy, endowment and choice overload each rest on one to four small studies, mostly on GPT-3.5/4-era models or gpt-4o-mini.
- The most robust, replicated agent-side regularities (ratings sensitivity, price sensitivity when ratings are matched, social proof/authority text, endorsement badges, position) are better described as *cue hypersensitivity* than as Kahneman–Tversky biases.
- Because pretraining backbone dominates bias profiles (2507.07186) and model updates reverse position preferences (ACES), any bias inventory is model- and version-specific and decays quickly.

### Gaps
- No readable text for the full Itzhak et al. "Instructed to Bias" results (PDF unparseable here), so decoy effect magnitudes for GPT-4 vs base models are not recorded.
- Malberg et al. per-bias numeric averages (anchoring, framing, loss aversion, endowment, availability) are only in a figure; not extracted.
- No LLM-agent study of choice overload / paradox of choice (varying assortment size and measuring deferral or satisfaction) was found.
- No direct LLM test of the price–quality heuristic (inferring quality from a higher price in the absence of other cues) was found.
- Endowment effect: only one GPT-3.5-era test (Suri et al.) found; no 2025–26 replication located.

## KQ2. Decoy effect and anchoring in product/price choice

### Takeaway
No one has published a direct LLM replication of Ariely's Economist web/print/print+web subscription decoy with a named frontier model; the closest tests find a **real but modest decoy effect in GPT-4 (hiring choices) and an ambiguous one in gpt-4o-mini (subscription choices)**, while "decoy" as a text-injection attack on product descriptions produced "mixed or inconsistent" effects. Anchoring is the most consistently present bias in LLMs, including in price negotiation with GPT-4o/GPT-4/Llama-3 and even (partially) o1.

### Cited Findings

**Decoy / asymmetric dominance**
- Itzhak et al. 2023/2024: decoy effect defined as subjects choosing one brand in control but switching once a decoy (worse than the target on every attribute, not worse than the competitor) is added; found in Flan-T5, Mistral-Instruct, GPT-3.5, GPT-4 and "more prevalent in instruction-tuned models" [GPT-3.5-era]. — [arXiv 2308.00225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00225)
- "Irrelevant Alternatives Bias LLM Hiring Decisions" (2024), gpt-3.5-instruct and gpt-4-turbo-1106-preview [AGENT]: recruiter role, target vs competitor with two equally important qualifications, treatment adds an asymmetrically dominated decoy; six occupations × all six order permutations; attraction effect positive and significant for GPT-4 in 5 of 6 occupations; "larger on average for GPT-4 compared to GPT-3.5"; GPT-4 shows much greater variance across decoy positions; no quantitative human comparison. (Effect sizes only in figures.) — [arXiv 2409.15299](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.15299)
- NHSJS 2026 (student journal; single model gpt-4o-mini, temperature 0) [AGENT, consumer choice]: 50 subscription triples; control (target + competitor + none): target 24/50, competitor 26/50; treatment (decoy priced at ~90% of target): target 23, competitor 20, **decoy itself chosen 7 times (14%)**; χ² p = 0.020, ω = 0.28, power 0.706. Note that the *target's* share did not rise — the distribution change is driven by the decoy absorbing choices, which is not the classic attraction effect. — [NHSJS](https://nhsjs.com/2026/a-study-of-cognitive-biases-in-large-language-models-in-decision-making-scenarios/)
- Bias Beware (EMNLP 2025), models LLaMA 8B/70B/405B, Claude 3.5 Sonnet (20241022), Claude 3.7 Sonnet ± thinking, Mistral Large 2 [AGENT, product recommendation]: "decoy" was one of ten biases embedded into product descriptions by rewriting; **decoy, contrast, denominator neglect, storytelling, authority and identity signaling had mixed or inconsistent effects**, whereas social proof and discount framing were consistently positive. — [arXiv 2502.01349](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01349v2)
- Search for an LLM replication of the Economist subscription experiment (16%/0%/84% → 68%/32% in Ariely's human version) returned only human sources; a 2016 Nature HSS paper notes the Economist scenario itself "previously failed to replicate" in humans, with decoy position a likely factor. — [Palgrave Communications 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/palcomms201682)

**Anchoring**
- Suri et al. 2023, GPT-3.5 [SIM]: random anchors shifted numeric estimates; humans on same prompts showed similar effects [GPT-3.5-era]. — [arXiv 2305.04400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04400)
- Price-negotiation anchoring (Findings of EMNLP 2025), buyer/seller agents on 161 CraigslistBargain products, models gpt-4o-2024-08-06, gpt-4-turbo-2024-04-09, Llama-3-70B-Instruct, o1-2024-12-17 [AGENT]: seller instructed to open above target raised seller utility by ≈0.37 (GPT-4o), 0.54 (GPT-4), 0.63 (Llama-3) and lowered buyer utility by the same amounts (GPT-4o buyer from −0.04 to −0.41); **buyer satisfaction rose for GPT-4o and Llama-3 despite the financial loss**; o1 as buyer lost as much as GPT-4o to the anchor alone (n.s. difference), but when warned, o1's loss was significantly smaller (−0.20 vs −0.32); warning GPT-4o reduced but did not remove the effect; Big-Five personality uncorrelated with susceptibility. — [arXiv 2508.21137](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21137v1); [ACL version](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.240.pdf)
- "LLM Rationalis?" (2025) bargaining: human negotiators anchor near the ZOPA midpoint; "all LLM buyers anchor uniformly at the seller's floor". — [arXiv 2512.13063](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.13063)
- Kraishan 2025, 7 models (4 families, reasoning vs non-reasoning siblings), 12,350 calls, reasoning budgets 0–8,192 tokens [AGENT vignettes]: **anchoring was the only one of six biases showing the human-like direction (d = 1.89)** and extra reasoning did not reduce it; a one-line "restate the anchor before answering" instruction lowered anchoring on all five items (p = .057). — [arXiv 2610.10049](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10049)
- "Anchoring Bias in LLMs: An Experimental Study" (2024): LLM responses sensitive to biased hints; Chain-of-Thought, Thoughts-of-Principles, "ignore anchor hints" and Reflection are "not sufficient"; section headings state "Stronger models are consistently biased by anchor hints" and "LLMs are much easier to be biased by expert anchors" (models not named in abstract). — [arXiv 2412.06593](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06593)
- "Anchors in the Machine" (2025), small open models Gemma-2B, Phi-2, Llama-2-7B (robust anchoring) vs GPT-2, Falcon-RW-1B, GPT-Neo-125M (variable): anchors "shift entire output distributions" in log-prob analysis; Shapley attribution over prompt fields. — [arXiv 2511.05766](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05766)
- Anchoring in LLM-as-judge: prior scores shown to a judge compromise independence (title-level evidence only). — [arXiv 2608.25869](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25869)
- Echterhoff 2024: "anchoring" operationalized as sequential self-anchoring to the model's previous admission decisions; post-hoc decision-change rates GPT-3.5 5.2%, GPT-4 17.5%, Llama-2-7b 39.9%, Llama-2-13b 52.1% [GPT-3.5-era]. — [arXiv 2403.00811](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.00811v3)

### Inferences
- Decoy pricing is the *least* well-established nudge for AI agents: the one consumer-choice test (gpt-4o-mini subscriptions) did not actually raise the target's share, and the one strong positive (GPT-4 hiring) is in a different domain. A seller banking on decoy pricing to steer an agent has weaker evidence than one banking on social proof or an endorsement badge.
- Anchoring is the best-supported classic bias in agents, but all agent-side evidence is for *negotiation openings and numeric hints*, not for posted reference prices; see KQ4 gap on strikethrough MSRP.

### Gaps
- No LLM replication of the Economist decoy or of any price-only (two-attribute price/quantity) decoy with GPT-4o/GPT-5/Claude 4/Gemini 2.5-class models was found.
- Effect sizes for the GPT-4 hiring attraction effect are in figures only.
- No study isolates whether a decoy works on an agent when the agent can compute dominance explicitly (e.g., with a calculator tool).

## KQ3. Position bias / primacy in multiple-choice and list ranking; persistence in 2025–26

### Takeaway
Position/order effects are the largest and best-replicated "bias" in LLMs, they persist in 2025–26 frontier models, and their *direction* is model-specific (first vs middle vs last; row vs column) and can flip between versions — which makes position a reliable lever only if the seller knows which model is shopping.

### Cited Findings
- Zheng et al. (ICLR 2024 Spotlight), 20 LLMs (llama 7–65B, llama-2 base/chat 7–70B, vicuna, falcon, gpt-3.5-turbo-0613) [GPT-3.5-era]: selection bias measured as recall SD across option IDs (RStd); 0-shot MMLU RStd ranges from 4.1 (llama-2-70B) to 28.7 (falcon-instruct-7B); gpt-3.5-turbo 5.5; moving the gold answer to its favored slot changes accuracy by up to +17.6 / −13.3 points (falcon-instruct-40B), +6.9/−6.3 for gpt-3.5-turbo; on balanced MMLU llama-30B picks A/B/C/D 34.6/27.3/22.3/15.8% while gpt-3.5-turbo picks 22.5/25.6/32.3/19.6%; bias attributed mainly to **token bias** (prior on option-ID tokens) and partly to position; PriDe (5% estimation) cuts RStd by 7.6 points on average and raises accuracy 1.2 points. — [arXiv 2309.03882v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.03882v4)
- Wang et al. 2023 "LLMs are not fair evaluators", GPT-4 and ChatGPT as judges [GPT-3.5-era]: by swapping response order, Vicuna-13B "could beat ChatGPT on 66 over 80 tested queries" under ChatGPT-as-judge; later work notes GPT-4 favors the first answer and GPT-3.5 the second. — [arXiv 2305.17926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926); [IJCNLP 2025 systematic study](https://aclanthology.org/2025.ijcnlp-long.18.pdf)
- "Lost in the Middle" (Liu et al. 2023): performance highest when relevant information is at the beginning or end of the context and "significantly degrades" in the middle, "even for explicitly long-context models". — [arXiv 2307.03172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172)
- Serial Position Effects of LLMs (Findings of ACL 2025), GPT-3.5-Turbo (0613/1106/0125), GPT-4-0125-preview, Llama-2 7/13/70B-chat, SOLAR, T5/FlanT5: primacy (first third of positions >40% of selections) was the most common effect, in 73 of 104 model-task instances; recency more pronounced in summarization; GPT-4-0125-preview showed no serial-position effect in label shuffling but did in summarization; CoT reduced but did not eliminate effects; "model identity had more influence on label distributions than prompt design". — [arXiv 2406.15981](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.15981)
- "Fragile Preferences" (2025), hiring: GPT-4o-mini, Claude 3 Haiku, Llama 3 8B; color choice: nine models incl. GPT-4.1-nano, Claude Sonnet 4, Llama 4 Scout, Gemini 2.5/3 Flash, Qwen 3 32B [AGENT]: position-dominant resume chosen 92.8% (GPT-4o-mini), 84.3% (Claude 3 Haiku), 72.3% (Llama 3 8B); higher-quality options show primacy, lower-quality show recency; some models show a middle/centrality bias (Claude 3 Haiku chose the middle option 83% for "Harsh" colors); at T=0, 35 of 36 model-tier pairs chose the same position ≥98% of the time; authors call position bias "widespread across models". — [arXiv 2506.14092v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14092v3)
- SCOPE (2025): ChatGPT series, LLaMA series and Gemini 1.5-flash tend to select the first option; Claude 3-haiku and Gemini 1.5-pro prefer the last. — [arXiv 2507.18182](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.18182)
- IJCNLP 2025 systematic LLM-judge study: Claude-3.5-Sonnet, GPT-4, Llama-3.3-70B all reach repetition-stability >0.95, so remaining position bias "is not due to random variations"; direction can be primacy or recency. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.ijcnlp-long.18.pdf)
- Multi-document evaluation (2025): GPT-4o strongly prefers first-position summaries; claude-3.5-sonnet prefers second position. — [arXiv 2502.20592](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.20592)
- **Shopping grid (ACES, Aug & Dec 2025)**, Claude Sonnet 4, GPT-4.1, Gemini 2.5 Flash; later Claude Opus 4.5, GPT-5.1, Gemini 3.0 Pro Preview [AGENT]: all models show a top-row premium; GPT-4.1 favors column 1, Claude Sonnet 4 the middle columns, Gemini 2.5 Flash columns 3–4; for Claude Sonnet 4, moving a product from bottom-right (selected 4.5%) to top row cols 2–3 raises selection ~5×; moving from row 2 to row 1 is worth roughly a *doubling of price* for Claude Sonnet 4 and GPT-4.1 (+17% for Gemini 2.5 Flash); **GPT-4.1 and GPT-5.1 show nearly opposite position preferences**; position bias "persists across prompts, including 'ignore position' instructions, and in headless JSON interfaces". — [arXiv 2508.02630](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02630); [ACM Web Conf 2026 version](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3774904.3792943)
- A secondary citation of ACES reports Claude Sonnet 4 picking from the top half of listings 77% vs 23% bottom half. — [search snippet, not verified in primary](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02630)
- **ABxLab (2025)**, 17 models, two-tab binary choice [AGENT]: "viewed first" effect ranges from −35.4pp (Claude 3.5 Haiku, i.e., recency) to +88.8pp (GPT-4.1 Nano, extreme primacy) in the original condition; under matched ratings and prices most models go *negative* (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Flash −50.5, GPT-4o Mini −50.5, Gemini 2.5 Pro −47.4); human order effect +4.0pp; in three-option trios, first-in-list effects were −16.1 to −37.4pp. — [arXiv 2509.25609v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25609v2)
- **Hotel search agents, 100-listing page ("Does Rank Still Matter?", 2026)**, Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite / 3.7 Flash / 3.1 Pro, Claude Sonnet 5, "GPT 6 Luna", plus open-weight models, 7,000 sessions [AGENT]: each rank lower reduced inspection probability by ≤0.0008 per LLM vs 0.0019 for humans (less than half the human linear penalty); inspection probability bottoms out at ranks 62–81 and rises toward the page bottom — a "middle penalty" significant in 12 of 14 LLM-effort conditions (lost-in-the-middle); for choice, the linear penalty ranges from zero to ~3× the human level; middle penalty on choice significant in 8 of 14 conditions, mostly at low reasoning effort, and **higher reasoning effort significantly reduced it in every model that had one**; LLMs averaged 1.63–5.83 inspections per session vs 1.12 for humans; proprietary LLMs never chose the outside option (vs ~34% of human click sessions ending without booking). — [arXiv 2608.22697v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22697v4)
- Incumbent Advantage (2026), skincare lists of 10 products, GPT-4o-mini, claude-sonnet-4-6, gemini-3-flash-preview: product parameters explained 82.4% of ranking variance, **list position 6.5%**, brand identity 1.2%. — [arXiv 2606.17443](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17443v1)

### Inferences
- In 2025–26 agents, position still matters, but the shape differs from human attention: humans show a steep monotonic rank decay; agents show a flatter decay with a mid-list dip and a direction/column preference that is idiosyncratic to the model and version.
- Because GPT-4.1 → GPT-5.1 reversed slot preferences, a seller cannot "buy the top-left slot" for AI traffic the way it does for humans; the robust human-analog is only "top row beats bottom row".
- The hotel-agent finding that reasoning effort removes the middle penalty but not necessarily the linear penalty suggests placement effects will shrink but not vanish as agents are given more compute.

### Gaps
- No 2025–26 study measures classic A/B/C/D MCQ token bias on GPT-5/Claude 4.x/Gemini 2.5 at the same granularity as Zheng et al.; the ACES/ABxLab results are grid/tab positions, not option letters.
- The "77% top half" statistic for Claude Sonnet 4 came from a secondary citation and was not confirmed in the ACES text I read.

## KQ4. Framing, loss aversion and reference-price anchors

### Takeaway
LLMs are clearly framing-sensitive, but the direction is unstable: GPT-4-class conversational models often show framing and loss-aversion effects *larger* than humans, several 2025–26 studies find reversals, and 2026 reasoning models (GPT-5.1, DeepSeek-R1) are nearly frame-invariant and close to expected-value maximizers. A framed discount ("26% off") moved product recommendations more than an actual 50% price cut in most of six models. No study of strikethrough MSRP / reference-price anchors on LLM value judgments was found.

### Cited Findings

**Framing**
- Suri 2023, GPT-3.5 [GPT-3.5-era]: item rated more effective when features described positively vs negatively with identical information. — [arXiv 2305.04400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04400)
- Echterhoff 2024 (admissions): framing Δ(admit−reject) = +0.165 GPT-3.5-turbo, **−0.405 GPT-4** (reverse direction), −0.060 Llama-2-13b [GPT-3.5-era]. — [arXiv 2403.00811](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.00811v3)
- GPT-4o (2024): "contradictory answers to positive vs negative framing of the same scenario" (inconsistent, no percentage). — [arXiv 2410.02820](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02820v1)
- gpt-4o-mini (2026) recruitment framing ("higher than X% of class" vs "lower than Y%"): acceptance 66% (33/50) positive frame vs 42% (21/50) negative, McNemar p = 0.0015, ω = 0.32. — [NHSJS](https://nhsjs.com/2026/a-study-of-cognitive-biases-in-large-language-models-in-decision-making-scenarios/)
- Kraishan 2025, 7 models: **framing reversed reliably** relative to the human-like direction; loss aversion, escalation and confirmation showed directional reversals; only five items per bias. — [arXiv 2610.10049](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10049)
- Nature Scientific Reports 2025 (social decisions): GPT-3.5 matched average human risk preference but "showed reversed framing effects"; GPT-4 "indiscriminately risk-averse across social contexts" [GPT-3.5-era]. — [Sci Rep 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-17188-7)
- Bias Beware (EMNLP 2025) **discount framing**: adding a framed discount (avg ~26%) to a product description raised recommendation visibility — LLaMA-70B +23.00, Claude 3.7 +37.40 on coffee machines — and "outperformed an actual 50% price cut in most models". — [arXiv 2502.01349](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01349v2)
- Hedonic/functional context cues (2026), 12 models incl. GPT 5.4: every model chose the premium option more often under hedonic than functional cues (p < .001), average more than 2× (justification cue +248%, payment-source cue +36%); but only 26% of model-cue combinations showed the human-like reweighting of price vs quality sensitivity. — [arXiv 2609.22225](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22225)
- Chen et al. PNAS 2023: GPT-3.5's GARP rationality "sensitive to contextual framing". — [arXiv 2305.12763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12763v3)

**Loss aversion / prospect theory**
- GPT-4o (2024): failed to account for expected value in >95% of loss-aversion vignettes; 86% chose the risk-free option in the certainty-effect task. — [arXiv 2410.02820](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02820v1)
- "Are LLMs rational investors?" (2024): GPT-4 shows pronounced loss-aversion bias on loss-framed queries [GPT-4-era]. — [arXiv 2402.12713](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.12713)
- Decision-making under uncertainty framework (2024): ChatGPT-4.0-Turbo, Claude-3-Opus, Gemini-1.0-pro "generally exhibit human-like patterns: risk aversion, loss aversion, and overweighting small probabilities". — [arXiv 2406.05972](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.05972v1)
- "Mind the (DH) Gap" (2026), 20 LLMs (Claude Haiku 4.5, GPT-4.1, GPT-5.1, Gemini 2.5 Flash/Pro, DeepSeek Chat/R1, Qwen, OLMo) vs 360 Prolific humans, four-parameter prospect-theory fit: **reasoning models correlate 0.86–1.00 with the expected-payoff benchmark; conversational models < 0.5**; reasoning models "largely insensitive to gain/loss framing" while conversational models are "highly sensitive"; GPT-4.1 near-random in losses with explicit prospects (βloss ≈ 0.01) but with implicit prospects βloss = 39 vs βgain = 0.01; neither group is especially human-like (best human match Claude Haiku 4.5 at r = 0.42); brief explanations increased rationality, math explanations reduced it for conversational models. — [arXiv 2602.15173](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.15173)
- "LLM economicus?" (2024): LLM behavior "neither entirely human-like nor entirely economicus-like"; most LLMs fail to keep consistent economic behavior across settings. — [arXiv 2408.02784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02784)
- Horton: GPT-4 fairness ratings fall by only 0.004–0.009 per $1 price increase (Kahneman 1986 snow-shovel-style price-gouging vignette). — [arXiv 2301.07543v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.07543v2)

**Reference prices / MSRP anchors / charm prices**
- Tool-Lab (2026), 8 models (Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite / 3.8 Flash / 3.1 Pro, "GPT 5.6 Luna/Terra/Sol", Claude Sonnet 5 / Opus 5), 16,400 sessions [AGENT]: with full information, 7 of 8 models weighted cents the same as whole dollars (no just-below/charm-price effect) and 6 of 8 weighted "20% off" savings the same as list-price reductions (Opus over-weighted discounts at 1.15×, Flash Lite under-weighted at 0.72×); agents were misled by $4.99/10oz vs $5.00/11oz traps only when attribute inspection was costly and the goal was vague (optimality fell from 0.89 to 0.29); normative choice in 99.3% of truncated trials; arithmetic errors 0.0%. — [arXiv 2609.28372](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28372v1)
- No study of strikethrough/"was $X now $Y" reference prices on LLM WTP or choice was found; the closest is the 2026 WTP benchmark (ChatGPT-4/GPT-4o synthetic WTP ~27% below human BDM WTP), which deliberately hid price ranges from humans to avoid anchoring. — [search result](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/414905330_How_price_sensitive_is_AI_Benchmarking_LLM-generated_willingness_to_pay_in_pricing_analytics)

### Inferences
- For a shopping agent, "framing" matters mainly as *text in the product description* (discount language, hedonic cues), where effects are large; it matters much less as *numerical presentation* (charm prices, percent-off vs dollar-off), where current agents with full information are nearly arithmetic-neutral.
- Loss-aversion-based nudges ("don't miss out", "final sale") have weak and inconsistent support on agents; ABxLab's negative-framing cues ("newer version available", "final sale") were not among the strong movers.
- The discount-framing > real-price-cut result (Bias Beware) is the closest analog to a reference-price effect and is the most actionable finding for sellers; it is also the one most likely to be treated as a manipulation by platforms.

### Gaps
- No LLM study of strikethrough MSRP / external reference price effects on choice or WTP.
- No LLM test of the "90% fat-free vs 10% fat" attribute-framing classic in a product-choice context with a 2025–26 model (Suri's GPT-3.5 result is the only direct analog).
- Loss-aversion coefficients (λ) comparable to the human ~2.0 are not reported in the sources read; "Mind the (DH) Gap" uses a decisiveness-ratio proxy instead.

## KQ5. Does bias attenuate with scale, reasoning models, or instruction tuning? Hyper-rationality?

### Takeaway
Scale alone does not reliably reduce bias (Malberg: no correlation; Bias Beware: 405B no more robust than 8B; context-cue paper: Gemma 27B three times as sensitive as 4B). Instruction tuning *increases* some classic biases (decoy, certainty, belief bias) while making models "hyper-rational" on CRT-type tasks. Reasoning models are substantially more expected-value-rational and frame-invariant on risky-choice tasks, and reasoning effort reduces position effects, but reasoning does not remove anchoring or product-description manipulation effects, and in some studies increases bias.

### Cited Findings
- Malberg et al. 2024/25: no clear correlation between size or Chatbot Arena score and biasedness across 20 models; bias profiles cluster by model family. — [arXiv 2410.15413](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.15413)
- "Planted in Pretraining, Swayed by Finetuning" 2025: pretraining backbone is the main source of bias patterns; finetuning data swaps ("cross-tuning") change profiles less than backbone identity. — [arXiv 2507.07186](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07186)
- Itzhak et al.: decoy, certainty and belief biases "more prevalent in instruction-tuned models" (Flan-T5, Mistral-Instruct, GPT-3.5, GPT-4). — [arXiv 2308.00225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00225)
- Hagendorff et al.: GPT-3 human-like intuitive errors on CRT; ChatGPT and GPT-4 "hyperrational". — [arXiv 2212.05206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.05206)
- Chen et al. PNAS: GPT-3.5 more GARP-rational than humans; the paper cites Aher et al. 2023's "hyper-accuracy distortion" (alignment making models unrealistically accurate/uniform). — [arXiv 2305.12763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12763v3)
- ACES (2025): single-attribute rationality improved sharply across generations — with a 1% price discount on an otherwise identical listing, Claude Sonnet 3.5 failed >63% and GPT-4o ~25%, GPT-4.1 ~9%; on a +0.1 rating edge, failures were 71.7% (GPT-4o), 28.7% (Claude Sonnet 4), 15–16% (GPT-4.1), 0% (Gemini 2.0 Flash); "the latest models fail below 1.7% on rating tests and almost never on price tests". Yet position bias persisted and *reversed* between GPT-4.1 and GPT-5.1. — [arXiv 2508.02630](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02630)
- "Mind the (DH) Gap" 2026: reasoning models (GPT-5.1, DeepSeek-R1 etc.) correlate 0.86–1.00 with expected payoff vs <0.5 for conversational models; frame- and order-invariant; "training for mathematical reasoning appears to be the main factor". — [arXiv 2602.15173](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.15173)
- Kraishan 2025: reasoning models were *not* less biased than non-reasoning siblings (Δ = +0.031 toward more bias, p = .157); more realized reasoning tokens did not reduce bias; "test-time reasoning should not be treated as a rationality guarantee". — [arXiv 2610.10049](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10049)
- Price negotiation (EMNLP-F 2025): o1 lost as much as GPT-4o to an unflagged seller anchor; only when warned did o1 do significantly better (−0.20 vs −0.32). — [arXiv 2508.21137](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21137v1)
- o1 clinical vignette study (Critical Care 2025): o1 showed no measurable cognitive bias in 7 of 10 vignettes; authors caution reasoning models are "still not entirely immune". — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13054-025-05591-5)
- Bias Beware 2025: LLaMA-405B and Claude 3.5 Sonnet were *more* likely than smaller models to promote a biased product to the top spot; Mistral Large 2 more robust; **Claude 3.7 with extended thinking showed similar manipulation effects to without**; a "focus on product features" system prompt did not remove effects (exclusivity still cut LLaMA-8B's rate by ~30 points). — [arXiv 2502.01349](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01349v2)
- Context-cue paper 2026: thinking mode "does not reduce choice context effects, and significantly increases them for Qwen 8B and 14B"; Gemma 27B ~3× as context-sensitive as Gemma 4B; two largest Qwen3 models less sensitive than the two smaller. — [arXiv 2609.22225](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22225)
- Hotel agents 2026: higher reasoning effort significantly reduced the mid-list choice penalty in every model that had one (non-significant at highest effort for Flash Lite, Pro, Sonnet; −63% for "Luna"); a position-neutral agent cost $0.011/session (Flash Lite high effort) vs $0.28 (Pro). — [arXiv 2608.22697v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22697v4)
- ABxLab 2025: o3 and o4-mini (reasoning) showed the largest rating effects (77.6 and 81.2pp) and still sizeable nudge effects when ratings and prices matched (48.4 and 38.5pp) — i.e., reasoning did not neutralize nudges; GPT-4.1 Nano showed an extreme +88.8pp primacy effect, far larger than larger siblings. — [arXiv 2509.25609v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25609v2)
- Echterhoff 2024: self-debiasing worked much better in GPT-4 than GPT-3.5 or Llama-2. — [arXiv 2403.00811](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.00811v3)
- Temporal preferences (2026): CoT in a 4B model "acts as a post-hoc justification engine that locks in present bias"; discount rates vary 100–400× from theoretical indifference points. — [arXiv 2606.05194](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.05194)

### Inferences
- "Hyper-rationality" is real but narrow: it shows up on tasks with a computable right answer (CRT, GARP, EV maximization, cheapest-item checks), which newer and reasoning models now pass. It does not generalize to preference-ordering tasks with no normative answer (position, brand, social-proof text), where bias is stable or growing with capability.
- For shopping agents, the practical prediction is a split: *numeric* tricks (charm prices, dominance-violating decoys, small price differences) will increasingly fail against capable agents; *textual/social* cues and *platform-structural* cues (badges, position, attribute completeness) will keep working.

### Gaps
- No study holds task constant across GPT-3.5 → GPT-4 → GPT-4o → GPT-5 → reasoning models for the full set of consumer biases; cross-generation comparisons above are pieced together from different papers.
- Numeric magnitudes for instruction-tuning increases in decoy effect (Itzhak) not extracted.

## KQ6. LLM-as-shopper benchmarks and agentic e-commerce evals: systematic choice patterns

### Takeaway
Across the 2025–26 agent shopping studies, agents (a) respond in the same direction as humans to ratings, price, order and nudges but with effects 3–10× larger, (b) are hypersensitive to ratings (a +0.1 star can be worth 25–67% of price; o4-mini 81pp for higher-rated), (c) prefer cheaper when ratings are matched, (d) reward "best seller"/"purchased by 50,000+"/expert-authority text and platform endorsements, penalize "Sponsored", and are *not* moved (or are repelled) by scarcity, (e) concentrate choices on a few "modal" products rather than dispersing like humans, and (f) treat brand name as a tiebreaker that any small spec advantage overrides. Shopping-MMLU/WebShop-style capability benchmarks measure task success, not choice patterns, and did not surface bias results in this search.

### Cited Findings
- **ACES ("What Is Your AI Agent Buying?", Yale/Columbia/Chicago, Aug–Dec 2025; ACM Web Conf 2026)**, mock marketplace, Claude Sonnet 4, GPT-4.1, Gemini 2.5 Flash, then Claude Opus 4.5, GPT-5.1, Gemini 3.0 Pro Preview [AGENT]:
  - Price elasticities implied by log-price coefficients ≈ −1.6 to −2.2, "similar to human SKU-level estimates".
  - +0.1 rating lifts a 10%-baseline product to 15.4% (Claude Sonnet 4), 20.3% (GPT-4.1), 16.0% (Gemini 2.5 Flash); doubling review count allows price increases of ~19% / 37% / 17%.
  - "Overall Pick" badge: 10% → 24.3% / 19.9% / 42.6% (price headroom +92% / +65% / +138%). "Sponsored": 10% → 8.9% / 8.0% / 7.9%. "Only X remaining" scarcity: weakly negative or insignificant (small positive only for Gemini 3.0 Pro Preview).
  - Choice homogeneity: Fitbit Inspire chosen 45% by Claude Sonnet 4, ~25% by GPT-4.1 and Gemini 2.5 Flash, 77% by Claude Opus 4.5, 6% by GPT-5.1; one iPhone case brand 62.6% under GPT-4.1 vs ~5% under GPT-5.1; Amazon Basics stapler dominates, Arrow never selected. Authors call this a "departure from human consumers, whose preferences are more dispersed"; "model updates act as demand shocks".
  - Seller agent rewriting descriptions gained +3.66 to +14.89 pp market share depending on buyer model (e.g., GPT-4.1 seller vs Gemini 2.5 Flash buyer +14.79; GPT-5.1 seller vs Gemini 3.0 Pro buyer +0.32 n.s.); ~33% of category-model pairs significant; office lamps +7.1 to +80.4 pp; some edits backfired.
  — [arXiv 2508.02630](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02630); [Columbia summary](https://business.columbia.edu/insights/digital-future-initiative/ai-shopping-agents); [Kantar write-up: removing a key attribute cuts selection probability 20–40%; a 4.4 vs 4.1 rating gap "frequently changed which product ranked first"; complete machine-readable attributes beat stronger marketing](https://www.kantar.com/north-america/Inspiration/Retail/New-study-shows-why-AI-agents-shop-like-super-consumers)
- **ABxLab (Sept 2025)**, OneStopMarket/WebArena, 17 models, >80,000 episodes, 30 Prolific humans × 50 decisions [AGENT]:
  - Higher-rated option gained 30–80pp in 14/17 models (o4-mini 81.2, o3 77.6, GPT-5 61.8, Claude Sonnet 4 46.7) vs humans +5.0pp.
  - Cheaper option preferred by 13/17 models (15.2pp o3 to 34.3pp GPT-4o Mini) vs humans +9.4pp; under matched ratings Llama 4 Maverick +93.2pp; but "price doubling produced only modest effects".
  - Nudges (authority: "highly recommended by leading experts", Wirecutter pick; social proof: "best seller", "purchased by 50,000+"; scarcity: "only for the next hour", "limited edition"; negative framing: "newer version available", "final sale"; incentives: free shipping, BOGO): with ratings and prices matched, best seller and BOGO shifted 10–60pp in 14/17 models; Claude Sonnet 4 +55.9, GPT-4o +62.1, GPT-4.1 +57.2, GPT-5 +53.3; humans +9.9pp. **Wirecutter (authority) had the largest effect, then BOGO, then best seller.** BOGO moved durables 40.6% (cell phones) and consumables 60.9% (cookies).
  - Average attribute sensitivity: humans ~7%; models 13% (Claude 3.5 Haiku) to 31% (Claude Sonnet 4).
  - Explicit user profiles "act as switches that override other cues".
  - Conclusion: agents are "strongly biased choosers even without being subject to the cognitive constraints that shape human biases"; human-oriented choice-architecture regulation "may understate risks".
  — [arXiv 2509.25609v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25609v2)
- **Bias Beware (EMNLP 2025)**, LLaMA 8B/70B/405B, Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet, Mistral Large 2; fictitious coffee machines/cameras/books + real Amazon laptops and pet toys [AGENT, recommendation lists]: social proof is the most effective lever (Claude 3.5 coffee machines +334% relative recommendation rate; expert-written social proof +42.12 rate points on Claude 3.7; offsets a ~0.27-star rating drop); discount framing beats a real 50% price cut; **scarcity and exclusivity *reduce* visibility** (exclusivity ~−45% relative rate, ~−116% position; scarcity on Claude 3.5 −13.5 rate points, ~−30% relative; on real laptop data exclusivity cut average rate from ~71% to 56%); decoy/contrast/authority/storytelling mixed. — [arXiv 2502.01349](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01349v2); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1140.pdf)
- **Incumbent Advantage (2026)**, GPT-4o-mini, claude-sonnet-4-6, gemini-3-flash-preview, 1 real brand + 9 fictional [AGENT]: identical specs → real brand chosen 100% of 670 trials; with clearly better fictional specs → real brand 1.7–4.6%; fictional brand wins 50% at +0.075 stars, 7.3% discount, or 1.6× reviews; persuasion text breakthrough rates (baseline ~4%): **Authority 73.3%, Social Proof 50.7%, Anchoring 12.9%, Scarcity 11.7%, Loss Aversion 9.6%**; Authority worth ≈ +0.17 stars / 15.3% discount / 1.9× reviews; when all nine fictional brands adopt authority language the incumbent recovers to 93.8% and non-adopters get zero recommendations in 4,745 trials. — [arXiv 2606.17443](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17443v1)
- **Hotel agents (2026)**: bookings concentrate on high review scores and low prices (review-score coefficient ≈0.04–0.05 per point); LLM agents inspect more listings than humans (1.63–5.83 vs 1.12) and never choose "no booking"; authors conclude "displayed attributes may matter more than placement". — [arXiv 2608.22697v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22697v4)
- **Tool-Lab (2026)**: under free inspection, choices track the full attribute set (unit price computed correctly); under costly inspection with vague goals optimality fell to 0.29–0.48; stating a precise criterion ("lowest price per ounce") restored it to 0.83–0.88; retailers advised to expose unit price, consumers to state precise criteria. — [arXiv 2609.28372](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28372v1)
- Cognitive-bias injection in product descriptions (EMNLP 2025, separate from Bias Beware per search result): when prompts include a price cap, models "tended to return options sorted solely by that constraint, disregarding their actual relevance or features". — [search snippet](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1140.pdf)
- Source preferences (ICLR 2026): 12 LLMs show "strong and predictable source preferences" that can outweigh content and persist under instructions to ignore them; includes an Amazon seller-selection case study (results not in abstract). — [arXiv 2602.15456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15456)
- Conversational steering of humans (2024): a GPT-4-powered shopping assistant that subtly steered participants flipped 36% of human choices without detection — relevant as the mirror image (agent nudging the human). — [arXiv 2409.12143](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12143v1)
- Vertical tacit collusion simulation (2026): when a platform sets rankings and sellers exploit agent biases, simulated consumer harm "more than double" that of either acting alone. — [arXiv 2601.03061](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03061v1)
- Amazon Shopping MMLU, WebShop, ShoppingBench: these appeared in my searches only as capability/task-success benchmarks; none of the search results reported systematic preference patterns (cheapest/most-reviewed/top-listed) from them. — no source found reporting such patterns

### Inferences
- The human nudge toolkit splits cleanly for agents:
  - **Works, often harder than on humans:** social proof text, authority/expert endorsement text, platform endorsement badges, "best seller", BOGO/free shipping, framed discounts, higher star rating (even +0.1), more reviews, complete structured attributes, top-row placement.
  - **Works but unpredictably (model/version-specific):** column/slot position, order of presentation (primacy vs recency), brand as tiebreaker, decoy options.
  - **Weak, null or reversed:** scarcity/urgency ("only 2 left", "next hour"), exclusivity, loss-framed "final sale", charm prices and percent-vs-dollar framing when the agent has full information, "Sponsored" labels (negative).
- Agents' choice concentration ("modal products") means a nudge that works on the model produces winner-take-most market outcomes rather than the marginal share shifts seen in humans — this is the main structural difference from human nudging, and it is why small cue changes translate into large share swings in ACES/ABxLab.
- The strongest defense available to the consumer is also the simplest: an explicit criterion in the delegation prompt (ABxLab "profiles act as switches"; Tool-Lab precise goal restores optimality), which suggests choice architecture matters most when the principal under-specifies the task.

### Gaps
- No study reports choice patterns from Shopping MMLU, WebShop or ShoppingBench runs; those benchmarks score task completion.
- The Amazon seller-selection results from the ICLR 2026 source-preference paper (do agents prefer Amazon-as-seller or lowest price?) were not accessible in the abstract.
- All agent studies use mock or sandboxed marketplaces (ACES, OneStopMarket, Tool-Lab, simulated hotel page); none measures agent behavior on a live retailer with real inventory, and most are preprints.
- Human baselines are small (ABxLab n = 30; hotel data from a 2018 study) and were not run under the same two-tab/grid interface fidelity as the agents in every case.

## KQ7. Synthetic-respondent evidence vs agent evidence: does the [SIM] literature transfer to [AGENT] questions?

### Takeaway
The simulated-human literature (Horton, Brand/Israeli/Ngwe, Goli & Singh, Chen et al.) establishes that LLMs *can* reproduce aggregate human bias patterns when prompted as humans, sometimes exaggerating them when told to be bias-prone — but these designs ask the model to predict a human, which is a different task from the model choosing on its own account, and they do not license conclusions about agent susceptibility.

### Cited Findings
- Status-quo chatbot study (2026), GPT-4.1 / 4.1-mini / GPT-5 / 5-mini simulating 1,100 Prolific participants [SIM]: minimal and "act naturally" prompts reproduced the aggregate status-quo effect (budget allocation: humans 0.51 status-quo vs 0.23 neutral); an explicit "bias-prone" prompt produced uniformly large effects (Cohen's h ≈ 2.2–2.8) including a false positive where humans showed none; individual-level accuracy only 0.46–0.69; replacing real participant replies with random text barely reduced accuracy, so models "rely on the scenario and general patterns rather than individual cues"; GPT-4.1 family outperformed GPT-5 family here. — [arXiv 2602.05597](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05597)
- Goli & Singh: "directly using LLMs to elicit preferences in place of human respondents could be misleading". — [INFORMS](https://informs.org/News-Room/INFORMS-Releases/News-Releases/New-Research-Provides-Insights-on-How-Generative-AI-Can-Mimic-Human-Responses-to-Preference)
- Brand/Israeli/Ngwe: GPT-3/3.5 as respondent reproduces realistic WTP structure and income effects. — [HBS WP](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/23-062_47458a4a-8be2-4b53-a3a3-9b5af20578b1.pdf)
- Market-experiment LLM agents (2025): LLMs "do not adhere strictly to rational expectations, displaying bounded rationality, similarly to human participants". — [arXiv 2505.07457](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07457v1)
- ABxLab explicitly contrasts the two roles: agents reproduce human-direction effects "without being subject to the cognitive constraints that shape human biases". — [arXiv 2509.25609v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25609v2)
- "Do LLMs choose like humans?" (2026): behavioral similarity "does not establish shared mechanisms"; LLM decision mechanisms "appear distinct from human ones". — [arXiv 2609.22225](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22225)

### Inferences
- When an agent is given a persona ("shop as a 35-year-old budget-conscious parent"), the [SIM] results become relevant again: persona prompts can import human biases, and explicit bias-prone instructions overshoot them. Agent designers who add personas for personalization may inadvertently re-introduce human-style status quo or framing effects.
- Conversely, the default assistant persona behaves like the [AGENT] studies: cue-hypersensitive, concentrated, numerically rational.

### Gaps
- No study runs the *same* consumer-choice nudges on the same model in both "predict what a human would choose" and "choose for the user" framings to quantify the gap.
