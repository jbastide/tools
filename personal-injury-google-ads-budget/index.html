How much should a personal injury law firm spend on Google Ads?

A personal injury law firm should budget at least 3 times its expected cost per lead each month for Google Ads. Below that, a month with zero leads can’t be told apart from bad luck, so the firm can’t tell whether the campaign works. This is the PPC Gumball Rule from Copy for Originals, applied to law firm budgets.

By Copy for Originals · Published October 5, 2026 · Last updated October 5, 2026

The formula

Minimum monthly budget = 3 × cost per click ÷ conversion rate

At $100 a click and a 5% conversion rate, a lead costs $2,000 and the minimum budget is $6,000 a month. At $200 a click, it’s $12,000.

This guide covers Google Ads Search campaigns for personal injury lead generation. Plan with your own cost per click, not an industry average.

How much should a personal injury law firm spend on Google Ads at minimum?

The minimum useful Google Ads budget for a personal injury law firm is 3 × its expected cost per lead per month, where expected cost per lead = cost per click ÷ conversion rate. That’s the PPC Gumball Rule checkpoint: the spend at which zero leads stops being plausible bad luck (about a 5% chance). With less, the firm can still get leads, but a month with none tells it nothing.

What does a personal injury lead cost on Google Ads?

A personal injury lead from Google Ads costs the firm’s cost per click divided by its conversion rate. At $100 a click and 5%, that’s $2,000. At $200 a click and 8%, it’s $2,500. The two numbers that matter are both in the firm’s own account.

Don’t plan with an industry average: averages mix cheap long-tail searches with the contested keywords that actually bring injury cases, so they can be several times too low.

Is $1,500 a month enough for personal injury Google Ads?

Usually not. At $100 a click, $1,500 buys 15 clicks. At a 5% conversion rate, the chance of zero leads from 15 clicks is 0.9515, about 46%, so nearly half of months would bring nothing even with a campaign set up well. The firm can’t tell bad luck from a broken campaign, and most firms in that spot quit before they find out.

A small budget works better on cheaper ground: Local Services Ads (pay per lead), longer and more specific keywords, or a smaller service area where clicks cost less. Run the numbers again with the cheaper cost per click.

Is Google Ads worth it for personal injury lawyers?

Google Ads is worth it for a personal injury firm when a signed case is worth several times what it costs in ads to sign one. Cost per signed case = cost per lead ÷ the share of leads that sign. At a $2,000 cost per lead and 1 in 5 leads signing, a case costs $10,000 in ads. Compare that to the firm’s average fee per case.

Track signed cases, not just leads. Import them into Google Ads as offline conversions so Smart Bidding learns which searches bring cases, not just calls.

FAQ

How much do personal injury lawyers spend on Google Ads?

It varies more with city and cost per click than with firm size. The useful question is the minimum: 3 × the firm’s expected cost per lead per month, by the PPC Gumball Rule. At $100 a click and 5%, that’s $6,000 a month; at $300 a click, $18,000.

How long should a law firm test Google Ads before deciding?

Until it has spent 3 × its expected cost per lead, however many days that takes. A firm spending $200 a day with a $2,000 expected cost per lead reaches the $6,000 checkpoint in 30 days.

What if we get zero leads after reaching the checkpoint?

Check conversion tracking first (submit a test lead), then the landing page form and phone number, the search terms report and location settings. The PPC Troubleshooter walks through each check.

Limitations

  • The budget is only as good as the cost per click and conversion rate you enter. Check both against your own account after the first month.
  • The PPC Gumball Rule treats clicks as independent; repeat clicks, bots or one bad keyword make zero leads more likely than the math says.
  • Leads aren’t cases. A campaign can pass the checkpoint with leads and still lose money if few of them sign.
  • This guide is about Google Ads budgets, not legal advertising rules. Check your state bar’s advertising rules separately.
