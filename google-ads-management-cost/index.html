Google Ads management cost: what agencies publish on their own price pages

Agencies that publish a Google Ads management price start at a median of $500 a month: that is the middle starting fee of 19 agencies pricing in US dollars, which run from $199 to $3,500. These are businesses that print a price on their own site, not the whole market. The large agencies we looked up print no fee we could read, so these figures sit lower than what large accounts pay. Of the 28 agencies and freelancers in the analysis, 20 charge a fixed monthly fee, 7 print a percentage of ad spend (5% to 20%) and 11 charge a setup fee.

By Jesse Bastide, Copy for Originals · Published October 31, 2026 · Last updated October 31, 2026 · Data (CSV)

How this was made. Every price, date and quotation from an agency, survey or Google page was checked word for word against the saved text of that page on October 31, 2026. The counts, medians and middle half are our own arithmetic from the data file, and the search counts in the method are from our own record of the searches.

How much does Google Ads management cost per month?

A median of $500 a month to start, among agencies that publish their price: that is the middle of 19 starting fees in US dollars, with the middle half between $375 and $949.50 and the full range $199 to $3,500. The highest monthly fee any of them prints is $6,000 (Stryde). These are prices read on the agencies’ own sites on October 31, 2026, not a survey of what every agency charges.

Most agencies that publish a price charge a fixed monthly fee: 20 of the 28 in this analysis. How do I compare Google Ads management quotes?

Put every quote on the same footing: the fee at your own monthly ad spend, any setup fee, the minimum term and notice period, who owns the account and what the fee covers, all in writing. Google’s rules already require an agency that charges a separate management fee to tell new customers in writing before the first purchase and to show the fee on every invoice.

  1. Ask for the fee at your spend, not the starting price. JumpFly’s flat fee runs from $900 to $3,000 a month across its tiers, and Third Marble Marketing’s own chart shows its $499 plus 5% plan as $549 on a $1,000 click budget.
  2. Add any percentage to the base. Bruce Clay’s 12% to 18% of spend comes on top of a $2,000 base fee. With a percentage and a minimum, find which one applies to you: PPC Buyers’ $400 minimum applies below $3,500 of spend, 12% above it.
  3. Ask about the setup fee and whether it can be waived. Eleven of the 28 charge one; GrossiWeb waives its $500 on a six-month term.
  4. Ask for the minimum term and the notice period. Some are month to month, some ask for three months first, and Mainstreethost’s starting price assumes annual billing.
  5. Get what the fee covers in writing, and what costs extra. Landing pages, display creative and custom dashboards are the add-ons agencies named in WordStream’s 2023 survey, as MediaPost reported it.
  6. Ask who owns the account. GrossiWeb says “you keep the account and all data if you leave”; ThinkProfits promises “full account ownership on your side”; Advantage Attorney Marketing says “You own everything.”
  7. Ask for Google’s exact cost in every report. Google tells agencies sharing cost data to “report the exact amount charged by Google, exclusive of any fees that you charge.”
  8. Read introductory prices as introductory. Knowurwhy’s rate is for founding clients, Footbridge Media’s $149 is the first month only, Bootstrap Creative’s $950 is a 90-day pilot, and G3 Group’s free setup is a “limited time offer”.

Google’s third-party policy puts the disclosure rule this way: “If you charge a management fee (separate from the cost of Google Ads or AdWords Express), you must clearly inform customers.”

Hiring for a law firm? Four law firm agencies are in this data (PaperStreet, Advantage Attorney Marketing, Grow Law and Skill Mammoth). The law firm Google Ads agency scorecard turns the questions above into checks a firm can run in its own account, and Google Ads for law firms covers what clicks and leads cost and how much to spend.

How were these prices collected?

By reading the price pages of 40 agencies and freelancers that publish a Google Ads or PPC management price on their own site, found through web searches and read once, on October 31, 2026. It is not a random sample: it is the businesses that print a price and that the searches surfaced, so it leans toward small and mid-size shops that sell on price or transparency.

  • The bias runs one way. A business that prints its price is usually a small or mid-size shop, often selling to small local accounts, and many of the pages are cost guides written by the sellers. The large agencies we looked up (Disruptive Advertising, KlientBoost, SimpleTiger, WordStream) print no fee we could read, so retainers of $10,000 a month and up are missing and the medians sit lower than what larger accounts pay.
  • Searches. Four core queries, each read through every result in order: “google ads management pricing”, “ppc management pricing”, “google ads management cost per month” and “ppc agency pricing plans”. They returned about 80 result links, most of them guides, and nine agencies with a price of their own. About 35 variant queries followed (wordings such as “packages”, “starting at”, “flat rate” and “no setup fee”; the UK, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, Ireland and one Swedish query; and niches: law firms, dentists, contractors, ecommerce, B2B SaaS, freelancers, Google Ad Grants), then look-ups of agencies that guides list with prices. Of the agencies looked up by name, only LYFE Marketing, JumpFly and Third Marble Marketing had a readable price on their own site. Four law firm agencies read for the site’s earlier law firm research (PaperStreet, Advantage Attorney Marketing, Grow Law, Skill Mammoth) were read again.
  • US-only search index. The search tool used a US English index, so businesses outside the US are few and were found only by country or name queries.
  • What counts as a row. The page belongs to the agency or freelancer and states a price it charges: a fee, a “from” price or a plan table. A market range with no price of the author’s own does not count. 87 other sites were reached and skipped: 46 guides or blogs with market ranges only, 19 that returned an error, timed out or had unreadable text when fetched, 10 with plans but no fee or “call for pricing”, 4 software vendors, 2 directories or marketplaces, 2 left out (a spam mirror domain and a free-hosting address whose operator couldn’t be verified), and 4 used only as survey or official sources.
  • Inclusion rule for the figures above (the first rule a row fails is its reason, in the exclusion_reason column). R1: the page states a fee the business itself charges. R2: the service is management of paid Google Ads (Ad Grants management, where Google’s grant pays for the clicks, is out). R3: the currency is US, Canadian, New Zealand or Australian dollars, pounds or euros. R4: the currency is known: the page names it, or the price is in “$” and the business’s country is shown by an address, a local phone number or a statement on the page or its own contact page; a toll-free number, an “LLC” or a “$” from a business in a non-dollar country doesn’t count. R5: a starting fee can be read (blank where the page contradicts itself).
  • Result: 28 included, 12 excluded. R1: 1 (Digital Logic, whose only range describes agencies in general). R2: 2 (REACHRIGHT and Getting Attention, both Ad Grants agencies). R3: 2 (SB Genus in Indian rupees, Kolonell in CFA francs). R4: 7 (PPC.io, Straight North, WebFX, Adlytic Marketing, Fusion Arc Hosting, Mike Khorev, HAW Company). R5: none, because PPC.io, whose page prints both $2,000 and $3,000 a month for the same flat service, had already failed R4. Straight North and WebFX are probably US firms, but none of their pages we could read shows it. Included rows by currency: US dollars 19, pounds 3, Canadian dollars 2, New Zealand dollars 2, euros 1, Australian dollars 1.
  • Calculations. Medians and the middle half (first to third quartile, inclusive method) are computed per currency from starting_monthly_fee in the included rows. Currencies are never converted.
  • Read once. Every price was read on one day, October 31, 2026. Most pages carry no date; the data marks them “undated, checked 2026-10-31”. Prices change, and what a fee includes and the contract terms are the agencies’ own words.
  • Who made this. Copy for Originals, which publishes this site, sells Google Ads management too. Its prices are not in the data and are not compared here.
