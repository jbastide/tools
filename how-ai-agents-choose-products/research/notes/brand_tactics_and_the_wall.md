# Brand tactics for earning AI recommendations, and whether the "third-party wall" is surmountable (2024–2026 evidence)

Scope note for the writer: every finding below is tagged as **[MEASURED]** (controlled experiment, correlation study with disclosed N, or log/traffic data), **[VENDOR]** (data published by a company selling AI-visibility tooling or agency services; often real data but unaudited and selectively framed), **[PLATFORM]** (official statement by OpenAI/Google/Perplexity/Amazon/Reddit/FTC), or **[ASSERTED]** (practitioner opinion with no disclosed data). Several primary pages (openai.com, searchengineland.com, lightspeedhq.com, help.openai.com) returned 403/404 to the fetch tool; where I rely on secondary summaries of them, I say so.

## Key Question 1: What does the Princeton GEO paper show, and have later studies confirmed or weakened it?

### Takeaway
The original paper shows large relative lifts (roughly +28% to +43% on its visibility metric) from adding quotations, statistics and citations to a page, with keyword stuffing slightly negative, and much bigger gains for pages that start lower in the source set. Later benchmarks on realistic retrieval pipelines and newer models (C-SEO Bench, NeurIPS 2025; E-GEO, Nov 2025) substantially weaken this: most on-page tactics are ineffective or harmful once the engine is modern, the retrieval is realistic, and competitors also adopt them; what survives is "genuinely answer the query, in structured, factual form."

### Cited Findings
- **[MEASURED] Core result.** Aggarwal et al. (KDD 2024) built GEO-bench (10,000 queries; 8K/1K/1K train/val/test) and tested nine content-rewriting methods on a GPT-3.5-based generative engine. On Position-Adjusted Word Count (PAWC), baseline = 19.5; Quotation Addition 27.8 (+42.6%), Statistics Addition 25.9 (+32.8%), Fluency Optimization 25.1 (+28.7%), Cite Sources 24.9 (+27.7%), Technical Terms 23.1 (+18.5%), Easy-to-Understand 22.2 (+13.8%), Authoritative 21.8 (+11.8%), Unique Words 20.7 (+6.2%), Keyword Stuffing 17.8 (−8.7%). On Subjective Impression (SI), baseline 19.3; Quotation 24.7 (+28%), Statistics 23.7 (+22.8%), Authoritative 22.9 (+18.7%). Headline claims "up to 40%" / "41% and 28%" — [Aggarwal et al., arXiv 2311.09735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735)
- **[MEASURED] Perplexity.ai validation** (200-query subset): Quotation Addition +20.7% PAWC / +29.9% SI; Statistics Addition +8.7% PAWC / +37.2% SI; Keyword Stuffing −9.1% PAWC. Combining Fluency + Statistics beat any single method by >5.5% — [Aggarwal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735)
- **[MEASURED] Lower-ranked sources gained most, top-ranked lost.** For sources at rank 5 in the retrieved set: Cite Sources +115.1%, Quotation +99.7%, Statistics +97.9%. For rank-1 sources the same tactics were negative: −30.3%, −22.9%, −20.6%. The authors frame this as evidence GEO could help smaller creators compete, but note it was not tested on live search rankings — [Aggarwal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735)
- **[MEASURED] Domain dependence.** Best tactic varied by query category: Authoritative worked for Debate/History/Science; Fluency for Business/Science/Health; Cite Sources for Statement/Facts/Law & Government; Quotation for People & Society/Explanation/History; Statistics for Law & Government/Debate/Opinion. Note: no e-commerce/product category was in the taxonomy — [Aggarwal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735)
- **[MEASURED] Weakening replication: C-SEO Bench (NeurIPS 2025 Datasets & Benchmarks).** Tests question answering and product recommendation (three domains each) and varies how many competing documents adopt the tactic. Authors conclude "most current C-SEO methods are largely ineffective," often hurt a document's ranking, and that "traditional SEO strategies" (raising the source's rank within the LLM's context) work significantly better; gains shrink as adopters grow — the problem is "congested and zero-sum" — [C-SEO Bench, arXiv 2506.11097](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11097). A secondary review of the paper reports that adding statistics lowered ranking in 19 of 24 tested settings and that position in the context window predicted citation better than content tricks — [Mario Ottmann review](https://marioottmann.com/articles/what-geo-research-actually-shows) (secondary; I could not verify these two numbers against the full paper).
- **[MEASURED] E-commerce replication: E-GEO (Nov 2025).** 13,747 multi-sentence consumer product queries, each with 10 retrieved Amazon listings; five re-ranking engines (GPT-5, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Gemini 3 Flash Preview, DeepSeek V3.2, Llama 4 Maverick); results on 2,000 test queries, metric = mean rank improvement. Hand-crafted heuristics mostly failed: "Authoritative tone" was negative on all five engines (−0.07 to −1.40 on GPT-5); "Fluent" negative on four of five; "Advertisement" −1.82; "Minimalist" −1.49; "Storytelling" (negative control) −4.36; FAQ +0.05 and Format (headings/bullets) +0.02 were marginal. Simply asking a strong LLM to rewrite the listing (one-sentence prompt) gave +0.56 (GPT-5) / +0.57 (Claude). Meta-optimized prompts converged on a stable pattern: explicit ranking goal, alignment with user intent, keywords and synonyms, structured sections and bullets, use cases, FAQ-style answers, avoidance of keyword stuffing, and a retained factuality clause — [E-GEO, arXiv 2511.20867](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.20867v2)
- **[MEASURED] E-GEO on manipulation.** With a simple in-prompt defense (re-ranker asked to flag misleading descriptions), "Superlatives" and "Emotional" rewrites were flagged 99–100% of the time and GPT-5/Claude gave them little or no rank gain (superlatives −4.14 on GPT-5). Optimization-based red teaming cut flag rates by ~60 points, but the attack prompts "drifted toward careful, fact-grounded prose" — i.e., the only way to beat the defense was to stop being manipulative — [E-GEO](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.20867v2)
- **[MEASURED, preprint] Structure over body text.** SAGEO Arena (Feb 2026 preprint) reportedly found body-text optimization can hurt retrieval while enriching structural fields (schema, meta descriptions, heading hierarchy) lifted retrieval ~22%; a separate 2026 preprint found 43% of topically relevant pages earn zero AI citations, mostly because no passage stands alone as a self-contained answer — both via [Mario Ottmann review](https://marioottmann.com/articles/what-geo-research-actually-shows) citing [arXiv 2602.12187](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12187) and [arXiv 2603.09296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09296) (secondary; not fetched directly).
- **[ASSERTED, secondary]** A single-author 2026 arXiv survey of 45 GEO studies reportedly concluded no reviewed technique showed a stable, cross-platform causal effect on discoverability — surfaced in search results via [DEV Community](https://dev.to/juanauriti/google-said-llmstxt-does-nothing-i-checked-what-137210-domains-actually-got-2dka); I could not locate the preprint itself.

### Inferences
- The Princeton lifts are real within their setup but are (a) measured on a 2023-era GPT-3.5 engine, (b) on informational not commercial queries, and (c) in a fixed five-source context where the page was already retrieved. They say nothing about getting *into* the retrieved set, which is the actual barrier for an unmentioned brand.
- The "rank-5 sources gain +100%" result is the paper's most relevant finding for small brands: once you are in the candidate set, better-written, evidence-rich content can win share of answer. But E-GEO and C-SEO Bench show that on 2025 models, tone tricks ("authoritative", "fluent") reverse sign, and only content that genuinely answers the query in structured, factual form helps.
- Net: on-page GEO is a necessary-but-not-sufficient hygiene step whose marginal value is shrinking as models improve and as competitors adopt it.

### Gaps
- No study was found that re-runs the exact Princeton methods on GPT-4o/GPT-5-class engines with live retrieval and reports per-tactic lifts; C-SEO Bench is the closest and its full per-tactic tables were not retrievable.
- No academic study isolates the effect of schema.org Product/Review/FAQ markup specifically on ChatGPT/Perplexity product recommendations.

## Key Question 2: Are there documented case studies with data of small/DTC brands gaining AI visibility through specific actions?

### Takeaway
I found aggregate traffic/conversion data (mostly vendor- or agency-sourced) showing AI referrals growing fast and converting well, but no named small/DTC brand case study with before/after AI-visibility data tied to a specific tactic. The one independent multi-month tracking test found a small B2B client essentially invisible (4 mentions vs. 47 for the market leader) despite monitoring. This absence is itself a finding.

### Cited Findings
- **[VENDOR] Visibility Labs, 94 seven/eight-figure ecommerce brands, Jan–Dec 2025:** ChatGPT referral sessions grew from 1,544 (Jan) to 18,202 (Dec) while non-branded organic grew 17%; ChatGPT converted at 1.81% vs 1.39% for non-branded organic — reported via [ALM Corp summary](https://almcorp.com/blog/chatgpt-vs-organic-search-conversion-rate/) (secondary).
- **[MEASURED, secondary] Similarweb:** brands recommended in ChatGPT were 2.5x more likely to get a site visit within seven days than non-recommended peers; much of the effect shows up as branded search, not ChatGPT referrals — via [ALM Corp](https://almcorp.com/news/chatgpt-ai-recommendations-2-5x-traffic-similarweb-study/) (secondary).
- **[PLATFORM/VENDOR, secondary] Shopify Q1 2026:** AI-referred orders grew ~13x year over year; Adobe: AI-referred traffic to US retail +393% YoY, converting 42% better than non-AI traffic — via [Pepper benchmark report](https://www.pepper.inc/blog/dtc-brand-visibility-ai-search-benchmark-2026/) (secondary; Shopify and Adobe primary posts not fetched).
- **[MEASURED, independent, N=1]** A practitioner tracked one client (corporate events agency) with Peec, Otterly and Profound for 3 months across 50 queries × 4 engines: the client logged 4 mentions (all on Perplexity, none on ChatGPT or Gemini) vs 47 for the market leader — [Roberto Serra](https://www.roberto-serra.com/en/geo/measuring-ai-visibility/tools/ai-visibility-tracking-tool-i-tested-peec-otterly-profound-3-months/)
- **[MEASURED, independent]** A 30-day, 40-prompt comparison found all three tools (Profound 22/40 prompts with mentions, Otterly 19, Peec 24) showed the most week-to-week volatility on category prompts — [Design Copy](https://designcopy.net/en/profound-otterly-peec-ai-visibility-tracking-2026/)
- **[MEASURED] Semrush/Indig "ghost citations":** of 3,981 domain appearances across ChatGPT, Gemini, AI Overviews and AI Mode, 61.7% were "ghost citations" (page linked as a source but brand never named in the answer); only 13.2% were both cited and mentioned — [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/the-ghost-citations-study/)
- **[VENDOR] Profound ChatGPT Shopping study (201,137 prompt runs, 812,190 product cards, June 18–25 2026):** top-ranked product cards had a median of 787 reviews vs 352 for bottom-ranked; GPT "budget/premium" tags on 2.39% of top cards vs 0.98% bottom; promo pricing 1.19% vs 1.05%; URL length and product-name length showed no difference — [Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/chatgpt-shopping-end-to-end-breakdown)
- **[VENDOR] Otterly** published an experiment on one award win across 32 prompts (result not visible in snippet) — [Otterly blog index](https://otterly.ai/blog/) (unverified).
- **[MEASURED, weak] Schema test:** Evergrow Marketing ran a LocalBusiness schema test Feb 27–Apr 4, 2026 and reported no effect on SERP or Maps rank but "positive influences coming from some LLMs, including ChatGPT" (no effect sizes in snippet) — [Evergrow Marketing](https://evergrowmarketing.com/how-schema-affects-google-and-ai/) (agency-sourced).
- **[MEASURED] llms.txt does nothing measurable:** SE Ranking analysis of ~300,000 domains found no link between having the file and AI citations; an Ahrefs log analysis of 137,210 domains found 97% of llms.txt files got zero bot requests in May 2026; GO MO Group tested on 4 clients for six months with no measurable AI-visibility gain — via [DEV Community write-up](https://dev.to/juanauriti/google-said-llmstxt-does-nothing-i-checked-what-137210-domains-actually-got-2dka) and [GO MO Group](https://www.gomogroup.com/en/insight/we-tested-llms-txt-so-you-dont-have-to-heres-what-the-data-actually-says/); Google's Mueller/Illyes say Google does not use it — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-says-normal-seo-works-for-ranking-in-ai-overviews-and-llms-txt-wont-be-used-459422)

### Inferences
- The pattern across vendor datasets is consistent: AI referral volume is growing fast from a small base and converts well, which raises the *stakes* of visibility but says nothing about how an unmentioned brand gets in.
- The absence of any disclosed, named, before/after DTC case study from Profound, Peec, Otterly, Scrunch, Goodie or Athena, despite strong commercial incentive to publish one, suggests either that clean attributable wins are rare or that they come from off-site activity that is hard to credit to a tool.
- Time-to-effect data do not exist in any form I could find; the only timing signal is that monitoring tools report 40–60% month-over-month swings, which would swamp most small interventions.

### Gaps
- No named small/DTC brand with before/after AI-visibility data tied to a specific intervention (on-page, PR, Reddit, feed). Vendor case-study pages (Profound, Peec, Scrunch, Goodie, Athena) were not surfaced by search and were not fetched directly.
- Shopify has published no merchant-level breakdown of which merchants get ChatGPT traffic.
- No experimental evidence on digital PR / earned media lift, Wikipedia-article creation lift, or YouTube/Quora seeding lift.

## Key Question 3: How much does direct feed/merchant integration matter, and do platforms accept payment for prominence?

### Takeaway
Feeds and merchant programs mainly affect *eligibility and data quality* (accurate price, availability, card rendering), not whether a product makes the shortlist: in ChatGPT, 87% of product cards still come from web crawl and the candidate set is heavily derived from Google Shopping results. OpenAI, Perplexity and Google all state that organic product results are not influenced by payment; ChatGPT (since Feb 2026) and Amazon Rufus (2025–26) now carry clearly labelled ads as separate placements. Instant Checkout was retired by OpenAI in March 2026 after very low merchant adoption, so checkout integration is no longer a lever in ChatGPT.

### Cited Findings
- **[PLATFORM, secondary] OpenAI's launch statement (Sept 2025):** product results are "organic and unsponsored, ranked purely on relevance"; Instant Checkout items are "not preferred in product results"; when several merchants sell the same product, ordering factors are availability, price, quality, whether the merchant is the primary seller, and whether Instant Checkout is enabled — summarized from [OpenAI "Buy it in ChatGPT"](https://openai.com/index/buy-it-in-chatgpt/) via search results (openai.com returned 403 on fetch); echoed in [Acadia guide](https://acadia.io/chatgpt-shopping-instant-checkout-guide).
- **[PLATFORM, secondary] Instant Checkout retired March 6, 2026.** OpenAI said the initial version "did not offer the level of flexibility that we aspire to provide"; reporting cites low adoption (roughly 12 of Shopify's merchants ever went live) and data-sync difficulty; purchases now complete on merchant sites, with ACP as the discovery/handoff layer; merchants get in via Shopify Catalog or by applying at chatgpt.com/merchants (approved partners) — [The Keyword](https://www.thekeyword.co/news/openai-chatgpt-instant-checkout-scrapped), [Agentic Commerce Feed](https://www.agenticcommercefeed.com/blog/2026-03-16-openai-kills-instant-checkout), [Exploding Topics](https://explodingtopics.com/blog/agentic-commerce-protocol)
- **[PLATFORM] OpenAI commerce docs** describe feeds as helping ChatGPT "accurately index and display your products with up-to-date price and availability," with "separate requirements for Ads and checkout"; the index page contains no ranking statement — [OpenAI developer docs](https://developers.openai.com/commerce)
- **[VENDOR] How ChatGPT actually sources products (Profound, June 2026, 812k cards):** 87.3% of product cards come from web crawl, 12.7% from direct merchant feeds; web-crawl cards draw on ChatGPT's fallback product search, Google's commercial indices, and merchant pages (not Bing); direct merchant sources supply live info just under 25% of the time; product fan-out queries drive card selection while regular fan-outs shape the narrative text — [Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/chatgpt-shopping-end-to-end-breakdown)
- **[MEASURED, small N] Google Shopping dependency:** Semrush decoded ChatGPT's hidden shopping fan-out queries (100 prompts × 5 runs) and found the top ChatGPT product appeared in Google Shopping's first three results 75% of the time — [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/chatgpt-searches-google-shopping/). A separate Search Engine Land analysis reported 83% of carousel products traced to Google Shopping via fan-outs — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/new-finding-chatgpt-sources-83-of-its-carousel-products-from-google-shopping-via-shopping-query-fan-outs-470723) (fetch 403; headline from search result).
- **[PLATFORM/VENDOR] ChatGPT ads timeline:** ads shown to US Free/Go users from Feb 9, 2026; self-serve Ads Manager May 5, 2026; product-feed shopping ads from ~May 12, 2026; from ~Sept 4, 2026 shopping ad placements reportedly exclusive to brands with connected feeds; shopping ads appear as a labelled "Sponsored" card below the response; reported $1B annualized run rate in <200 days — [Common Thread Collective tracker](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/every-chatgpt-ads-update-in-2026-updated-weekly) and [CTC Sept 2026](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/chatgpt-ads-september-2026-product-feeds-now-required-platform-expands-globally-and-the-new-tools-ecommerce-brands-need-right-now) (agency-sourced; OpenAI primary pages not fetched).
- **[PLATFORM, secondary] Perplexity Merchant Program:** free, no commissions; Perplexity's own wording promises "increased chances of being a 'recommended product'" because products are indexed with "more robust details"; product cards are said to be ranked by relevance not ad spend, using the same authority/relevance criteria as regular answers — [1Digital Agency](https://www.1digitalagency.com/perplexity-shopping-optimization/), [Hypotenuse](https://www.hypotenuse.ai/blog/perplexity-shop-like-a-pro-what-it-is-and-ways-to-gain-visibility) (agency summaries of Perplexity help pages; primary not fetched).
- **[MEASURED, secondary] Amazon Rufus:** Amalytix (Nov 2024, >1,300 Rufus-recommended products) found Rufus essentially never recommends products below 4.0 stars, median 4.5; an April 2026 Workflow Labs analysis claims structured backend data is the primary lever — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/rufus-shows-5-products-not-50-what-brands-must-know-about-amazons-ai-filter/). Sponsored Products/Brands now appear inside Rufus conversations; sources conflict on whether placements are paid CPC (GA early 2026) or still free beta — [Intentwise](https://www.intentwise.com/blog/explained-advertising-on-amazons-rufus), [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/artificial-intelligence/amazons-ai-personal-shopper-is-sharing-ads-with-its-advice). Amazon said Rufus users are 60% more likely to complete a purchase (Q3 2025 call, via vendor summaries).
- **[PLATFORM] Google:** "There are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary"; "There's also no special schema.org structured data that you need to add"; AI text files/AI-specific markup are unnecessary; AI features use query fan-out — [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)

### Inferences
- For a brand with no third-party mentions, a feed gets products *rendered correctly* and makes them eligible for ChatGPT's fallback product search and for ads, but the organic shortlist is still assembled from web/Google Shopping signals where review count and retailer presence dominate. Feed participation therefore does not substitute for third-party presence; it prevents a brand that has earned a mention from being dropped for bad data.
- The one place integration plausibly changed the calculus (Instant Checkout as a merchant tie-breaker) no longer exists in ChatGPT. Paid prominence now exists in ChatGPT and Rufus but as labelled ad units, which is a budget question rather than an organic-visibility question.
- Because ChatGPT leans on Google Shopping, Google Merchant Center listing quality is an indirect ChatGPT lever, which is a measured and actionable finding for small merchants.

### Gaps
- No data on which merchants were surfaced during Instant Checkout's life, beyond the "~12 Shopify merchants live" figure.
- No published evidence either way on whether ad spend in ChatGPT or Rufus leaks into organic recommendations.
- Perplexity's and OpenAI's help-center ranking language was only accessible second-hand.

## Key Question 4: Do AI assistants cite a brand's own site as the primary source for a category-level recommendation?

### Takeaway
Across multiple independent and vendor datasets, brand-owned domains are near-absent as sources for category-level product recommendations; the shortlist is sourced from Reddit, YouTube, independent review sites (RTINGS, PCMag, CNET), Forbes, big-box retailers and Google Shopping, and brand pages supply product details afterward. Semrush's data additionally shows that even when a brand's page is cited, 62% of the time the brand is not named in the answer.

### Cited Findings
- **[MEASURED, vendor-run] Cloro.dev six-engine study (3,312 product-intent responses; electronics, wearables, home, kitchen, audio, outdoor; May 2026, updated Sept 2026):** top cited sources were YouTube 19%, Reddit 19%, RTINGS 16%, google.com 12%, Forbes 9%, PCMag 8%, CNET 8%, Tom's Guide 6%, TechRadar 6%, Amazon 5%; the authors write "Brand-owned domains are conspicuously absent from the top of this list." Retailer citations: Best Buy 12%, Walmart 11%, Target 5%, Amazon 4%. Recommendations were highly fragmented (606 merchants, HHI 0.046; 3,481 products, HHI 0.0005; top product Garmin Forerunner 265 in only ~2% of responses) — [Cloro.dev](https://cloro.dev/blog/ai-shopping-chatgpt-recommends/)
- **[VENDOR] Profound (812k ChatGPT product cards):** Reddit is the single most-indexed domain, ~one-third of all shopping citations; inclusion is associated with alignment to product fan-out queries, strong placement in ChatGPT's fallback search and Google Shopping, optimized PDPs/feeds, and "overall citation visibility" — [Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/chatgpt-shopping-end-to-end-breakdown)
- **[VENDOR] Beauty category:** Reddit was the most-cited source "by a substantial margin," followed by Who What Wear, Wikipedia, Sephora and Allure — [GCI Magazine](https://www.gcimagazine.com/retail/digital-e-commerce/news/22968759/the-top-sites-chatgpt-is-using-for-beauty-recommendations)
- **[VENDOR] General ChatGPT citation share (Profound, 680M citations, mid-2025):** Wikipedia 7.8%, Reddit 1.8%; Reddit's share then swung from ~7% to ~1% in Sept 2025 and back to ~3% — [BuzzStream summary](https://www.buzzstream.com/blog/top-sites-chatgpt/), [Profound on Reddit](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/the-data-on-reddit-and-ai-search); Semrush's three-month study still had Reddit and Wikipedia as ChatGPT's two most-cited domains — [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/most-cited-domains-ai/)
- **[MEASURED] Ghost citations:** 61.7% of 3,981 cited domains were not named in the answer text; only 13.2% both cited and mentioned — [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/the-ghost-citations-study/)
- **[MEASURED] Semrush/Indig topic-ownership study (1,094 categories, Jan–Jun 2026, 50k+ brands, 600k+ citations):** only 21% of the most-cited domains in a category were also the most-mentioned brand, with a slight negative correlation (−0.229) between being the top cited domain and being the top mentioned brand — [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/chatgpt-topic-authority-study/)
- **[MEASURED, sponsored] Lightspeed/Vaer AI (~460,000 responses, 10 categories):** small retailers were *cited* about as often as chains, but large chains received ~2.5x as many leading recommendations; on specific queries small stores got about 1 in 10 first-place recommendations — [Fit Small Business summary](https://fitsmallbusiness.com/smb-news/ai-shopping-recommendations-small-retailers/) (Lightspeed primary page 404; study is Lightspeed-sponsored).

### Inferences
- The data support a two-stage model: third-party corpora (Reddit, review sites, retailers, Google Shopping) assemble the shortlist; brand pages and feeds then supply attributes. A brand site being "cited" is therefore often a details-stage event, not a recommendation-stage event, which is consistent with the ghost-citation and citation-vs-mention divergence findings.
- The near-flat product HHI in the Cloro data (no product above ~2%) suggests that in mass-market electronics the shortlist is wide and churning, so the barrier is not one incumbent but the requirement to appear in the third-party corpora at all.

### Gaps
- No study separates "brand site cited for category recommendation" from "brand site cited for product details" explicitly; the inference above is drawn from citation mix and ghost-citation data.
- No category-by-category breakdown of brand-site citation share was found.

## Key Question 5: Do practitioners report that off-site mentions outperform on-site optimization, and with what data?

### Takeaway
Yes, nearly unanimously, but the supporting data are correlational (Ahrefs 0.664 for branded web mentions vs 0.218 for backlinks; Seer 0.65 for Google keyword footprint vs 0.10 for backlinks) and the most-quoted figure, Indig's "85% off-site," has no published methodology. Rand Fishkin's SparkToro study adds that recommendations are so inconsistent run-to-run that only visibility averaged over 60–100+ runs is meaningful, and that corpus prominence and candidate-pool size, not website quality, drive who appears.

### Cited Findings
- **[MEASURED] Ahrefs (75,000 brands, DR>40, Spearman):** branded web mentions 0.664, branded anchors 0.527, branded search volume 0.392, DR 0.326, referring domains 0.295, branded traffic 0.274, backlinks 0.218, ad traffic 0.216, URL rating 0.18, number of pages 0.17. Authors state correlation is not causation, all correlations are "moderate to very weak," and spam manipulation of AI Overviews has been demonstrated — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/)
- **[MEASURED, cited by Ahrefs] Seer Interactive (ChatGPT mentions):** Google keywords 0.65, Google organic keywords 0.65, Bing keywords 0.50, SERP features 0.41, DR 0.25, backlinks 0.10 — via [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/)
- **[MEASURED, cited by Ahrefs] Kevin Indig:** correlation of 0.334 between AI chatbot mentions and brand search volume — via [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/)
- **[ASSERTED] Kevin Indig:** "85% of your brand mentions are influenced by stuff that's not on your website"; recommends shifting from evergreen blog content to original research, interactive assets and off-site reputation; no study or N given — [Previsible interview](https://previsible.com/seo-ai-news/ai-visibility-has-nothing-to-do-with-your-website/)
- **[MEASURED] Semrush/Indig topic study:** traditional metrics are weak predictors of ChatGPT category ownership: owners had higher branded search volume in 55.7% of pairs (the only statistically significant edge), higher organic traffic in 48.4%, higher Authority Score in 52.5%; only 15.2% of categories had a clear owner, 53.7% were unsettled; high-demand topics were *more* open (11.3% owned vs 19% in low-demand half) — [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/chatgpt-topic-authority-study/)
- **[MEASURED] Rand Fishkin / SparkToro + Gumshoe (600 volunteers, 12 prompts, 2,961 responses across ChatGPT, Claude, Google AI, Nov–Dec 2025):** <1 in 100 chance that two runs of the same prompt list the same brands (ChatGPT, Google AI); same order <1 in 1,000; visibility tracks "how many entities frequently appear in documents in the AI corpora" and candidate-pool size; tight markets (LA Volvo dealers, cloud providers) are consistent, broad ones (sci-fi novels, brand agencies at 30–40% max visibility) are not; headphones: Bose, Sony, Sennheiser, Apple appeared in 55–77% of 994 responses; Fishkin calls tools reporting precise rank positions "full of baloney" — [SparkToro](https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-ais-are-highly-inconsistent-when-recommending-brands-or-products-marketers-should-take-care-when-tracking-ai-visibility/)
- **[ASSERTED] Dan Petrovic (Dejan):** measures brand visibility as mention frequency × position, in model-memory vs grounded modes, noting grounding changes answers substantially; proposes (does not report) a test of whether schema-only information reaches the model, predicting it arrives "as plain text" not as schema — [Marketing Speak interview](https://www.marketingspeak.com/what-ai-really-thinks-of-your-brand-with-dan-petrovic/)
- **[ASSERTED] Mike King / Andrea Volpini:** webinar material on AI Mode query fan-out and structured data; no published experimental data surfaced — [iPullRank recap](https://ipullrank.com/ai-mode-webinar-recap), [iPullRank AI Mode](https://ipullrank.com/how-ai-mode-works)
- **[PLATFORM] Reddit on seeding:** "There is no guarantee that what is on Reddit will make its way into LLMs, and we question the legitimacy of any provider suggesting they can" — [AdExchanger quoting Reddit](https://www.adexchanger.com/daily-news-roundup/thursday-04062026/)

### Inferences
- Every quantitative study points the same direction (off-site footprint > on-site signals > backlinks), but none is causal, and the Semrush study shows even branded search volume is only a modest predictor. The honest summary is: being talked about where models look is strongly associated with being recommended; nobody has shown that *buying* or *manufacturing* that talk produces the effect.
- Fishkin's variance finding implies most small-brand "we went from 0 to 3 mentions" anecdotes are within noise unless measured across hundreds of runs.

### Gaps
- No data from Aleyda Solis, Lily Ray or Bernard Huang/Clearscope surfaced in this pass; their views are presumably in the same direction but I have no citable study from them.
- No controlled study of digital PR (e.g., placing N articles and measuring mention lift) exists in the public record that I could find.

## Key Question 6: What are the failure modes and enforcement risks?

### Takeaway
Three enforcement regimes now bite: the FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (final Aug 14, 2024; prohibits fake/AI-generated reviews, bought sentiment, undisclosed insider reviews, company-controlled "independent" review sites, review suppression, and fake engagement, with civil penalties; warning letters issued Dec 2025); Google's May 15, 2026 clarification that all spam policies, including cloaking for AI crawlers, scaled AI content and inauthentic brand mentions, apply to AI Overviews/AI Mode; and Reddit's LLM-based detection and removal of coordinated inauthentic accounts amid documented AEO-driven astroturfing. Prompt injection on product pages demonstrably works against agents (41–68% success in a benchmark) but modern re-rankers flag manipulative copy 99–100% of the time, and the tactic is a policy violation everywhere.

### Cited Findings
- **[PLATFORM] FTC final rule (announced Aug 14, 2024; 16 CFR Part 465; effective 60 days after Federal Register publication):** bans creating/buying/selling reviews from nonexistent people or without real experience "including AI-generated ones" where the business knew or should have known; compensation tied to review sentiment; undisclosed officer/manager reviews; presenting company-controlled review sites as independent; suppression via baseless legal threats or intimidation and claiming suppressed review sets are representative; buying/selling bot or hijacked followers/views. Khan: "Fake reviews not only waste people's time and money, but also pollute the marketplace." Rule enables civil penalties against knowing violators — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials), [Final rule PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/r311003consumerreviewstestimonialsfinalrulefrn.pdf). Secondary sources cite an inflation-adjusted penalty around $53,088 per violation and an October 2024 effective date — [Fourscore Law](https://www.fourscorelaw.com/resources/ftc-bans-fake-reviews-and-testimonials), [Audit Socials](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/ftc-consumer-review-rule-2026-fake-reviews-testimonials-ai-social-proof)
- **[PLATFORM, secondary] FTC enforcement posture:** Dec 2025 warning letters to ten companies under the rule — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2025/12/ftc-warning-letters-ai-consumer-reviews); the 2024 Rytr order (AI review-writing tool) was set aside Dec 2025 as unduly burdening AI innovation, read by counsel as a retreat from pursuing tool vendors, not from pursuing businesses that publish fake reviews — [Audit Socials](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/ftc-rytr-order-set-aside-2025-ai-review-tool-enforcement-shift-advertiser-synthetic-content)
- **[PLATFORM] Google spam policies, May 15, 2026 changelog:** updated "to make it clear that the spam policies apply to all of Google Search, including generative AI responses"; covered policies include scaled content abuse (explicitly including generative-AI content), cloaking (separate pages for AI crawlers count), hidden text, doorway abuse, scraping, link spam, site-reputation abuse; the policy adds that seeking inauthentic brand mentions "isn't as helpful as it might seem" — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-spam-policies-now-officially-cover-ai-overviews-and-ai-mode-in-search)
- **[MEASURED, journalistic] Reddit astroturfing for AEO:** 404 Media (June 2026) reported r/Biohackers moderators imposed a moratorium on peptide/HRT posts, writing that "companies are using us for" AEO; Reddit responded that its Public Content Policy restricts inauthentic content, it blocks unknown scrapers, is improving detection, and will ask suspicious automated accounts to verify they are human — [AdExchanger roundup](https://www.adexchanger.com/daily-news-roundup/thursday-04062026/), [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/companies-are-using-reddit-to-manipulate-chatgpt-and-google-ai-search/) (partially paywalled)
- **[MEASURED, journalistic] Caught agencies:** Trap Plan admitted (Nov 2025) to using multiple fake Reddit accounts to promote War Robots: Frontiers; its client My.Games disavowed the Reddit work — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/trap-plan-marketing-astroturf-warrobots-fake-reddit-accounts-posts-2000642503), [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Marketing-company-Trap-Plan-gets-caught-boasting-about-astroturfing-Reddit-with-100-fake-player-posts-for-War-Robots-Frontiers.1160577.0.html). GEO agencies report seeded posts being removed and account suspensions rising — [PlanIt](https://www.planit.co.uk/post/reddit-s-ai-spam-crackdown-is-a-warning-for-brands-chasing-geo), [TechWyse](https://www.techwyse.com/news/platform-updates/reddit-ai-spam-brand-safety-risk). An unverified claim that a Reddit update removed ~70% of automated posting accounts appears in [SocialDay](https://socialday.live/features/reddits-ai-crackdown-forces-brands-to-abandon-bulk-marketing-accounts).
- **[MEASURED] Prompt injection works on agents:** StakeBench (NTU/ST Engineering/IBM/UIUC): 3,168 adversarial runs, 264 cases, two browser agents; indirect prompt injection hidden in product reviews/metadata succeeded 41.67%–68.16% of the time; swapping GPT-5 for Gemini 2.5 Flash raised success by 26.49 points on one agent; in a preliminary multimodal test, changing only a product image raised a manipulated product's selection rate from 10% to 76.67% — [CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4184455/prompt-injection-breaks-todays-ai-agents-study-warns.html), [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/370972/ai-agents-prompt-injection-attacks-research). Unit 42 reported the first in-the-wild web-based indirect prompt injection, targeting an AI ad-review system — [Palo Alto Unit 42](https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/ai-agent-prompt-injection/). IBM demoed an agent overpaying for a book ($55) after hidden seller text — [StartupHub](https://www.startuphub.ai/ai-news/ai-video/2026/hidden-prompt-injection-why-ai-agents-can-be-tricked-into-overpaying-for-books/) (demo, not a study).
- **[MEASURED] But modern re-rankers punish manipulative copy:** in E-GEO, "Superlatives"/"Emotional" rewrites were flagged 99–100% under a simple defense and lost rank on GPT-5 (−4.14/−3.79); "Advertisement"-style copy −1.82; red-team optimization only succeeded by drifting to factual prose — [E-GEO](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.20867v2)
- **[MEASURED] Keyword stuffing:** −8.7% to −9.1% visibility in the Princeton experiments — [Aggarwal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735)
- **[MEASURED] Fabricated statistics risk:** per the C-SEO Bench review, adding statistics lowered rank in 19 of 24 settings on realistic pipelines — [Ottmann summary](https://marioottmann.com/articles/what-geo-research-actually-shows) (secondary), undermining the "add stats" tactic even before considering legal exposure.

### Inferences
- The incentive asymmetry is now stark: the measured upside of grey tactics (seeded Reddit, fake reviews, hidden text) is unproven and decaying as models and platforms harden, while the downside is codified (FTC civil penalties, Google demotion/removal, Reddit account purges, public exposure as with Trap Plan).
- Hidden prompt-injection text is a security exploit, not a marketing tactic: it works against today's browsing agents but is detectable, is explicitly cloaking/hidden text under Google policy, and is the kind of practice OpenAI/Perplexity defenses are being tuned against.

### Gaps
- No OpenAI or Perplexity policy page on manipulative/cloaked content for their crawlers was retrieved (help.openai.com returned 403); I found no documented OpenAI/Perplexity penalty case against a brand.
- No Reddit primary-source enforcement figures.
- No FTC enforcement action specifically targeting AI-assistant review manipulation (as opposed to traditional fake reviews) was found.

## Key Question 7: Is the wall surmountable in some categories (niche/long-tail) but not others (mass-market electronics)?

### Takeaway
Evidence is consistent with "yes, partially": in categories with no authoritative review coverage, the candidate pool is small and whoever is present in the corpus wins consistently (SparkToro's tight-market consistency; arXiv "competitive vacuums" in niche e-commerce segments; Semrush finding that 54% of categories have no settled owner). In mass-market electronics the shortlist is sourced from RTINGS/PCMag/CNET/Reddit/YouTube and big-box retailers, brand sites are absent, and even the top product appears in only ~2% of answers, so entering requires third-party coverage, not better owned content.

### Cited Findings
- **[MEASURED] Category "ownership" is rare and high-demand categories are more open:** 15.2% of 1,094 ChatGPT categories had a clear owner, 31.2% an emerging leader, 53.7% unsettled; only 11.3% of top-half-demand topics were owned vs 19% of bottom-half (the top half carries 98% of AI search volume); clear owners held first place in 90.4% of month-over-month comparisons — [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/chatgpt-topic-authority-study/)
- **[MEASURED] Tight markets are consistent, broad ones are not:** SparkToro found LA Volvo dealerships and cloud providers highly consistent across runs, sci-fi novels and brand agencies highly inconsistent; headphones converged on four incumbents at 55–77% visibility — [SparkToro](https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-ais-are-highly-inconsistent-when-recommending-brands-or-products-marketers-should-take-care-when-tracking-ai-visibility/)
- **[MEASURED, preprint] Competitive vacuums in niche e-commerce:** across 50 brands, 250 category queries, 3 models, 3,750 responses (Feb 2026), mean Gini 0.28 (moderate concentration); e-commerce most concentrated (Gini 0.53, Amazon-driven); the three models agreed on the top brand in only 41.6% of queries; 8.0% of queries were competitive vacuums, concentrated in narrow segments such as "Electronics in Nordics" (CVI 0.73), luxury fashion online (0.87); lesser-known brands (Temu, ASOS, Vinted, Teladoc) were absent from 33–38 of 50 queries each but hit COI 1.00 on specific queries; one author is affiliated with an AI brand-intelligence vendor — [arXiv 2606.23057](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23057v1)
- **[MEASURED, vendor-run] Mass-market electronics sourcing:** RTINGS 16%, PCMag 8%, CNET 8%, Tom's Guide 6%, TechRadar 6%, plus YouTube/Reddit 19% each; brand domains absent; product HHI 0.0005 — [Cloro.dev](https://cloro.dev/blog/ai-shopping-chatgpt-recommends/)
- **[VENDOR] Cross-engine agreement by sector:** BrightEdge reports 88–97% brand agreement across five engines in retail, travel and tech vs 60–71% in healthcare and finance — [BrightEdge](https://www.brightedge.com/resources/weekly-ai-search-insights/where-ai-engines-agree-on-brands)
- **[MEASURED, sponsored] Small vs large retailers:** cited equally often, but chains get ~2.5x the leading recommendations and small stores get ~1 in 10 first-place slots on specific queries — [Fit Small Business on Lightspeed/Vaer AI](https://fitsmallbusiness.com/smb-news/ai-shopping-recommendations-small-retailers/)
- **[MEASURED] Within the retrieved set, underdogs gain most from content quality:** rank-5 sources +98–115% from citations/quotes/stats vs −21 to −30% for rank-1 — [Aggarwal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735)
- **[PLATFORM] Amazon Rufus filter:** essentially nothing below 4.0 stars is recommended (median 4.5) — [PPC Land/Amalytix](https://ppc.land/rufus-shows-5-products-not-50-what-brands-must-know-about-amazons-ai-filter/)

### Inferences
- The wall is better described as a *corpus-presence requirement* than an editorial-pick requirement. In niche categories the corpus is thin, so a brand's own structured content, a handful of forum threads, a YouTube review and a Google Shopping listing can constitute most of what the model sees, and the arXiv vacuum data show single brands dominating such segments. In mass-market electronics the corpus is deep and dominated by RTINGS/Wirecutter-class sources and retailer review volumes; owned content cannot displace that, and the only measured levers are review count, retailer presence and Google Shopping placement.
- The practical corollary for an unmentioned brand: (1) pick or define a sub-category where the candidate pool is small; (2) ensure Google Merchant Center/Shopify feed hygiene so retrieval does not fail on data; (3) earn genuine reviews (volume and ≥4.0 rating are the clearest measured correlates); (4) earn authentic third-party and UGC coverage, which is the only lever every correlational study agrees on; (5) treat on-page GEO as hygiene, not as the breakthrough.

### Gaps
- No study directly tests an unknown, zero-review brand across niche vs mass-market categories; the niche/mass contrast above is inferred from separate datasets with different methods.
- No evidence on the timeline for a new brand to become "corpus-present" (how many mentions, over what period, before models recommend it).
- Wirecutter's specific share of AI shopping citations did not appear in any dataset retrieved; RTINGS was the dominant named editorial source in the electronics data.
