# How LLM assistants and AI shopping agents select, rank and recommend products (mechanics, 2024–2026)

Scope note: these notes cover the "decision pipeline" inside ChatGPT shopping / Instant Checkout, Perplexity Shopping, Google AI Mode / Gemini agentic checkout, Amazon Rufus / Buy for Me, Microsoft Copilot Checkout, and (thinly) Claude with web search. Evidence is labelled as **[vendor claim]**, **[measured study]** (with method), or **[secondary/unverified]**. Several primary pages (OpenAI help article, OpenAI "Buy it in ChatGPT" post, Perplexity shopping blog) returned 403/404 to the fetch tool; where that happened I relied on search-engine extracts of the same page or on dated press coverage, and I say so.

## Key Question 1 — What do OpenAI, Perplexity, Google, Amazon and Microsoft officially say about how product recommendations and merchant feeds feed into results? Is there paid placement or stated neutrality?

### Takeaway
All five vendors describe a two-layer system: an LLM interprets the query and synthesizes an answer, while a structured product layer (merchant feeds, Shopify catalog, Google Shopping Graph, Amazon catalog, Bing/third-party providers) supplies candidate products, prices and merchants. OpenAI and Perplexity explicitly state organic product results are not ads and cannot be bought; Google and Amazon do run ads adjacent to or inside AI shopping surfaces; Microsoft's documentation is thin. No vendor publishes an actual ranking function — every official statement is a list of "factors considered" (relevance, price, availability, reviews, quality, primary-seller status, user memory) without weights, and the feed specs explicitly say eligibility "does not guarantee display."

### Cited Findings

**OpenAI / ChatGPT shopping**
- OpenAI's help article "Shopping in ChatGPT" states product results "are selected by ChatGPT independently and are not ads" and are not influenced by OpenAI partnerships; ads are handled separately. Selection weighs the query plus personal context (Memory, custom instructions); example factors include available options, price, reviews, ease of use; a stated dislike (e.g., "I dislike clowns") can filter products out. **[vendor claim; obtained via search extract because the page returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool]** — [OpenAI Help: Shopping in ChatGPT](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11128490-shopping-in-chatgpt)

- At the April 28, 2025 launch of improved shopping results, OpenAI said the system weighs structured metadata from third-party providers (price, description) and third-party content (reviews), plus "ChatGPT's earlier model responses, before new search results" and OpenAI safety standards; a stated budget shifts weighting toward price; "not all available products will appear"; the initial displayed price "typically comes from the first merchant listed, which may not be the lowest available price"; merchant display order is "predominantly determined by these providers," not ranked by price or shipping policy; Bing (Microsoft) is named as a current third-party search provider. **[vendor claim, as reported]** — [PPC Land, May 1 2025](https://ppc.land/openai-introduces-shopping-to-chatgpt-with-1-billion-weekly-searches/)

Review signals are aggregate only: `review_count` and `star_rating` (0–5); "Raw review entries and question-and-answer lists are not part of this discovery contract." No refresh cadence is mandated; merchants are told to update when price/availability change. Formats: OpenAI JSONL/CSV/TSV, or Google-compatible TSV/CSV with OpenAI confirmation (XML/RSS/Atom not supported). The spec makes no statement about how feed data affects ranking. **[vendor doc]** — [OpenAI Product Feed Spec](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/specs/feed)

### Inferences
- Every official description places the LLM downstream of a structured product index: candidate products come from feeds/catalogs/Shopping Graph; the model's job is interpretation, filtering against user context, and synthesis. This means "ranking" in the user-visible answer is a composite of (a) provider-supplied candidate order (OpenAI says merchant order is "predominantly determined by these providers") and (b) the model's own selection — and vendors do not disclose the split.
- "Eligibility does not guarantee display" (OpenAI spec) plus "not all available products will appear" is the vendors' own acknowledgement that a non-deterministic model layer decides the final consideration set.
- Stated neutrality (OpenAI, Perplexity) covers organic product cards; OpenAI's feed spec nonetheless includes an `is_ads_eligible` flag and Google already shows ads in AI Overviews and is piloting Direct Offers in AI Mode, so the "neutral organic + separate ads" architecture is converging across vendors.
- OpenAI's statement that selection considers "earlier model responses, before new search results" is the clearest official admission that parametric knowledge is an explicit input alongside retrieval.

### Gaps
- No vendor publishes ranking weights, the number of candidates considered, or how often products are chosen from memory vs. feed. OpenAI's help page and "Buy it in ChatGPT" post could not be fetched directly (403); quotes rely on search extracts and PPC Land.
- Perplexity's original shopping blog is gone (404); its current merchant-program terms and whether sponsored products are labelled inside shopping results are undocumented.
- Google does not state whether AI Mode/Gemini product listings are organic-only or whether Shopping ads/Direct Offers can appear in them.
- Microsoft has no public documentation found on how Copilot picks products or whether placement is paid.
- Claude web search: Anthropic's developer docs redirected to platform.claude.com and were not retrieved; no official description of source selection for shopping-type queries was captured.

## Key Question 2 — What do independent studies say about the signals that drive inclusion (brand frequency in training data, retrieved page content, structured data, review aggregators, domain authority, recency)?

### Takeaway
Measured studies converge on three findings: (1) at the page level, content features that make a passage quotable (citations, statistics, quotations, fluency) raise visibility 20–40% in controlled GEO experiments while keyword stuffing does nothing; (2) at the brand level, off-site brand mentions and branded search volume correlate far more strongly with AI visibility than backlinks, domain rating or content volume, and the correlations are strongest for Google AI Overviews and weakest for ChatGPT; (3) the sources that get cited are heavily platform-specific (Wikipedia-heavy ChatGPT, Reddit-heavy Perplexity, YouTube/LinkedIn-heavy Google), volatile month to month, and dominated by pages brands cannot easily influence.

### Cited Findings

**Princeton GEO paper (Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024; v1 Nov 2023 — historical baseline)**
- GEO-bench: 10K queries from nine sources (8K/1K/1K split), each paired with the cleaned text of the top-5 Google results; generative engine simulated with GPT-3.5-turbo at temperature 0.7, responses sampled five times. Nine methods tested: Authoritative, Statistics Addition, Keyword Stuffing, Cite Sources, Quotation Addition, Easy-to-Understand, Fluency Optimization, Unique Words, Technical Terms. **[measured study]** — [arXiv 2311.09735 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09735v3)
- Best methods improved visibility up to 41% (Position-Adjusted Word Count) and 28% (Subjective Impression); Cite Sources, Quotation Addition and Statistics Addition reached ~30–40% / 15–30%; Fluency and Easy-to-Understand ~15–30%; Authoritative tone no significant gain; Keyword Stuffing scored below baseline on PAWC. — [arXiv 2311.09735 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09735v3)
- Rank-dependence (Table 2): Cite Sources changed rank-1 sources by −30.3% but rank-5 sources by +115.1%; Quotation Addition −22.9% / +99.7%; Statistics Addition −20.6% / +97.9% — i.e., optimization helps lower-ranked sources most. — [arXiv 2311.09735 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09735v3)
- On live Perplexity.ai (200-sample subset): Quotation Addition +~22% PAWC; Statistics Addition up to +37% Subjective Impression; Keyword Stuffing ~10% worse than baseline. Efficacy varied by domain (e.g., Cite Sources best for Facts/Law & Government; Statistics for Debate/Opinion). — [arXiv 2311.09735 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735)

**Brand-level correlation studies (vendor research with disclosed method)**
- Ahrefs (May 26, 2025; 75,000 brands with DR>40 and ≥800 monthly searches on top keyword; Spearman; AI Overviews only; ~26% of brands had zero mentions): branded web mentions 0.664, branded anchors 0.527, branded search volume 0.392, DR 0.326, referring domains 0.295, branded traffic 0.274, backlinks 0.218, ad traffic 0.216, ad cost 0.215, URL rating 0.18, site pages 0.17. Author stresses correlation ≠ causation. **[measured, vendor]** — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/)
- Ahrefs follow-up across ChatGPT, AI Mode and AI Overviews: YouTube mentions show the highest correlation (~0.74); branded search volume lowest (~0.35–0.47; ChatGPT 0.352, AI Mode 0.466); link metrics correlate only weakly; content volume shows almost no relationship. Ahrefs separately notes correlations with classic authority metrics are strongest for Google AIO and "very weak" for ChatGPT. **[measured, vendor; figures from search extracts of the Ahrefs post]** — [Ahrefs: Top brand visibility factors](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-brand-visibility-correlations/); [Ahrefs: Google more biased to big brands](https://ahrefs.com/blog/branded-web-mentions-visibility-ai-search/)
- Kevin Indig: correlation of 0.334 between AI-chatbot mentions and brand search volume; "the popularity of a brand broadly decides how visible it is in AI Chatbots." Attribution is ambiguous — one Ahrefs post attributes the 0.334 to Indig, another presents it as Ahrefs' own. **[measured; original Indig write-up not retrieved]** — [Ahrefs citing Indig](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/)
- Semrush + Kevin Indig/Growth Memo (reported June 2026; 3,981 domain appearances across 115 prompts, 14 countries, ChatGPT/AIO/Gemini/AI Mode): comparative prompts ("best," "vs," "recommend") produced a 43.3% brand-mention rate vs 18% for informational prompts (~2.4×); informational prompts had an 89.3% citation rate (citations ≠ brand mentions); country variation from ~50% (India, Sweden) to 18–22% (Italy, Brazil, Netherlands). **[measured, vendor; figures via LSEO secondary write-up]** — [LSEO summary](https://lseo.com/mention-engine/resources/articles/why-best-vs-and-recommend-prompts-produce-more-brand-mentions/); [Semrush Ghost Citations study](https://semrush.com/blog/the-ghost-citations-study/)

**Which sources get cited (citation-share studies)**
- Semrush (230,000+ prompts, 100M+ citations, weekly snapshots July 14–Oct 12, 2025; ChatGPT search, Google AI Mode, Perplexity): on ChatGPT, Reddit appeared in ~60% of responses in early August falling to ~10% by mid-September 2025 and Wikipedia fell from ~55% to under 20%; on AI Mode LinkedIn was cited in ~15% of responses and Wikipedia ~2–3%; on Perplexity top sources were Reddit, LinkedIn, NIH, Microsoft, Google and Wikipedia ~0.8%. Semrush's analyst doubts Google's num=100 removal fully explains the ChatGPT drop and suggests ChatGPT may be deliberately avoiding over-citing certain sites. The study does not break out product queries. **[measured, vendor]** — [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/most-cited-domains-ai/)
- Profound (~680M citations, Aug 2024–Jun 2025): ChatGPT's top source Wikipedia 7.8% of all citations (47.9% of its top-10 share), Reddit 1.8%, Forbes 1.1%, G2 1.1%; Google AIO Reddit 2.2%, YouTube 1.9%, Quora 1.5%, LinkedIn 1.3%; Perplexity Reddit 6.6% (46.7% of top-10), YouTube 2.0%, Gartner 1.0%, Yelp 0.8%; .com domains = 80.41% of ChatGPT citations, .org 11.29%. No shopping-query breakdown; collection method not described. **[measured, vendor]** — [Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/ai-platform-citation-patterns)
- Ahrefs (September 2025 US data, 730K query pairs for content similarity, 540K for citations): Google AI Overviews and AI Mode responses to the same query had 86% average semantic similarity but cited the same URLs only ~13% of the time (16% for top-3); AI Mode favored Wikipedia/Quora/health sites, AIO favored YouTube. **[measured, vendor]** — [Ahrefs AIO vs AI Mode](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-vs-ai-mode); [SEJ coverage](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-ai-mode-ai-overviews-cite-different-urls-per-ahrefs-report/563364)
- Ahrefs (top 1,000 pages ChatGPT cited in September 2025): ~67% were sources hard for brands to influence (Wikipedia, homepages, app-store listings); the remaining third were educational content, reviews, news, blogs. **[secondary newsletter summary; original not retrieved]** — [Dofollow Digest](https://dofollow-digest.beehiiv.com/p/dofollow-digest-50-the-real-story-behind-chatgpt-citations)

**ChatGPT shopping internals (reverse engineering)**
- Analyst David Konitzny (as reported by PPC Land) found internal result-type labels (product, web, news), structured product queries targeting price/reviews/merchants alongside broader web searches for expert opinions and comparisons, and a second internal search triggered once specific products are identified whose sources do not appear in the UI. **[secondary; original write-up not retrieved]** — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/openai-introduces-shopping-to-chatgpt-with-1-billion-weekly-searches/)

### Inferences
- Page-level GEO effects (citations/stats/quotes) and brand-level popularity effects operate at different pipeline stages: GEO changes which retrieved passage the model quotes; brand mentions/search volume change which brands the model knows and expects to be "the answer." A brand can be well-retrieved but not named, or named without retrieval (see KQ4).
- Because citation shares swing by 5× within weeks (Semrush) and the two Google surfaces agree on meaning but not on URLs (Ahrefs), "source selection" is clearly a retrieval-and-sampling layer that is far more volatile than the brand consideration set it feeds.
- Weak correlations with backlinks/DR on ChatGPT and stronger ones on Google are consistent with Google's AI surfaces drawing on its own ranking stack while ChatGPT draws on Bing plus its own model priors.

### Gaps
- No study found that isolates structured data / schema markup as a causal driver of product inclusion in ChatGPT or Perplexity shopping carousels; vendor guides assert it without controlled tests.
- No study found quantifying recency (freshness) as a ranking factor for product answers.
- No independent study found of Amazon Rufus or Copilot ranking signals with disclosed methodology.
- The Ahrefs "15 million queries" query-fan-out study for ChatGPT appeared in results but was not retrieved — [Ahrefs query fan-out](https://ahrefs.com/blog/query-fan-out-chatgpt).

## Key Question 3 — How much do recommendations vary across runs, models, and phrasing? Measured consistency/variance data.

### Takeaway
Run-to-run variance is large and now measured by several independent efforts: identical prompts almost never return the same brand list (<1% on ChatGPT and Google AI), same list in same order <0.1%; yet the *set* of top brands is fairly stable (top brands appear in 55–77% of runs) and set recovery saturates after ~5–10 runs. Cross-model agreement is lower than within-model agreement (Jaccard 0.35 ChatGPT vs Claude, against a 0.50–0.61 same-model rerun baseline). Phrasing matters mainly through intent: comparative wording roughly doubles brand-mention rates, and the same shopper need expressed in different human words yields near-zero semantic overlap in answers.

### Cited Findings
- SparkToro/Gumshoe.ai (Rand Fishkin, Patrick O'Donnell; published Jan 2026; not peer-reviewed; Gumshoe sells AI tracking tools): hundreds of volunteers ran 12 brand-recommendation prompts (chef's knives, headphones, cancer-care hospitals, marketing consultants, sci-fi novels, etc.) across ChatGPT, Claude and Google AI (AIO, or AI Mode when no AIO) a combined 2,961 times in Nov–Dec 2025, 60–100 runs per prompt per platform, using participants' normal (unstandardized) settings. Chance of the same brand list across repeated runs: under 1 in 100 for ChatGPT and Google AI; same list in same order: under 0.1%. Claude was slightly more likely to repeat the same list but less likely to repeat the order. In 994 headphone responses, Bose, Sony, Sennheiser and Apple each appeared in 55–77% of answers. Tight categories (cloud providers) were stable; broad ones (sci-fi novels) scattered. 142 participants writing their own headphone prompts produced answers with semantic similarity of 0.081. Authors note they are not data scientists and call for larger studies. **[measured; independent but non-academic]** — [SEJ, Jan 30 2026](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-recommendations-change-with-nearly-every-query-sparktoro/566242/); original: [SparkToro](https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-ais-are-highly-inconsistent-when-recommending-brands-or-products-marketers-should-take-care-when-tracking-ai-visibility/); [WARC](https://www.warc.com/content/feed/llms-produce-random-results-when-listing-brand-recommendations/en-GB/11352)
- Secondary coverage of the same study reports average visibility for the top three brands of 64% (ChatGPT), 73% (Claude), 68% (Google AI), and the authors' conclusion that visibility rate is a more reliable metric than rank. **[secondary]** — [Communicate Online](https://communicateonline.me/news/ai-brand-recommendations-vary-marketers-warned-on-tracking-tools/)
- Żatuchin, "The Dice Roll Method" (arXiv 2609.04047, Sept 2026, v2 Oct 14 2026; single-author preprint): re-analyzes five brand-recommendation studies (~190,000 observations, 270+ brands, 6 languages, 5–40 iterations) and decomposes variance into sampling, prompt-phrasing, run-to-run and model-version components. Across 250 questions the generalizability coefficient is 0.96 at five runs (1–2 runs reach 0.80), but a single Gemini question had G = 0.27 (95% CI 0.00–0.52); simulated power to detect a large effect in one cell is 0.15–0.19 at five runs and ~0.8 at 15–25 runs; a pre-registered test reproduced the D-study prediction in 37 of 39 cells. Recommends pilot-based run counts rather than fixed tiers. **[measured; preprint, not peer reviewed]** — [arXiv 2609.04047](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04047)
- Żatuchin, "Measuring Brand and Source Discovery under Repeated LLM Queries" (arXiv 2609.05059, Sept 2026): 4,500 responses to 50 buying questions across six configurations × 15 calls; median recovery of the 15-call brand set from ten calls was 92.6–95.2% (89.5–94.7% after re-adjudication); single-call recovery of a five-call set rose from 80.0–92.5% (February wave) to 90.0–100% (September wave); annotation was by two blinded Gemini 3.1 Pro roles (micro-F1 0.908 canonical-name agreement) with no human reference. Explicitly disclaims causal retrieval effects. **[measured; preprint; AI-annotated]** — [arXiv 2609.05059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05059)
- Secondary summary of 2609.05059 states that engines without web search kept adding new brands even at run 15 while a retrieval-enabled engine's list converged faster. **[secondary; not confirmed in the abstract I retrieved]** — [papers.cool](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2609.05059)
- Jack, Lehman, Maloney & Xu, "Divergent Recommendations, Convergent Diagnoses" (arXiv 2606.26116, May 2026): 215 commercially framed prompts in four batches on ChatGPT and Claude; cross-provider recommendation Jaccard 0.35 vs a same-prompt rerun baseline of 0.50–0.61 (providers disagree on recommended brands ~two-thirds of the time). **[measured; preprint; authors' affiliations not captured]** — [arXiv 2606.26116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26116)
- Columbia/MyCustomAI/Yale ACES sandbox: for the same fitness-watch assortment Claude Sonnet 4 picked the Fitbit Inspire ~45% of the time vs ~25% for GPT-4.1 and Gemini 2.5 Flash — different models converge on different "modal" products. **[measured; academic preprint]** — [arXiv 2508.02630](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02630v2)
- Phrasing: Semrush/Indig comparative vs informational prompts 43.3% vs 18% brand-mention rate (see KQ2). BrightEdge slide deck claims "cheap," "affordable," "where," "buy," "deals" correlate with brand mentions and that "best" averages only 4.8 mentions — no published methodology. **[vendor marketing; low confidence]** — [BrightEdge deck](https://videos.brightedge.com/assets/weekly-insights/chatgpt-brand-mentions/ChatGPTComparisons.pdf); Otterly reports category prompts like "best CRM" trigger web search inconsistently. **[vendor]** — [Otterly](https://ai-cache.otterly.ai/blog/how-often-does-chatgpt-trigger-a-web-search/index.html)
- Ahrefs: Google AIO vs AI Mode for the same query — 86% semantic similarity but ~13% URL overlap (see KQ2), i.e., cross-surface variance within one vendor is high at the citation layer but low at the answer layer. — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-vs-ai-mode)

### Inferences
- Variance is layered: list order is close to random; list membership is moderately stable; the "head" of the consideration set (2–4 brands) is quite stable for tight categories. Tracking tools that report rank are measuring noise; tools that report inclusion rate over ≥5–15 runs are measuring signal (Fishkin; Żatuchin).
- Cross-model disagreement (Jaccard 0.35) exceeding within-model noise (0.50–0.61) implies model-specific priors and retrieval stacks each contribute measurably to what is recommended — a brand's visibility must be measured per engine.
- Prompt phrasing acts through two routes: intent words change whether the model enters "recommendation mode" at all (43% vs 18% mention rates), and natural variation in human wording changes which retrieved documents and which brands appear (semantic similarity 0.081).

### Gaps
- No controlled study found that varies a single price/quality word ("best" vs "cheapest") while holding everything else constant across repeated runs.
- No published data on whether API calls vs consumer UI (with memory/personalization) show the same variance — flagged as an open question by Fishkin.
- No variance data found specific to Perplexity Shopping, Copilot, Rufus or Gemini shopping surfaces.

## Key Question 4 — Is there evidence of "parametric brand memory" (well-known brands named regardless of retrieval) versus retrieval-driven answers for long-tail queries?

### Takeaway
Yes, and it is now directly measured: in controlled tests a recognized brand beat spec-identical fictional competitors 100% of the time across GPT-4o-mini, Claude Sonnet and Gemini 3 Flash; LLM rankers favor popular items in proportion to their frequency; and one cross-provider audit estimates Claude recommends "from priors" 43–52% of the time versus 8–29% for ChatGPT. Retrieval does override memory when it carries a clear differentiating signal (a ~0.1-star rating edge or authority-style evidence claims), and failures for long-tail brands are overwhelmingly "discoverability" failures (the brand never reaches the model) rather than positioning failures.

### Cited Findings
- Chu & Hou, "Incumbent Advantage" (arXiv 2606.17443, June 2026, v3 Oct 10 2026; GPT-4o-mini, Claude Sonnet, Gemini 3 Flash; skincare as a credence good, with a search-goods robustness check; three experiments): with identical specifications, well-known brands were recommended 100% of the time (Incumbent Advantage Index 10.0, the theoretical maximum; 670 valid trials per search extract, one real brand vs nine fictional). The advantage disappears when a competitor has a rating advantage just over 0.1 stars; authority-style marketing language including fabricated clinical-evidence claims breaks the incumbent's monopoly at a "Bias Surplus Value" of +0.17 rating points; when all brands use the same GEO strategy, individual payoff falls from +0.802 to +0.007 and non-participating brands received zero recommendations. **[measured; preprint]** — [arXiv 2606.17443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17443)
- Jack et al. (arXiv 2606.26116): Anthropic recommends from priors 43–52% of the time, OpenAI 8–29% (the abstract does not define how "priors" vs retrieval was measured). When neither provider recommends a brand, both diagnose the same failure mode 95.1% of the time (CI 94.3–95.7%) across 7,763 joint failures; agreement rises from 81% for category leaders to 99.6% for long-tail regional brands; failure modes are discoverability (brand never reaches the model), compellingness (reaches model, not mentioned), positioning (mentioned, not recommended). **[measured; preprint]** — [arXiv 2606.26116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26116)
- Hou et al., "LLMs are Zero-Shot Rankers for Recommender Systems" (arXiv 2305.08845, ECIR 2024 — historical baseline; gpt-3.5-turbo at temp 0.2 on MovieLens-1M and Amazon Games; GPT-4, LLaMA-2-70B, Vicuna, Alpaca on 200-user subsets): popular items tend to rank higher (Figure 3c, popularity measured by normalized item frequency); the authors hypothesize this reflects pre-training text frequency but could not measure it directly; popularity of top-ranked items falls as user-history length decreases. **[measured; peer-reviewed]** — [arXiv 2305.08845 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.08845v2)
- Kamruzzaman et al., "Global is Good, Local is Bad?" (EMNLP 2024, arXiv 2406.13997 — 2024 context): LLMs associate global brands with positive attributes and recommend luxury gifts more often for high-income countries; country-of-origin effects can boost local-brand preference in specific contexts. **[measured; peer-reviewed]** — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.707); [arXiv 2406.13997](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.13997)
- OpenAI's own April 2025 description says product selection considers "ChatGPT's earlier model responses, before new search results" alongside third-party metadata and reviews. **[vendor claim]** — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/openai-introduces-shopping-to-chatgpt-with-1-billion-weekly-searches/)
- How often retrieval happens at all (ChatGPT) — estimates conflict widely and are all from GEO vendors: Texta (100k+ prompts) ~23% overall, 78% for news, 8% for general knowledge; Nectiv (8,500+ prompts, Oct 2025) 31% with ~2.17 searches per searching prompt; Graphite (logged-out, mid-2026) 58%, warning that some tools force search on every prompt; Cloro (5,200 queries, 52 countries) 80.5%, arguing lower figures were skewed by rate-limiting. Texta claims 89% of brand citations occur when search is triggered (method unverified). **[vendor measurements; no consensus]** — [Texta](https://www.texta.ai/blog/how-often-does-chatgpt-trigger-web-search-complete-analysis); [Graphite](https://graphite.io/five-percent/research/do-not-force-search-prompt-tracking); [Cloro](https://cloro.dev/blog/chatgpt_grounding_frequency_study/); [Otterly](https://ai-cache.otterly.ai/blog/how-often-does-chatgpt-trigger-a-web-search/index.html)
- Brand-popularity correlates (branded search volume 0.33–0.47; web mentions 0.66 on AIO) are strongest predictors of visibility while on-site metrics are weak — consistent with memory/popularity rather than page authority driving brand naming (see KQ2). — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/)
- Secondary summary of Żatuchin 2609.05059: no-web-search configurations kept adding new brands at run 15 while retrieval-enabled runs converged faster. **[secondary/unconfirmed]** — [papers.cool](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2609.05059)

### Inferences
- The pipeline behaves as "memory proposes, retrieval disposes": parametric familiarity sets the default consideration set (100% incumbent win on ties), and retrieved evidence only displaces an incumbent when it carries a legible delta (ratings, evidence claims). For long-tail products the binding constraint is getting into the retrieval set at all (discoverability), not persuasion.
- Provider differences in prior-reliance (Claude 43–52% vs ChatGPT 8–29%, one study) plus wide disagreement about how often ChatGPT searches (23%–80%) mean the memory/retrieval mix is engine-, prompt-type- and session-dependent; any single "X% from training data" figure should be treated as unreliable.
- The Incumbent Advantage finding that marketing language with fabricated evidence can flip recommendations is a direct vulnerability of the retrieval-override path.

### Gaps
- No study found that measures, on live ChatGPT shopping or Perplexity shopping, what fraction of named products came from a feed/retrieved page versus the model's prior for the same query.
- The definition of "priors" in 2606.26116 and the exact trial design in 2606.17443 were not verifiable from abstracts alone.
- No evidence found for Rufus/Copilot/Gemini on this question.

## Key Question 5 — How do agents handle the "consideration set" step vs. the "final choice" step? Evidence of position bias or list-order effects in LLM product comparisons.

### Takeaway
Controlled agent benchmarks show that when an LLM agent moves from a shortlist to a single pick, the pick is strongly shaped by presentation: all tested models over-select the top row of a results grid (a 5× swing from bottom-right to top-row placement for Claude Sonnet 4), models penalize "Sponsored" tags and heavily reward platform endorsements ("Overall Pick" lifted a 10% baseline to 20–43%), agents in multi-seller markets accept the first adequate offer 80–100% of the time, and welfare *falls* as the number of options grows from 3 to 100 because agents explore too few candidates. Position bias in LLM re-ranking is also documented in the recommender-systems literature, with shuffling-and-merging ("bootstrapping") and iterative-selection prompting as mitigations.

### Cited Findings
- Allouah, Figueroa, Besbes, Kanoria & Kumar, "What Is Your AI Agent Buying?" (arXiv 2508.02630, Aug 2025, v2 Oct 2025; ACES sandbox: 8 products in a 2×4 grid across 8 categories; Claude Sonnet 4, GPT-4.1, Gemini 2.5 Flash): all models favor the top row but differ by column (GPT-4.1 first column, Claude middle columns, Gemini columns 3–4), "undermining the assumption of a universal 'top' rank"; effects persist in text-only "headless" interfaces; for Claude, moving a product from bottom-right (~4.5% selection) to top row col 2–3 raises selection ~fivefold. **[measured; academic preprint]** — [arXiv 2508.02630 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02630v2); [Columbia GSB page](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/what-your-ai-agent-buying-evaluation-implications-and-emerging-questions-agentic-e)
- Same paper — final-choice rationality: GPT-4.1 misses the cheapest of otherwise identical items ~9% of the time when the discount is small; a +0.1 rating edge is missed 15–16% (GPT-4.1) and 28.7% (Claude 4.0) in one section (the paper's own figures are inconsistent across sections — 71.7% cited for GPT-4o in the intro); price elasticities −1.6 to −2.2, comparable to human SKU-level estimates; a "Sponsored" tag lowers a 10% baseline to 8.9% (Claude), 8.0% (GPT-4.1), 7.9% (Gemini); an "Overall Pick" tag raises it to 24.3% / 19.9% / 42.6%; scarcity tags had little effect; choices concentrate on a few "modal" products with some brands (Amazon Basics, Arrow staplers) never chosen; an AI-rewritten seller description raised market share by +2.69 pp (Claude), +5.64 pp (GPT-4.1), +4.83 pp (Gemini) with ~25% of category–model pairs significant. — [arXiv 2508.02630 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02630v2)
- Microsoft Research + ASU, "Magentic Marketplace" (MSR-TR-2025-50, Oct 2025; Bansal, Hua, Huang, Fourney et al.; GPT-5 and Claude Sonnet 4 among frontier models plus open-source models): "severe first-proposal bias, creating 10–30x advantages for response speed over quality"; 80–100% of customer agents accepted the first proposal received without checking alternatives; as search results grew from 3 to 100, welfare dropped for almost every model because agents explored too few businesses ("paradox of choice"); some frontier models resisted fake reviews/fake awards/prompt injection while others had all payments redirected to attackers; frontier models approached optimal welfare only under ideal search conditions. **[measured; simulation, not live commerce]** — [Microsoft Research publication page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/magentic-marketplace-an-open-source-environment-for-studying-agentic-markets/); [PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/multi-agent-marketplace.pdf); press: GPT-4o and GPT-OSS-20b fully compromised, Qwen3-4b fell for authority appeals, Claude Sonnet 4 resisted — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/347709/microsoft-ai-agents-fake-money-buy-online-they-spent-scams)
- Hou et al. (2305.08845): LLM rankers are sensitive to candidate order; performance "drops significantly" when ground-truth items sit in the last positions of a 20-candidate list; bootstrapping (rank 3 times with shuffled candidates, merge) improves results on both datasets; ~3% of GPT-3.5 outputs fell outside the candidate set. **[measured; peer-reviewed, 2023–24]** — [arXiv 2305.08845 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.08845v2)
- Bito, Ren & He, "Evaluating Position Bias in LLM Recommendations" (arXiv 2508.02020, Aug 2025): reports "systemic biases of LLMs with high sensitivity to input orders" and proposes RISE (Ranking via Iterative SElection) prompting to reduce order sensitivity without fine-tuning; quantitative magnitudes not in the abstract. **[measured; preprint]** — [arXiv 2508.02020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02020)
- Islam & Faruk (arXiv 2505.04948, withdrawn by authors): abstract reported that explicit instructions to reduce position bias were ineffective — unreliable given withdrawal. **[withdrawn]** — [arXiv 2505.04948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04948v2)
- Consideration-set size in live products: OpenAI says "not all available products will appear" and that the initial price comes from the first merchant listed; the ChatGPT shopping flow runs a product-level search then a second internal search once specific products are identified (Konitzny via PPC Land). **[vendor claim + secondary]** — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/openai-introduces-shopping-to-chatgpt-with-1-billion-weekly-searches/)
- SparkToro: visibility rate across runs is stable while rank is not (see KQ3) — the consideration set is the real, measurable object; rank within it is noise. — [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-recommendations-change-with-nearly-every-query-sparktoro/566242/)
- Columbia press summary: agents "consistently penalize sponsored tags while rewarding platform endorsements," and "sensitivities to price, ratings, and reviews vary sharply across models." **[secondary]** — [MediaCat](https://mediacat.uk/ai-shopping-agents-prefer-bot-written-copy-study-finds/)

### Inferences
- The two-step structure is empirically visible: a shortlist is shaped by memory + retrieval (KQ4), then the final pick is shaped by presentation order, labels and speed-of-response — which are platform-controlled levers. Whoever controls the ordering of the candidate list (merchant feed provider, Shopping Graph, Bing) therefore has outsized influence on the final choice even when the LLM is "neutral."
- The sponsored-tag penalty plus endorsement bonus suggests agents have absorbed human heuristics about ads; platforms that label picks ("Overall Pick," "Amazon's Choice") can steer agents far more than an ad label can.
- First-proposal bias and choice-overload collapse mean agents behave as satisficers, not optimizers: the realistic consideration set is often the first 1–3 adequate items, consistent with the 3–5-brand "head" stability seen in SparkToro's data.

### Gaps
- All position-bias and first-proposal evidence comes from sandboxes (ACES, Magentic Marketplace) or offline recommender datasets; no measurement found on live ChatGPT/Perplexity/Google shopping carousels of how carousel order affects the model's narrative pick.
- No study found that measures the typical size of the candidate set the model actually sees (e.g., how many feed items are retrieved before it names 3–5).
- Numeric effect sizes for Bito et al. and for Hou et al.'s position experiments live in figures not retrievable here.

## Key Question 6 (synthesis for the writer) — What is the end-to-end "decision pipeline," and which stages are documented vs. inferred?

### Takeaway
Combining official descriptions and measured studies, the pipeline has six stages; three are documented by vendors (query interpretation with user context, retrieval from feeds/indexes/search providers, merchant ranking by provider metadata), and three are only characterized by independent measurement (prior-driven candidate generation, stochastic synthesis, presentation-sensitive final choice).

### Cited Findings
- Stage 1, query interpretation + personalization: documented by OpenAI (Memory, custom instructions, budget shifts weighting; dislikes filter) — [OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11128490-shopping-in-chatgpt); by Amazon (Rufus LLM decides which sources to query per question) — [Amazon Science](https://www.amazon.science/blog/the-technology-behind-amazons-genai-powered-shopping-assistant-rufus); by Google (Gemini models + Shopping Graph; query fan-out claimed only by third parties) — [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/shopping/agentic-checkout-holiday-ai-shopping/); [Productsup (secondary)](https://www.productsup.com/blog/inside-google-shopping-ai-mode-how-it-works-and-what-powers-it/)
- Stage 2, candidate retrieval: ChatGPT uses Bing plus first/third-party structured product metadata, Shopify Catalog and direct feeds, with a second internal product search — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/openai-introduces-shopping-to-chatgpt-with-1-billion-weekly-searches/); [OpenAI feed spec](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/specs/feed); Google uses the Shopping Graph (50B listings, 2B hourly updates) — [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/shopping/agentic-checkout-holiday-ai-shopping/); Amazon uses RAG over catalog/reviews/Q&A/Stores APIs — [Amazon Science](https://www.amazon.science/blog/the-technology-behind-amazons-genai-powered-shopping-assistant-rufus); Perplexity indexes merchant-program data — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/11/18/perplexity-ai-ceo-shopping-product-recommendation-search-tools); Copilot sources recommendations from merchant feed data — [ChannelEngine (secondary)](https://www.channelengine.com/en/marketplaces/copilot)
- Stage 3, prior-driven candidate generation (undocumented, measured): 100% incumbent win on spec ties; Claude 43–52% / ChatGPT 8–29% from priors — [arXiv 2606.17443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17443); [arXiv 2606.26116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26116)
- Stage 4, source selection/citation: platform-specific and volatile (Reddit 60%→10% on ChatGPT in six weeks; 13% URL overlap between Google's two AI surfaces) — [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/most-cited-domains-ai/); [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-vs-ai-mode); quotable-content features raise inclusion 20–40% — [arXiv 2311.09735](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09735v3)
- Stage 5, synthesis/shortlist: stochastic (<1% identical lists; top brands 55–77% presence) — [SEJ/SparkToro](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-recommendations-change-with-nearly-every-query-sparktoro/566242/); set converges by ~10 runs — [arXiv 2609.05059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05059)
- Stage 6, final choice / merchant selection: merchant order "predominantly determined by these providers," ranked by availability, price, quality, primary-seller — [OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11128490-shopping-in-chatgpt); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/openai-introduces-shopping-to-chatgpt-with-1-billion-weekly-searches/); agent picks shaped by grid position (5×), endorsement tags (2–4×), sponsored tags (−10–20%), first-proposal bias (80–100%) — [arXiv 2508.02630](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02630v2); [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/magentic-marketplace-an-open-source-environment-for-studying-agentic-markets/)

### Inferences
- The stages vendors document (1, 2, 6-merchant) are the ones where structured data and explicit rules exist; the stages that determine *which brands get named* (3, 4, 5) are the ones no vendor documents and that independent studies find to be prior-heavy and stochastic.
- For a brand, the practical levers therefore split by stage: feed completeness and eligibility flags (stage 2), off-site mentions/popularity (stage 3), quotable third-party content with stats/citations (stage 4), and rating/label deltas visible at the final-choice step (stage 6).

### Gaps
- No single source — vendor or academic — documents the full pipeline for any one product; the synthesis above is assembled from partial descriptions and should be presented as a reconstruction.
- Claude-with-web-search and Gemini app shopping have essentially no independent mechanistic measurement in the material found.
