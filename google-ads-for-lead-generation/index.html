Google Ads for lead generation: what to set, what to spend, and when to worry

Google Ads for lead generation means paying for Search ads so that people looking for a service call, book or send a form. A lead gen campaign pays when three numbers are set before launch: a target cost per lead worked out from your margin, a daily budget of at least three times that target, and the number of clicks after which zero leads stops being bad luck. This guide gives each rule with a worked example and links the free tool or study that goes deeper.

By Jesse Bastide, Copy for Originals · Published November 7, 2026 · Last updated November 7, 2026

Which Google Ads campaign type is best for lead generation?

A Search campaign is the best place to start lead generation in Google Ads, because it shows ads only to people who typed a search about your service. Our lead gen guides leave out Performance Max, which in our experience is too broad and not proven for lead gen.

Start with one campaign, one service and the area you actually serve, using phrase and exact match keywords. The Lead Gen Search Campaign Checklist walks through every setting in five phases, from account access to the first weeks after launch. Local service businesses can also buy Local Services Ads, which charge per lead instead of per click; the Local Services Ads vs Google Ads guide covers how those leads are charged and credited, using law firms as the example.

How much do Google Ads cost per lead?

A lead from Google Ads costs your cost per click divided by your conversion rate. At $5 a click and a 5% conversion rate, a lead costs $100; at $10 a click and the same rate, it costs $200. LocaliQ’s 2026 averages, quoted in Google Ads in the USA for Swedish companies, are $5.42 per click and $66.69 per lead in search advertising, across 20+ industries.

Plan with your own cost per click, from the account or from the top of page bid (high range) in Google’s Keyword Planner, not an industry average. An average mixes every keyword and every city: LocaliQ’s $9.30 for personal injury clicks sits beside law firm owners who report $200 or more for contested injury keywords. Expensive industries are expensive per lead too: WordStream’s 2026 benchmarks put Attorneys and Legal Services at $131.63 a lead, the highest in its data.

The PPC Troubleshooter has a table of clicks a month and expected leads for daily budgets from $10 to $100 and clicks from $2 to $50, so you can see what a budget buys before you spend it.

How do I set a target CPA for lead generation?

Set a target CPA from your margin, not from what a competitor pays. Gross profit is the average sale times the gross margin; the break-even cost per lead is that gross profit times the share of leads that become customers; and the target CPA is the break-even figure times the share of gross profit you are willing to spend on ads.

Worked example: a $2,000 job at a 50% gross margin is $1,000 of gross profit. If one lead in five becomes a customer, a lead is worth $200 at break-even. Spending half of the gross profit on ads gives a target CPA of $100.

Most people overestimate how many leads close, so check your gut guess against a count from your records. The Target CPA & ROAS Calculator asks one question at a time and gives the number to type into Google Ads. To move an existing target, change it by 15% or less, once a week, starting from what conversions cost now.

How much should I spend on Google Ads for lead generation?

Spend at least three times your target CPA a day on each lead gen campaign. A budget of one or two times the target sounds like enough for a lead or two a day, but one expensive lead or a few costly clicks use up the day, the campaign shows as limited by budget, and Smart Bidding learns from too few conversions.

Monthly spend is the daily budget times 30.4, the average number of days in a month that Google uses for its monthly spending limit. With a $60 target cost per lead, the minimum daily budget is $180, which is about $5,472 a month and enough for roughly 91 leads a month at target.

If three times your target is more than you can spend each day, run one campaign instead of several, use exact match only, and tighten the location to the area that brings your best customers. The rule and the defaults behind it are in the checklist’s budget section; law firms have their own budget guidelines by practice area.

Which Google Ads settings should I change before launching a lead gen campaign?

Change six defaults before a lead gen Search campaign spends anything, because each one widens where ads show without asking: the location option, the Display Network, Search Partners, automatically created assets, auto-applied recommendations and which conversion actions count as primary.

The location default, “Presence or interest,” also shows ads to people outside your area who search about it, so a plumber in Denver pays for clicks from people in Ohio reading about Denver. Count only real leads as primary conversions, such as forms and calls of 60 seconds or more; shorter calls are mostly hang-ups and wrong numbers.

How many clicks without a conversion is normal in Google Ads?

Zero conversions is normal up to about two divided by your expected conversion rate in clicks, and a sign that something is broken past three divided by it. At a 2.5% conversion rate that is normal up to 80 clicks and a red light at 120; at 10% it is normal up to 20 clicks and a red light at 30.

This is the PPC Gumball Rule: once a campaign has spent three times its expected cost per conversion with zero conversions, a campaign converting at the expected rate would look like that only about 5% of the time. Measured in spend, the chance of still seeing zero after spending k times the target CPA is about e−k: 37% at one times, 13.5% at two times and 5% at three times.

In our study of 62 forum threads about clicks but no conversions, 15 of the 23 advertisers who gave a click count were already past the 120-click red light at a 2.5% rate when they asked for help (median 190 clicks), and only 3 of 62 stated a target CPA or expected conversion rate. The PPC Troubleshooter runs the luck math on your own numbers and lists the likely causes and fixes.

How do I find wasted spend in a Google Ads lead gen account?

Find wasted spend in two reports: the search terms report, which shows what people actually typed, and the keyword report, which shows what each keyword cost per conversion against your target.

Read the search terms report for the last 90 days in most accounts, so that more terms reach your limits and zero conversions means more. The Negative Keyword Finder reads that report in your browser and lists the terms and recurring words that spent past your limit without converting, ready to paste as exact- and phrase-match negatives.

The Keyword Triage tool reads the keyword report and labels each keyword Cut, Trim bids, Keep, Scale or Needs data. A keyword is cut when its cost per conversion is more than twice the target, or when it has no conversions after spending the zero-conversion threshold. Neither tool uploads your report anywhere.

Should a lead gen campaign use Smart Bidding or Manual CPC?

Use Smart Bidding with a target CPA when Google receives enough real conversions to learn from, and Manual CPC when it can’t or shouldn’t. Google recommends judging Target CPA performance over periods with at least 30 conversions, and broad match keywords need even more: in our experience, at least 50 conversions a month.

Manual CPC fits when the result that matters isn’t sent to Google: calls you don’t track, sales closed offline, or a healthcare practice that keeps patient data away from Google’s tags. Jesse Bastide’s essays on why you might want manual control over your Google Ads and when to use Manual CPC for healthcare ads make that case from his own accounts; a manual campaign is then judged by whether total leads or new patients grow with spend.

Either way, a person approves the changes. Should AI fully manage your Google Ads campaigns? weighs auto-applied recommendations, AI Max, Smart Bidding without a target and AI agents, and concludes that AI suggests and a person approves.

Why did Google Ads cost per lead go up in 2026?

The cause Reddit advertisers named most often, in 31 of 123 threads, is Google’s August 17, 2026 change to Target CPA and Target ROAS campaigns that were limited by budget: those campaigns now deliver close to the target instead of beating it. It was a bidding change, not a Search algorithm update, and Google says Manual CPC campaigns were not touched.

Our study of 123 first-hand Reddit reports of Google Ads results getting worse from June to October 2026 found 31 posters blaming the August 17 change, a minority of the reports, and one blaming the economy. It sets those reports beside pump prices, consumer confidence and the 2022 analog, with rules of thumb for telling a bidding change from a drop in demand inside your own account.

Can a healthcare practice use Google Ads for lead generation?

A healthcare practice can use Google Ads for new patients as long as no protected health information reaches Google. Google offers no business associate agreement for Google Analytics, and we found no Google page that offers one for Google Ads conversion tracking, so the tracking setup decides what a practice may measure.

The HIPAA-compliant conversion tracking guide compares the options by price and setup effort, from healthcare tracking platforms to click-ID-only offline imports and Manual CPC judged by new-patient growth. Google Ads for fertility clinics covers what Google’s Health category stops a clinic from targeting. Practices and law firms share most of the same problems: few expensive leads, phone calls as the lead and the real result recorded offline, which why healthcare PPC experience transfers to law firms sets side by side.

Does Google Ads lead generation work for law firms?

Google Ads works for a law firm when the budget buys enough clicks to judge it, the firm counts signed cases instead of leads, and someone answers the phone. Legal clicks are among the most expensive in Google Ads, so the 3× budget rule and the math for clicks without a conversion matter more there.

How much does it cost to hire someone to run Google Ads?

Agencies that publish a Google Ads management price start at a median of $500 a month, among 19 agencies pricing in US dollars whose starting fees run from $199 to $3,500. Most charge a fixed monthly fee; 7 of 28 print a percentage of ad spend, from 5% to 20%. Ad spend is paid to Google on top.

Those are businesses that print a price on their own site, so the figures sit lower than what large accounts pay. Google Ads management cost has all 40 agencies looked up, their fee models, setup fees and terms. Whoever runs the account, the business should own it with Admin access and see Google’s exact charges; the 12-point agency scorecard lists the checks, written for law firms but usable by any business.
