How much should a law firm spend on Google Ads? Budgets by practice area

A law firm should budget at least 3 times its expected cost per lead each month for Google Ads. This guide gives the minimum for any cost per click, the published benchmark for seven legal practice areas, and what law firm owners in each reported spending. Criminal defense, family law, estate planning and personal injury have their own pages.

By Copy for Originals · Published October 13, 2026 · Last updated October 13, 2026

The formula

Minimum monthly budget = 3 × cost per click ÷ conversion rate

At $40 a click and a 10% conversion rate, a lead costs $400 and the minimum budget is $1,200 a month. These are example figures; use your own.

Educational only, not legal advice. This guide covers Google Ads Search campaigns for law firm lead generation. Plan with your own cost per click, not an industry average.

How much should a law firm spend on Google Ads?

A law firm should spend at least 3 times its expected cost per lead each month on Google Ads, where cost per lead is cost per click divided by conversion rate. At $40 a click and a 10% conversion rate, a lead costs $400 and the minimum budget is $1,200 a month. Below the minimum, a month with no leads says nothing about whether the campaign works.

The three-times checkpoint is the PPC Gumball Rule: a campaign that spends three times its expected cost per conversion without one is only about 5% likely to be unlucky.

What is the Google Ads budget for each legal practice area?

Using LocaliQ’s median cost per lead for each practice area, three times the cost per lead comes to between $217 a month for estate and probate and $478 a month for personal injury. Those are floors from 2022 and 2023 medians. Law firm owners who reported results spent far more, from $500 to $20,000 a month.

Why is the benchmark budget lower than what law firms actually spend?

Because a benchmark is a median across every keyword and city, and the searches a firm most wants are the expensive ones. LocaliQ’s median personal injury click is $9.30, while law firm owners report injury clicks of $200 or more. A budget built on the median buys far fewer of the clicks that bring cases than the math suggests.

Three more things pull the benchmark down. It is a median, so half of campaigns paid more. It counts as a lead whatever each advertiser tracked, which can include short calls and wrong numbers. And it is from 2022 and 2023; LocaliQ’s 2026 report puts the average legal click at $9.87 across all practice areas, against $9.21 in this one.

Use the benchmark to compare practice areas, and your own account to set a budget.

What about immigration, employment and other practice areas?

LocaliQ’s legal benchmark has no figures for immigration, employment or business law, so use the formula with your own cost per click. In our thread data an immigration solo spent $500 a week on Local Services Ads and an employment solo spent $2,000 a month on Google Ads, and both reported the spend paying for itself.

How do you find your own cost per click and conversion rate?

If your ads have run, read the Avg. CPC column in the Campaigns table of Google Ads, and divide conversions by clicks for the conversion rate. If they have not, use the “Top of page bid (high range)” in Keyword Planner for your main keywords and start with a conversion rate of 5%.

Google describes the high range as roughly the 80th percentile of what advertisers have paid for a keyword’s top of page position. It is a better planning figure than an industry average because it is for your keywords in your area.

A 5% starting conversion rate is cautious against LocaliQ’s medians of 5% to 14% by practice area. Replace it with your own rate once the campaign has a month of clicks.

How long should a law firm test Google Ads before deciding?

Until it has spent 3 times its expected cost per lead, however many days that takes. A firm spending $40 a day with a $400 expected cost per lead reaches the $1,200 checkpoint in 30 days. Zero leads at that point is about 5% likely to be bad luck.

If the checkpoint passes with no leads, check conversion tracking first by submitting a test lead, then the landing page form and phone number, the search terms report and the location settings. The PPC Troubleshooter walks through each check.

A campaign can pass the checkpoint with leads and still lose money if few of them sign. Tracking signed cases back to Google Ads clicks shows which ads produce clients, and the main guide, Google Ads for law firms, covers what else goes wrong.

Limitations

  • The benchmark is a median from 256 campaigns in 2022 and 2023, with as few as 10 campaigns in a practice area. It has no immigration, employment or business law category.
  • LocaliQ’s cost per click, conversion rate and cost per lead are separate medians, so they do not multiply out exactly.
  • The owner-reported ranges come from a handful of owners per practice area on r/LawFirm. They are not typical results.
  • The three-times rule treats clicks as independent. Repeat clicks, bots or one bad keyword make a month with no leads more likely than the math says.
  • This guide is about Google Ads budgets, not legal advertising rules. Check your state bar’s advertising rules separately.
