How much should a estate planning law firm spend on Google Ads?

A estate planning law firm should budget at least 3 times its expected cost per lead each month for Google Ads. Below that, a month with no leads can’t be told apart from bad luck. This guide gives the minimum by cost per click and conversion rate, the published benchmark for estate planning search ads, and what estate planning firm owners reported spending.

By Copy for Originals · Published October 13, 2026 · Last updated October 13, 2026

The formula

Minimum monthly budget = 3 × cost per click ÷ conversion rate

At $15 a click and a 10% conversion rate, a lead costs $150 and the minimum budget is $450 a month. These are example figures; use your own.

Educational only, not legal advice. This guide covers Google Ads Search campaigns for estate planning lead generation. Plan with your own cost per click, not an industry average.

How much should a estate planning law firm spend on Google Ads at minimum?

A estate planning law firm should budget at least 3 times its expected cost per lead each month, where cost per lead is cost per click divided by conversion rate. At $15 a click and a 10% conversion rate, a lead costs $150 and the minimum budget is $450 a month.

That is the PPC Gumball Rule checkpoint. A campaign that spends three times its expected cost per lead with no lead at all is only about 5% likely to be unlucky. With a smaller budget the firm can still get leads, but a month without any tells it nothing.

The conversion rate is the share of ad clicks that call or submit a form. Find your cost per click in Google Ads under Campaigns, in the Avg. CPC column. For a new account, use the “Top of page bid (high range)” in Keyword Planner for the keywords you will bid on.

The minimum gets you a verdict, not a caseload. To plan for a number of leads, budget leads wanted × cost per lead. The law firm Google Ads budget guidelines cover daily budgets, bid strategy and when to raise or cut spend.

What does a estate planning lead cost on Google Ads?

LocaliQ’s legal benchmarks put the median estate planning search ad at $7.92 a click, a 9.65% conversion rate and $72.24 a lead, from campaigns that ran between April 2022 and March 2023. Three times that cost per lead is about $217 a month, which is a floor and not a plan, because the keywords a firm most wants cost more than the median.

Read the benchmark with three cautions. LocaliQ says its averages “are technically median figures,” so half of campaigns paid more. The whole legal sample was 256 campaigns, with at least 10 in each practice area. And the data is from 2022 and 2023.

The figures also do not multiply out: $7.92 ÷ 9.65% is $82, not $72.24, because each is a separate median. Use your own cost per click and conversion rate in the table above.

What do estate planning firms report spending on Google Ads?

Estate planning owners on r/LawFirm reported Google Ads spend from $500 to $5,000 a month. One solo said $2,000 a month of search and Local Services Ads drove most of the firm’s growth, and another stopped a $3,500 a month campaign because SEO was better value.

Is $500 a month enough for estate planning Google Ads?

It can be, if clicks are cheap enough. At $15 a click, $500 buys 33 clicks, and at a 10% conversion rate the chance of no leads at all from 33 clicks is about 3%, with 3.3 leads expected. At $30 a click the same budget buys 17 clicks and the chance of a month with no leads rises to about 17%.

The three-times minimum at $15 a click and 10% is $450 a month, so $500 is above it. One estate planning solo in our thread data started at $500 a month across Google Search ads and Local Services Ads before raising it to $2,000.

The odds come from one line of arithmetic: the chance of zero leads is (1 − conversion rate) raised to the number of clicks. The PPC Troubleshooter runs it for any budget and cost per click.

What can a estate planning firm afford to pay for a lead?

A estate planning firm can afford to pay for a lead the fee from an average case, times its margin, times the share of leads that sign, times the share of that profit it is willing to spend on ads. The answer comes from the firm’s own fees and sign rate, not from a benchmark.

Estate planning is mostly flat-fee work, so the sum is simple: one owner paying $3,500 a month for SEO said a single new client covers it. If a plan is worth $3,000 and one lead in four signs, each lead is worth $750 in fees before costs.

Should a estate planning firm use Local Services Ads or Search ads?

Compare them by cost per signed case, because Local Services Ads charge per lead and Search ads charge per click. One solo doing family law and some probate reported about 20 mostly good calls a day from $2,000 a month of Local Services Ads.

Owners in transactional practices reported Local Services Ads leads at $70 to $80 each.

The Local Services Ads vs Google Ads guide for lawyers has a calculator that compares the two on your own numbers and explains which leads Google credits.

Limitations

  • The budget is only as good as the cost per click and conversion rate you enter. Check both against your own account after the first month.
  • The benchmark is a median from a small sample of campaigns in 2022 and 2023, and its definition of a lead is whatever each advertiser tracked.
  • The owner reports are what individual owners wrote on r/LawFirm, one firm each, with vendor names withheld.
  • Estate planning owners in our data talked about SEO more than about ads: 14 of the 32 estate planning and probate rows in the data file are about SEO. People often take months to decide on a will or trust, which also makes ad results slower to judge.
  • The three-times rule treats clicks as independent. Repeat clicks, bots or one bad keyword make a month with no leads more likely than the math says.
  • This guide is about Google Ads budgets, not legal advertising rules. Check your state bar’s advertising rules separately.
