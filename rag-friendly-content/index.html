How to write content that LLMs retrieve and cite

RAG-friendly content is web content that a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipeline can fetch, split into chunks, match to a question and quote without losing meaning. This guide explains what answer engines such as ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Microsoft Copilot and Google AI Overviews do with a page, and rates every common answer engine optimization (AEO) practice by the strength of its evidence.

By Copy for Originals · Last updated October 3, 2026

  • Strong primary docs, peer-reviewed or controlled experiment
  • Moderate large observational or industry study
  • Inference follows from how RAG works, not tested on production engines
  • Weak widely believed, little or mixed evidence

Short answer

A page gets cited by answer engines when every fact is in the raw HTML (most AI crawlers don’t run JavaScript), when each section makes sense on its own, when headings closely match the questions people actually ask, and when the text contains specific, quotable facts such as numbers, formulas, prices and dates. Schema markup and llms.txt files show no measurable effect on citations. Domain authority and presence in the Google and Bing indexes still matter more than any single page-level change.

How answer engines process a web page

An answer engine processes a web page in five steps: it fetches the HTML, extracts the main text, splits the text into chunks, retrieves the chunks closest to each sub-question, and reranks them before quoting the best ones. Each step drops some content, and each one implies a writing rule.

Why JavaScript-rendered content is invisible to most AI crawlers

JavaScript-rendered content is invisible to ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity crawlers because those crawlers download the HTML and never execute the scripts. Vercel’s analysis of crawler traffic found that some AI crawlers download JavaScript files without running them, and that ChatGPT’s and Claude’s crawlers spend about 34% of their fetches on 404 pages, compared with about 8% for Googlebot. Strong

To see what an AI crawler sees, request the page with curl and read the raw HTML. Anything missing from that output, such as text inserted by React, numbers drawn on a canvas, or results computed in the browser, cannot be cited.

How to structure a page so each section can be retrieved on its own

Structure a page so that every H2 section answers one question completely in roughly 150 to 400 words, starts with the direct answer, and names its subject explicitly. Retrieval systems judge each chunk alone, so a section that depends on the one above it is hard to match to a question. Inference

  • One H1, then a clean H2 and H3 tree. Heading-aware splitters cut at headings and often attach the heading path to the chunk.
  • Open with a definition: “X is a Y that does Z for W.” A definition is the ideal standalone chunk.
  • Answer first, then explain. Treat every section as a small inverted pyramid.
  • Repeat the entity name. A chunk that says “it supports CSV” can’t be matched to the question “Does Keyword Triage support CSV?”.
  • Write formulas and thresholds as plain text, for example “efficiency = target CPA ÷ keyword CPA; below 0.5 is Cut”.
  • Use real tables and lists. Microsoft recommends comparison tables, numbered steps and Q&A formats for content it wants Copilot to quote. Microsoft Advertising

Which writing techniques increase AI citations

The writing techniques with the best evidence are matching the user’s question closely, adding statistics, quotations and cited sources, and stating explicit prices, numbers and dates. Formatting changes on their own have little effect.

Does schema markup help with AI citations?

Schema markup on its own does not measurably increase AI citations. When Ahrefs tracked 1,885 pages that added JSON-LD schema between August 2025 and March 2026 against matched control pages, citations changed by −4.6% in Google AI Overviews, +2.4% in AI Mode and +2.2% in ChatGPT, and the last two were statistically indistinguishable from zero. Moderate Ahrefs

In a separate test, none of five AI systems found a product price that existed only in JSON-LD. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini fetched the page directly, and Perplexity and Google AI Mode answered from their search indexes. Strong searchVIU

Schema is still worth adding for classic search and for describing entities: WebApplication or SoftwareApplication for tools, Article, Organization, BreadcrumbList and dateModified. The rule is that JSON-LD must repeat visible text and never be the only place a fact appears.

Does llms.txt help AI search visibility?

There is no evidence that llms.txt increases AI citations. SE Ranking analyzed about 300,000 domains, found llms.txt on about 10% of them, and found no meaningful relationship between having the file and how often a domain is cited. Moderate Search Engine Journal

An llms.txt file, as proposed at llmstxt.org, is a Markdown file at the site root with an H1 name, a one-line summary in a blockquote, and H2 sections of annotated links. It is cheap to add and can help coding agents pointed at documentation, but don’t expect it to change answer-engine citations.

Which AI crawlers to allow in robots.txt

To appear in AI answers, allow the search and user-fetch crawlers. Blocking the training crawlers keeps your content out of model training, but does not remove you from AI search answers.

RAG-friendly content checklist

Use this 12-point checklist before publishing any page you want answer engines to cite.

  1. Every fact is in server-rendered HTML. Check it with curl.
  2. One H1 that names the entity and the category people search for.
  3. A definitional first sentence: “X is a Y that does Z.”
  4. Question-shaped H2s, each answered in its first sentence.
  5. Self-contained sections that repeat the entity’s name instead of “it”.
  6. Numbers, thresholds, formulas and prices in plain text.
  7. A worked example with real numbers.
  8. Real tables with captions and header cells, and lists for steps.
  9. Two or three citations to primary sources.
  10. An FAQ for adjacent questions, a glossary and a limitations section.
  11. A visible byline and “Last updated” date that match dateModified.
  12. A canonical URL, a sitemap with lastmod, a robots.txt that allows search and user crawlers, and submission to both Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
