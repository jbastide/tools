Entry prices on each vendor’s own pricing page, read October 31, 2026: CallRail $50 a month on a yearly plan ($55 monthly), WhatConverts $30 a month with a $30 usage credit, CallTrackingMetrics $65 a month billed yearly ($79 monthly) and CallScaler $65 a month on its yearly option ($95 monthly), each plus usage; Invoca gives quotes only. What does call tracking do for a law firm’s Google Ads?

Call tracking gives each ad or marketing source its own phone number, so a law firm can see which ads produce calls and count those calls as conversions in Google Ads. Google can do this itself with its own forwarding numbers, which it provides on a website at no cost; a call tracking vendor instead rents the firm tracking numbers by the month, records the calls under its own settings, and posts them back to Google Ads as conversions.

Google’s own route. Google’s help page says: “Tracking call conversions from ads and from a website and importing call conversions require Google forwarding numbers.” Numbers that replace your business number on your site are “dynamically-generated Google forwarding numbers” that come “without any cost to you.” Google counts a call as a conversion in one of two ways, “by analyzing call recordings using Google’s AI to identify high-quality leads, or by counting calls that last longer than a minimum duration you set.”

Two things come with it. Google’s call recording setting “is now enabled by default to allow for AI-powered lead qualification,” except where the advertiser turned it off earlier or Google identified the business as healthcare or financial services, and callers hear “a short automated message at the beginning of the call” saying the call will be recorded. And Google’s forwarding numbers are Google’s: they “can change or be reassigned,” and “you can’t use your Google forwarding number outside of the call reporting, and website call conversion tracking.” What Google records and keeps is covered in our page on whether Google records calls to law firms.

A vendor’s route. The firm pays a plan fee plus usage for numbers and minutes, gets recordings kept under the vendor’s retention rules, and can send calls to Google Ads tied to the ad click. Google also accepts call conversions “that you track in another system,” and offline conversions matched by the Google click ID, which is how a firm reports a lead that later signs; our guide to tracking signed cases back to Google Ads clicks covers that import. This page is part of the Google Ads for law firms guide.

Can a law firm record calls from its call tracking numbers?

Federal law allows recording when one party to the call consents, but state laws differ, and the vendors say so themselves. The federal wiretap statute says it is not unlawful for a person not acting under color of law to intercept a call “where such person is a party to the communication or where one of the parties to the communication has given prior consent to such interception”; CallRail’s help adds that “Some states require both parties to consent to any recorded conversation.”

The vendors’ own wording, in short:

  • CallRail: “Laws concerning call recordings differ across U.S. states and federal agencies.” It lets an account enable automated greetings that tell callers the line is recorded, and recording can be switched off on a tracking number, though not paused during a call.
  • CallTrackingMetrics summarises federal law as requiring that “at least one party taking part in the call must be notified of the recording (18 U.S.C. §2511(2)(d))” (its summary, in its words, not the statute’s), and says some states require only one party to be aware while others require both.
  • CallScaler’s terms: “You are solely responsible for complying with all applicable call recording laws in every jurisdiction where your calls occur. Many states and countries require all-party consent before recording a call.”
  • WhatConverts lists Call Recording, Call Greeting and Call Whisper as options to review when setting up a tracking number.

This page doesn’t list the states: the research for it didn’t read state statutes. No lawyer reviewed this page. Before recording, check your state’s law and your bar’s rules with your own lawyer. Bar publications on recorded ad calls are on our page about Google’s call recording.

Can a law firm use a call tracking number with Local Services Ads?

Google’s Local Services Ads help says the business phone number, “the phone number where your calls are routed to”, “must not be a tracking number” (Google, read October 31, 2026). Changing that number goes through Google support. CallRail and WhatConverts publish set-up steps that have Google support put one of their tracking numbers in that field, and CallScaler’s law-firm page says to assign a tracking number to the LSA profile. Google’s page names no exception, including for a number its support team enters. Before you follow a vendor’s LSA steps, ask Google support whether a tracking number is allowed on your account, and get the answer in writing. Your LSA lead reports already show charged leads by call, message and booking.

The number on the ad itself is a Google forwarding number that forwards calls to your business number, and in the US and Canada Google records all inbound calls through it, with a pre-recorded message to the caller first. Google’s page also says you may not hear the “Call from Google” message if any type of call forwarding or VoIP is set up on the business phone number.

Two vendor details matter if you go ahead. CallRail says LSAs “require a direct, manual number assignment in your LSA account” that “cannot be done via CallRail’s API.” WhatConverts says such calls are tracked in its account, “though conversions won’t be sent back to Google Ads unless you’re using Enhanced Conversions.”

The rules may move. Google says existing Local Services Ads campaigns “will be automatically migrated to a specialized Performance Max campaign type optimized specifically for pay-per-lead goals,” that in Google Ads “you can edit the phone number to which your call and message leads are routed in real time,” and that LSA performance reports “will not transition to Google Ads.” The August 2026 phase names home and storefront services; the late 2026 phase covers service-area businesses without storefronts; remaining categories move in 2027. Law firms aren’t named, and the migration page doesn’t say whether the tracking-number rule carries over. The Local Services Ads vs Google Ads guide for lawyers covers the move.

Which call tracking tools send calls to Google Ads by click ID?

CallRail and CallTrackingMetrics say so in their help pages and name the same three Google click IDs (gclid, gbraid and wbraid); CallScaler’s feature page says it matches calls to ad clicks via gclid; WhatConverts says it sends each new Google Ads lead to Google Ads as a conversion without naming the click ID; and Invoca’s pricing page lists offline conversion import with a Google Ads integration.

  • CallRail: “Our integration posts conversions using Google’s click IDs (gclid, wbraid, and gbraid.)” It needs visitor tracking and auto-tagging, counts only calls to the website pool with a source of Google Ads (“Calls to source trackers won’t report as conversions with this integration”), and doesn’t apply to calls before it was switched on. Its manual import covers calls and forms “that included a Google Ads click (gclid) within the past 90 days.”
  • CallTrackingMetrics: its Google Ads Direct Connect integration “lets you track conversions directly into Google Ads for any activity with a GCLID, GBRAID, or WBRAID” (help article dated September 30, 2022).
  • CallScaler: “CallScaler’s Google Ads integration matches phone calls to ad clicks via gclid and sends conversions back to Google Ads in real time” (its own feature page, May 2026).
  • WhatConverts: offers “one of three ways to send your leads to Google Ads as conversions,” the first being “New Lead Received.”
  • Invoca: Pro lists “Import offline conversions and revenue” by file upload or API, and integrations with Google Ads and Google Analytics 4.

Without a vendor, Google’s offline conversion import does the same job for a lead that signs later: you save the Google Click ID with the lead and, when that person “converts” in the offline world, “by signing a contract, for example,” you give that GCLID back to Google Ads. The signed-case tracking guide has the steps.

What call tracking do law firms actually use?

In the site’s own homepage scan of 87 Chicago personal injury firms (October 6, 2026), 34% of homepages ran call tracking, and CallRail was on 24 of the 30 sites with call tracking. Advertisers in the scan were more likely to show it: 22 of 42 advertisers (52%) against 8 of 45 non-advertisers (18%).

These are figures from the study’s page, not from its data file: per-firm technology flags were kept out of the public data. Two caveats from the study apply. Tools loaded through Google Tag Manager or on inner pages are undercounted, and after its October 13, 2026 correction the study says the shares in its technology tables may be understated by up to about five percentage points. The method and the other tools it found are in the Chicago personal injury lead generation study.
