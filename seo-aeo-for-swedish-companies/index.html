What is the difference between SEO and AEO?

SEO, search engine optimization, is about a page ranking high in Google’s regular search results. AEO, answer engine optimization, is about the page being retrieved and cited when an AI service such as ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity or Google’s AI Overviews answers a question. One page can do both, but the requirements are not identical.

The two are less tied together than you might think. According to an Ahrefs measurement reported in our guide to content that LLMs retrieve and cite, only 38% of the pages cited in Google AI Overviews in March 2026 ranked in the top ten for the original query. So a page can be cited without ranking first, if it holds the best answer to a sub-question.

Google says AEO is just SEO. Is that right?

Partly. Google writes in its guidance that optimizing for generative AI search is still SEO, and for Google’s own AI features that is broadly true. But Google speaks only for Google Search. ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity fetch and read pages in their own way, and there it isn’t enough for the page to rank. The sensible course is to follow Google where independent measurements back Google up, and to go further where the mechanics of the AI services call for it.

“From Google Search’s perspective, optimizing for generative AI search is optimizing for the search experience, and thus still SEO.”

The reason to go further than Google’s advice is mechanical. An AI service fetches the HTML, extracts the main text, splits it into passages and quotes the passages that best answer the question. A page written for that chain also works for Google. The reverse isn’t always true. The whole chain, with sources, is described in our guide.

On Reddit, the most established voices almost all agree with Google. Among moderators and flair-verified professionals in the SEO forums, 23 of 24 coded replies said AEO is the same work as SEO. Across all 2,969 coded top replies it was closer: 151 to 104. The question they rarely answer was asked by one poster: “If AEO and GEO is 90% SEO then why is my website ranking on google but not showing in AI search?” The answer is that ranking and being cited are two different steps.

One reservation: the evidence is strongest for the mechanics and for controlled experiments. Case studies of individual companies’ results mostly come from the people selling the tools, and we don’t cite those.

How do you get ChatGPT, Claude and Google’s AI answers to mention your company?

Nobody can order a mention, but three things have to be in place for one to be possible: the AI services’ crawlers must be allowed to fetch the page, the page must contain a clear answer with facts that can be quoted, and the website must be in Google’s and Bing’s indexes. Beyond that, what others write about the company matters, and no company fully controls that.

  • Let the right crawlers in. OpenAI writes that sites opted out of its OAI-SearchBot crawler will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers. A firewall can do that without the owner knowing: in our study, 13 of 78 websites refused a request carrying an AI crawler’s name.
  • Answer the question with facts of your own. This is the part the framework is built for: a question customers ask, and an answer with numbers nobody else has.
  • What others write. In our coded Reddit replies, third-party mentions were named most often as the way to get cited (444 replies), ahead of the content on your own page (343) and ordinary rankings (106). The most established voices answered the other way round: 18 of their replies said ranking comes first, and 2 pointed to mentions. Google, for its part, writes that seeking inauthentic mentions across the web isn’t as helpful as it might seem.

The service covers the first two points. Mentions planted for the AI services’ sake are not part of it.

Why doesn’t our company show up on Google or in AI answers?

A company doesn’t show up on Google or in AI answers for one of three reasons: the website has no page that answers the question customers ask, the page says the same thing as a hundred others, or the crawlers can’t read it. The first and second are solved with content, the third with technology.

  • No page answers the question. Customers ask what it costs, how long it takes and what can go wrong. The website describes the company.
  • The page says what everyone else says. Then there is no reason to place it ahead of the others, and nothing in it to quote.
  • The crawler can’t get in. In our study of 100 Chicago law firm websites, 13 of 78 sites (17%) refused a request carrying an AI crawler’s name, and 12 of the 13 had no robots.txt rule that explained it. The owner often doesn’t know the firewall is doing it.
  • The facts are only in JavaScript or in images. Most AI services’ crawlers don’t run JavaScript. What isn’t in the raw HTML can’t be cited.

The fear of being invisible online is reasonable. Someone who asks an AI service about a supplier in your industry gets an answer built from the pages the service could read. If your page isn’t among them, you never learn that the question was asked.

How do you measure visibility in AI answers, and can the numbers be trusted?

Measure what the search services report themselves and what shows up in your own systems, and be careful with tools that state a position in AI answers. Since 2026 Google Search Console has a report on impressions in AI Overviews and AI Mode, and Bing Webmaster Tools shows how often pages are cited in Copilot. In SparkToro’s study, the same prompt returned the same list of brands in fewer than 1 in 100 runs, so a single position says almost nothing.

Does visibility in AI answers bring in any customers?

That is not settled. What can be measured is that Google’s own AI answers reduce clicks: Ahrefs measured a 58% lower clickthrough rate for the top-ranking page when an AI Overview was shown, in data from December 2025. Whether visitors from AI services become customers is disputed. In our coded Reddit replies, 29 said they convert well and 31 said the volume is too small to matter.

  • The clicks. Pew Research Center followed the searches of 900 US adults in March 2025. When an AI summary appeared, users clicked a traditional search result in 8% of visits, against 15% without a summary. A link inside the summary was clicked in 1% of visits.
  • Sweden. According to the Swedish Internet Foundation’s report Svenskarna och internet 2026, just over six in ten Swedes use AI services, against four in ten the year before. 52% use ChatGPT, and one in seven Swedes say they google less often, with AI services as the main reason.

The conclusion is not to move the budget from Google to the AI services. It is that the same page should work for both, and that you should ask new customers how they found you, so you know instead of guessing.

What is original content built on open sources (OSINT)?

Original content built on open sources is pages that report something new, worked out from publicly available material such as statistics, registers, government pages, forums and reviews. OSINT stands for open source intelligence. The point is that the reader can check every item, and that no competitor has the same numbers.

The difference from ordinary content production is where the text starts. An ordinary article starts with a keyword and summarizes what is already online. A page built on open sources starts with a question customers ask that nobody has answered with numbers: What do companies in our industry actually pay? How many of our competitors do this? What do customers complain about most?

That kind of material has the strongest support in the research on what AI services cite. In the GEO study (KDD 2024), reported in our guide, statistics, quotations and cited sources raised a source’s visibility in generated answers by up to about 40%, while keyword stuffing did worse than doing nothing.

How does the agentic framework work?

The agentic framework splits the work into fixed phases and gives each phase to whoever does it best: AI agents collect, sort and check the material, and a person picks the question, judges the conclusions and approves what gets published. The phases are the same every time, so the result can be audited afterwards.

  1. The question. What do your customers ask before they buy, and which open sources can answer it?
  2. Collection. Agents fetch the material from the sources. Every item is saved with its address and date.
  3. Sorting. The material is coded into a dataset that can be counted, and that can be published with the page.
  4. Checking. Every quote is compared word for word with the page it came from. Claims that say more than the source shows are cut or rewritten.
  5. Writing. The page is written so both search engines and AI services can read it: questions as headings, the answer first, real tables, sources and dates.
  6. Approval. I go through the conclusions, and you approve what is published in your name.
  7. Updating. When the sources change, the page is corrected, with a version number and a date.

Can content produced by AI agents be trusted?

Not without checking, which is why checking is a phase of its own. In our own study of nine agent runs, a script found all 593 quotes word for word on the pages given as sources, but two independent graders judged that 41 of the 593 claims (6.9%) said more than the quote showed. The quotes were real. It was the conclusions that went too far.

That is how AI-produced content usually goes wrong: the source exists, but it doesn’t quite say what the text claims. A framework that only checks that the link works misses it. So every claim is compared with its quote, and a person decides what stays. The full study is here: Haiku, Sonnet or Opus for online research?

Is content produced by AI agents the same as programmatic SEO?

No. Traditional programmatic SEO fills a template with a list of keywords and produces hundreds of pages that say the same thing with different place names. An agentic framework for original content does the opposite: one question, new material, one page. AI is used as an intelligent collaborator that carries out tasks hands-off and coordinates other agents, not as a machine that prints pages. Google’s scaled content abuse policy is about many pages with no value to users, no matter how they were created.

Google’s own texts draw the line at value, not at the tool. The scaled content abuse policy describes large amounts of unoriginal content “that provides little to no value to users, no matter how it’s created”. Google’s guidance on generative AI also says the technology “can be particularly useful when researching a topic, and to add structure to original content”, and that all AI-generated content should be fact-checked by a person before it is published. That last part is the checking phase and the approval in the framework.

The line cuts both ways. An agentic framework can also be misused to print pages with nothing new in them. That is why the checking and the approval are not optional.

When is this not a fit?

This is not a fit for companies that want a guaranteed ranking, many texts fast or a new website. Nobody can promise a position on Google or a mention in an AI answer, and anyone who does is guessing.

  • No ranking guarantees. What can be promised is that every number on the page can be traced to its source.
  • Quality before volume. One page with numbers of its own takes longer than ten pages without.
  • You publish on your own website. I don’t build websites at the moment, so someone on your side has to be able to put the page up.
  • You approve the claims. In industries with advertising rules, you and your lawyer decide what may be said.

FAQ

Does AI write all the content?

No. The AI agents collect, sort and check the material and write drafts. Which question to answer, which conclusions hold and what gets published is decided by a person.

Do you promise a position on Google or in AI answers?

No. Nobody can promise a position on Google or a mention in an AI answer. What I promise is that the content is built on facts that can be checked and that the page is written so search engines and AI services can read it.

What language is the content produced in?

Swedish or English. English is my native language. Swedish copy is produced with the AI tool Claude and proofread by Swedish-speaking proofreaders at Copy for Originals before it is published. The Swedish version of this page was produced the same way.

We rank on Google but don’t show up in AI answers. Why?

Ranking and being cited are two different steps. According to Ahrefs, only 38% of the pages cited in Google AI Overviews in March 2026 ranked in the top ten for the original query. ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity also fetch pages with their own crawlers, which can be shut out by a firewall and which don’t run JavaScript.

What should you ask an agency that sells AEO?

Ask what is delivered, how it is measured and what happens if nothing changes. Warning signs are guaranteed mentions, a promised position in AI answers, llms.txt as the main deliverable and a large number of pages a month. Google writes that third-party tools have no access to Google’s data and can’t guarantee performance. We have not found any independent survey of what AEO services cost.

The boss or the client is asking about AI search. What do you say?

That three things can be shown without guessing: whether the AI services’ crawlers can reach the website, how many impressions the pages get in Google’s AI answers according to Search Console, and which customer questions the website has no answer to. That is a more honest picture than a position in an AI answer that changes the next time the question is asked.

The AI service says wrong things about our company. Can it be corrected?

Not directly, because no outsider can edit an AI answer. What can be done is to publish the correct facts clearly on your own website, in raw HTML and with dates, so the service has something correct to fetch the next time the question is asked. There is no guarantee the answer changes.

Should we start posting on Reddit to show up in AI answers?

Only if your customers actually ask questions there and you have something factual to answer. In our coded Reddit replies, third-party mentions were named most often as the way to get cited, but Google writes that seeking inauthentic mentions isn’t as helpful as it might seem. About one in four threads about AEO in our review was promotion, and it tends to be recognized.

Do llms.txt and schema markup help a page get cited by AI services?

Not measurably, according to the studies reported in our guide. SE Ranking found no relationship between llms.txt and how often a domain is cited across about 300,000 domains, and Ahrefs found no reliable increase for 1,885 pages that added schema markup. What has support is facts in raw HTML, sections that can be understood alone and concrete facts to quote.
