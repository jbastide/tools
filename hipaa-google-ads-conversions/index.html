HIPAA-compliant conversion tracking for Google Ads: the options compared

A HIPAA-covered practice can run Google Ads only if no protected health information (PHI) reaches Google, because Google offers no business associate agreement (BAA) for Google Analytics and we found no Google page that offers one for Google Ads conversion tracking (Local Services Ads, which has its own BAA, is the exception). In practice that means conversion tracking that tells Google Ads which ad clicks became leads or patients while sending Google only the click ID, the conversion time, an optional value and a neutral conversion name. This guide compares every way to do that we found as of October 2026, from healthcare tracking platforms such as Freshpaint to click-ID-only offline conversion imports, with prices, setup effort, and whether a practice without an IT department can realistically run each one. This guide is educational only and is not legal advice. Confirm any tracking setup with a healthcare attorney or your compliance officer before you use it.

By Copy for Originals · Last updated October 31, 2026 · Sources accessed October 5, 2026, rechecked October 30, 2026; WhatConverts checked October 31, 2026

Short answer

Every compliant option works the same way at the end: Google receives only the ad click ID, a timestamp, an optional value and a neutral conversion name, never anything that identifies the patient or the care they asked about. What differs is who does the work and what it costs. A Google Ads offline conversion import that sends only the click ID needs no tracking vendor, but takes staff time. Managed healthcare platforms such as Curve and Ours Privacy do the setup for you; neither shows a dollar price on its pricing page. Freshpaint and the enterprise CDPs typically cost tens of thousands of dollars a year. For a practice whose ad budget doesn’t justify those costs, Manual CPC judged by new-patient growth is a sound alternative.

Can a HIPAA-covered practice run Google Ads?

A HIPAA-covered practice can run Google Ads if no protected health information (PHI) reaches Google through its tags, uploads or audiences. Google offers no business associate agreement (BAA) for Google Analytics, we found no Google page that offers one for Google Ads conversion tracking, and Google’s ad policies keep health conversions out of enhanced conversions and bar advertisers promoting products or services in Google’s Health sensitive interest category from using advertiser-curated audiences. Local Services Ads is the exception on the BAA: Google signs one for it.

  • Google Analytics: no BAA. Google’s help page says Google “does not offer Business Associate Agreements in connection with this service,” and that HIPAA-regulated entities “must refrain from exposing to Google any data that may be considered Protected Health Information (PHI)”. Primary Google Analytics Help
  • Google Ads: no BAA found. No Google page we read offers a BAA for Google Ads conversion tracking. Google Cloud does sign a BAA, and Cloud Run is a covered service, but Google Ads, Google Analytics and Google Tag Manager are not on the covered list. Primary Google Cloud
  • Local Services Ads: Google signs a BAA. Google’s Local Services Help says providers “who are subject to HIPAA and want to use Local Services Ads must review and accept a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) with Google.” The BAA covers features of Local Services Ads, or Google products named on the Local Services Ads site, “that enable connections or communications between an End User and a Customer.” Search campaigns and their conversion tags are not named. Primary Local Services Help, Local Services Ads BAA
  • Customer data policy: no health conversions in enhanced conversions or store sales uploads. Google lists “Health or medical information, like purchases of medical services, prescription drugs or medical devices” among its sensitive categories, and says “Conversions related to sensitive categories can’t be used for measurement in enhanced conversions or store sales (uploads).” Primary Google Ads Help
  • Personalized advertising: Health is a sensitive category. Google counts personal health content, such as “Physical or mental health conditions, including diseases, sexual health, and chronic health conditions”, as a sensitive interest category. Advertisers promoting products and services in sensitive interest categories “are unable to use advertiser-curated audiences”; they can use predefined Google audiences, which Google says are configured without sensitive user signals. Google’s Customer Match policy says the same rules apply to remarketing with Customer Match. Primary Health in personalized advertising, Personalized advertising, Customer Match policy
  • HHS guidance: narrower since 2024, as law firms describe it. According to Proskauer, the HHS-OCR bulletin said HIPAA obligations are triggered where a tracking technology connects an individual’s IP address with a visit to “an unauthenticated public webpage addressing specific health conditions or health care providers”, which Proskauer calls the “Proscribed Combination”. Morrison Foerster says HHS revised the guidance on March 18, 2024, after the American Hospital Association challenged it in court, and that the court vacated the guidance as to the Proscribed Combination. Proskauer says HHS-OCR withdrew its appeal of that Northern District of Texas order (Am. Hosp. Ass’n v. Becerra, June 20, 2024) on August 29, 2024. At the time of its July 2024 alert, Morrison Foerster wrote that the other portions of the guidance “remain in effect”, and that regulated entities still need to keep tracking technologies off authenticated (login-protected) pages, and off public pages that clearly indicate a subjective motive for the visit, unless they have a BAA. Law-firm summaries Morrison Foerster, Proskauer

Why can’t a healthcare practice use the standard Google Ads conversion tag?

A healthcare practice can’t safely use the standard Google Ads conversion tag because the tag sends Google the visitor’s IP address, page URLs and browser identifiers, and no Google page we found offers a business associate agreement (BAA) for Google Ads conversion tracking. Without a BAA, no PHI may reach Google.

  • Lawsuits don’t depend on HIPAA alone. Proskauer wrote in September 2024 of an “ongoing wave” of class actions over website tracking technologies under state wiretapping and telecommunications laws, and said caution around website tracking should continue. Proskauer. Sutter Health’s $21.5 million settlement over Google Analytics and Meta Pixel on its patient-portal login page received final approval in March 2026. ClassAction.org

Which option fits a practice without an IT department?

A practice without an IT department has three realistic paths: pay a managed healthcare platform to do the work, run a click-ID-only offline conversion import with existing staff, or skip conversion-based bidding and run Manual CPC. The right choice depends on how the tracking cost compares with the ad budget.

  • Managed platform (Curve, Ours Privacy). The least work for the practice and the highest cost. Neither Curve nor Ours Privacy publishes a price, so get a quote and compare it with the monthly ad budget before signing.
  • Offline conversion import. No tracking vendor, and practical for a front desk that can update a spreadsheet. It needs the website form to capture the click ID, which many scheduling and EHR booking widgets can’t do. See the steps below.
  • Manual CPC. No conversion data sent to Google at all. Results are judged by new-patient growth against ad spend. See Can Manual CPC work without conversion tracking?

Server-side GTM and the enterprise CDPs need a developer or analytics consultant to set up and maintain, so they fit practices that already work with one.

How does a Google Ads offline conversion import work?

A Google Ads offline conversion import records a conversion by uploading the ad click ID (GCLID) and the conversion time, so Google learns which clicks produced patients without receiving any patient data. Google’s file-import help page now calls offline conversion import “a legacy feature” and says to import conversions using the Google Ads Data Manager. Primary Google Ads Help

  1. Turn on auto-tagging in Google Ads (Admin, Account settings), so every ad click carries a GCLID in the landing page URL.
  2. Add a hidden field to the website contact or booking form that saves the GCLID with the lead.
  3. Create an import conversion action with a neutral name such as “Qualified lead” or “New patient”, and wait 4 to 6 hours before the first upload.
  4. When a lead becomes a patient, add a row with only the GCLID, the conversion name, the conversion time and an optional value.
  5. Upload through Google’s Data Manager or the legacy file import (a scheduled import needs Google Sheets or a file linked over HTTPS or SFTP), within 90 days of the click. Uploads that carry personal data for enhanced conversions have a 63-day limit; this setup sends none. Upload on a steady schedule, daily if you use Smart Bidding. Imported conversions take 24 to 48 hours to appear.

The hard step is the second one: if the booking tool can’t store the GCLID, there is nothing to upload. Also keep in mind the open question in What this guide could not verify: Google’s sensitive-category rule names enhanced conversions and store sales, and we could not confirm whether it also covers click-ID-only imports.

Can a healthcare practice use enhanced conversions?

Enhanced conversions are risky for a healthcare practice because Google’s own policy excludes health-related conversions from them. Google’s customer data policy says conversions in sensitive categories, which include “health or medical information,” can’t be used for measurement in enhanced conversions or store sales uploads. Primary Google Ads Help

Enhanced conversions send a hashed email address or phone number. The FTC has said that hashing does not make data anonymous. FTC, July 2024

Some healthcare tracking platforms, including Curve and Freshpaint, describe enhanced conversions with PHI removed in their own materials. If a vendor offers it, ask the vendor how it fits Google’s policy, and have your counsel review the answer before turning it on.

How do you track phone calls from Google Ads under HIPAA?

To track phone calls from Google Ads under HIPAA, use a call tracking service that signs a BAA and send Google only the call conversion, never call recordings, transcripts or caller details. A BAA with the call tracking vendor does not extend to Google.

What changed for Google Ads offline conversion imports in June 2026?

Google’s help page says that starting June 15, 2026, offline conversion import and enhanced conversions for leads uploads “will be migrated to the Data Manager API and blocked in the Google Ads API”, except for developer tokens allowlisted for legacy access. Tokens that haven’t sent a request between January 2026 and June 2026 are not allowlisted. Primary Google Ads Help

Curve, a tracking vendor, gives a different window: citing Google’s developer blog, it says tokens that had not imported offline conversions between December 2025 and May 2026 lost access on June 15, and that tokens already importing can keep doing so while they integrate with the Data Manager API. Curve, June 21, 2026

Google’s file-import help page, now labelled legacy, still describes scheduled imports from Google Sheets or from a file linked over HTTPS or SFTP, and recommends the Google Ads Data Manager. Vendors with existing integrations, such as RudderStack, say their connections are unaffected.

Google’s default advice doesn’t fit a healthcare practice. Google recommends that advertisers who haven’t adopted offline conversion import start “with enhanced conversions for leads instead.” Enhanced conversions send hashed customer data such as an email address or phone number (Google Ads Help), and Google’s customer data policy says health-related conversions can’t be used for measurement in enhanced conversions. See Can a healthcare practice use enhanced conversions?

Can Manual CPC work without conversion tracking?

Manual CPC can work well without conversion tracking when a practice judges Google Ads by the relationship between ad spend and new-patient growth over time, instead of by a tracked conversion count. Smart Bidding strategies such as Maximize conversions need conversion data to bid; Manual CPC doesn’t.

In Copy for Originals’ client work, a Manual CPC campaign with high-six-figure spend held its own against the same campaign’s results on Maximize conversions the previous year, once the benchmark was the practice’s actual new-patient growth rather than a tracked “conversion.” That is one client’s experience, not a controlled study, but it shows the tradeoff is about measurement, not about whether a practice can compete.

To run Google Ads this way, track monthly ad spend next to new patients from the practice management system, ask new patients how they found the practice, and watch how new-patient numbers move when spend goes up or down. HIPAA Google Ads conversion tracking FAQ

Is Google Ads HIPAA compliant?

Google doesn’t say so. Google offers no business associate agreement (BAA) for Google Analytics, and we found no Google page that offers one for Google Ads conversion tracking; Local Services Ads is the exception, with its own BAA. So a healthcare practice can use Google Ads only if no protected health information reaches Google. In practice that means sending only the ad click ID, a timestamp, an optional value and a neutral conversion name.

Is Freshpaint the only HIPAA-compliant option for Google Ads conversions?

No. Curve and Ours Privacy are healthcare tracking platforms that compete directly with Freshpaint, enterprise CDPs such as Segment, RudderStack, Tealium and Piwik PRO offer a BAA on their top tiers, server-side Google Tag Manager can run on a host that signs a BAA, and a Google Ads offline conversion import that sends only the click ID needs no tracking vendor at all.

What is the cheapest HIPAA-compliant way to track Google Ads conversions?

Usually a Google Ads offline conversion import that sends only the click ID, because it needs no tracking vendor, only staff time. The practice captures the GCLID in its website form, then uploads the click ID, a neutral conversion name and the conversion time through Google Ads Data Manager or a file import within 90 days of the click. The Google import help pages checked for this guide state no price.

Can a healthcare practice run Google Ads without conversion tracking?

Yes. A practice can run Manual CPC and judge Google Ads by the relationship between ad spend and new-patient growth over time instead of by a tracked conversion count. Smart Bidding strategies such as Maximize conversions need conversion data, but Manual CPC does not.
